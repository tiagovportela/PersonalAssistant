# Raw records and deterministic checks outperform clever memory

The evidence published since 1 February 2026 points in one direction for a system like the Personal Work Intelligence layer: keep raw, dated source records as the system of record and keep them searchable, let code (not the model) decide validity, supersession, arithmetic, provenance and permissions, and spend the model and the owner's attention only where judgement is needed. Independent controlled studies find that LLM-built memory structure gives no consistent accuracy advantage over well-ranked retrieval of raw records, that an agent driving a ranked keyword index matches or beats embedding retrieval at this corpus size, and that "the cited item exists" is the check that least predicts whether a statement is supported. The labs' own publications agree on method: Anthropic treats transcript search as a legitimate foundation of memory, reports that people approve 93–97% of per-item prompts while catching 13.6% of planted dangerous ones, and says containment at the environment layer is what holds when model-level defences miss. The layer's existing shape (SQLite FTS5, exact tool allowlists, one wiki page per run, labels computed at read time, versions, no delete tools) sits on the side of the evidence that held up. The main gaps the evidence exposes are a support check behind each citation, keyed supersession in place of word-overlap heuristics, code-recorded origin labels that summarisation cannot upgrade, an owner-run deletion path, an evaluation larger and more controlled than five manual days, and a confirmation flow that asks for fewer, better-evidenced decisions. Most architecture numbers are authors' own results on benchmarks whose scores swing by 7–9 points with the judge or prompt alone; nothing in the window tests Portuguese–English data, a mixed store of Git, session, browser and app events, or human confirmation of memory, so every recommendation needs confirming on the owner's own data.

## How far this evidence can be trusted

Every source cited here is dated 2026-02-01 or later, and the date or arXiv ID with its v1 date sits next to each citation. Nothing is added from memory, and older systems (Whisper large-v3-turbo, Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3, Mem0, embeddinggemma-300m, bge-m3) appear only as a 2026 source measures them.

The research ran under access limits that shape how much weight each claim carries. The page-fetch tool could not resolve most hosts, so arXiv, Hugging Face and modelcontextprotocol.io were read through the shell. The hosts openai.com, developers.openai.com, deploymentsafety.openai.com, blog.google, the Anthropic system-card PDF hosts, nist.gov and sqlite.org stayed unreachable, so OpenAI, Google and system-card material is largely from search snippets, from the OpenAI Cookbook repository on GitHub, or from Anthropic's own summaries of its system cards. Many arXiv papers were read as abstracts only. openreview.net and aclanthology.org were not checked, so peer-review status is mostly unknown; all arXiv items should be read as preprints. One inconsistency in the notes is worth stating: the security notes mark four OpenAI posts, one Google and one DeepMind post and several simonwillison.net posts as "opened", while the other note files list those hosts as unreachable. Those items are vendor or relayed claims either way and should be re-verified before anything depends on them.

Three provenance labels recur. **Independent** means the authors measured systems they did not build. **Authors' own** means a preprint reporting on its own method or benchmark, not replicated. **Vendor-reported** means a lab or company describing its own product. Depth is stated as full text, abstract or search snippet where it matters.

Several date-rule edge cases remain. Anthropic's documentation pages (Claude Code memory, Managed Agents memory, the memory tool, the Citations API) are living pages read on 2026-11-27 with no per-passage dates; the memory tool identifier is `memory_20250818` and the Citations API launched in January 2025, so only the pages' current text is used. The jina-embeddings-v5 paper is dated 2026-02-17 but its Hugging Face repositories were created on 2026-01-22. The OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications carries no date and is left out.

## Memory: structure earns its place on updates, not on recall

### What the evidence supports

**Keep raw records retrievable and treat claims and wiki pages as views over them.** A naive baseline of dense retrieval plus reranking over raw history ranked first among memory systems in 6 of 7 benchmark settings, and "even complete access to transformed memory stores leaves an accuracy gap relative to raw evidence" (independent, full text; [arXiv 2610.30755, 2026-10-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30755)). Six explicit memory systems showed "no consistent overall advantage over raw-history RAG", with construction costs reaching hours per conversation (independent, full text; [arXiv 2610.24671, 2026-10-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24671)). A study of 12 systems over 11 datasets concluded that "retaining the original conversational content is more important than increasing abstraction or hierarchy" and that maintenance touching a bounded subset of memory is the most cost-efficient (independent, full text; [arXiv 2606.24775, 2026-06-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24775)). Anthropic's own position matches: "it's also reasonable to consider transcript search as the foundation of Claude's memory", with the caution that maintained representations must not "accumulate errors or hallucinations" (vendor, full text; [Anthropic, 2026-11-06](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)).

**Serve the wiki and raw search together.** On about 350,000 internal files and 25 questions, Anthropic measured 48% from the wiki alone, 53% from search alone and 61% from both, with about 20,000 tokens saved per question; on 24 multi-document merger questions the wiki scored 87% against 73% for search. Where criteria could be met from a single document the wiki showed no clear gain, and one wiki grew to 1.77 times its corpus and "effectively abstracted nothing". These are vendor numbers on small sets, some from single runs ([Anthropic, 2026-11-06](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)).

**Quote exact terms and verify pages against sources.** When several agents summarised contract terms into one shared table, 41 of 57 graded facts survived; one page per contract, written by one agent and quoting terms word for word, kept 57 of 57 ([Anthropic, 2026-11-06](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)). This is direct support for the layer's one-page-per-run design. A study of LLM wikis over 6,800 questions found blind compilation "fails entirely" (53–60% catastrophic failures), while probing the compiled page with diagnostic questions and forcing dropped facts back in recovered 80% of the lost quality; the gain came from targeted diagnosis (+0.95), not generic pinning (+0.16) (authors' own, full text; [arXiv 2605.07068, 2026-05-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07068)).

**Typed, dated claims with supersession chains are justified by update and safety behaviour.** A longitudinal benchmark found a budgeted curated-map memory leading at three weeks (94.2%) but dropping to 72.2% recall of early content by nine weeks, while a provenance-typed graph rose to 90.4% and blocked all 14 planted injection probes; "the operative boundary is provenance surviving representation" (independent benchmark whose authors also release one tested system, full text; [arXiv 2607.21962, 2026-07-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21962)). The layer's "label computed at read time" design is close to the "preserve at write, resolve at read" line of work.

**Enforce validity at read time.** Serving only the active value of each correctly keyed slot removed stale exposure in all 1,200 keyed prompts, against 70.3% of prompts exposing a superseded value without update resolution (authors' own, full text; [arXiv 2610.10265, 2026-10-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10265)). In five memory systems that mark contradicted facts invalid without deleting them, the revoked fact "outranks its replacement, and leads agents to the unsafe action" wherever the label is visible to retrieval (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2609.08258, 2026-09-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08258)). A stale statement sitting beside the current one is worse than noise: swapping one unrelated statement for the obsolete competing one cut accuracy from 100% to 44.6% on a 7B model (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2610.36308, 2026-10-26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36308)). Search output should therefore carry event date, label and a "superseded by" pointer, and default to active claims.

**Adopt the labs' store mechanics.** Anthropic's Managed Agents memory stores give every change an immutable version, support a content-hash precondition on writes, cap each memory at 100 kB, and can be attached read-only because "a successful prompt injection could write malicious content into the store" (vendor documentation, living page; [Claude Platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/memory)). Its "dreaming" pass checks notes against the original transcript and writes to a separate output the owner can discard (vendor; [Dreams docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/dreams)). The SQLite activity store plays the transcript's role for a periodic wiki and claims audit.

### What it warns against

**Continuous LLM rewriting.** Consolidated memory utility "first rises, then degrades, and can fall below the no-memory baseline"; agents given the choice keep raw episodes by default and double the accuracy of forced consolidation; the recommendation is that abstraction be "selective, delayed, and grounded in recoverable trajectories" (independent, full text; [arXiv 2605.12978, 2026-05-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12978)). A nightly-rewrite memory stored replaced details "as fact even when another turn held the correct value" (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2611.21648, v1 2026-10-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21648)). Pages should be rebuilt from claims, not repeatedly rewritten as prose.

**Agent-curated stores as a quality lever.** In the first systematic study of file-based memory, organisation roughly halved retrieval cost on large material but "no agent we measure converts organization itself into better answers", and the agent-curated store was the weakest on one tier (37.5% against 78.1% for a verbatim dump). Its columns rest on 32 to 158 questions (independent, full text; [arXiv 2607.26637, 2026-07-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26637)).

**Silent age-based deletion.** At an 8k-token budget every eviction policy tested produced fully irreversible errors (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2609.08279, 2026-09-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08279)). A 90-day rule for unconfirmed data needs a promote-or-summarise check before expiry.

**Letting an LLM classify memory operations unchecked.** One representative system chose the right operation (create, update, reinforce, supersede) 20% of the time, a failure "entirely invisible" to question-answering tests, and store-level and answer-level scores diverged by 16 points on average (independent, one system tested, full text; [arXiv 2610.22209, 2026-10-15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22209)). Consolidation also erases authority: "authority collapse" appeared in 48 of 49 configurations, and persisting authority labels cut unauthorised actions from 16.9% to 0.0% (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2608.01679, 2026-08-03](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01679)). Wiki prose must never state an unconfirmed or disputed claim more firmly than its label.

**Rigid "what to record" rules with current models.** Anthropic reports "it helps to tell Claude what memory is for, but not how to use it": a fixed "record only failures" rule cut the gain from memory by 0.234 on Opus 5, while an older model nearly doubled its score when told to read memory first. On one task the with-memory score ran from 0.364 (Sonnet 4.6) to about 0.83 (newest models), so prompts and tool descriptions need re-testing at each model change (vendor, one task; [Anthropic, 2026-11-06](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)).

**Reading public leaderboards as design guidance.** On LoCoMo, "changing only the judge moves a score by up to 7.2 points and changing only the answer prompt by up to 9.3" (vendor evaluating its own system, answers released; [arXiv 2611.19199, 2026-11-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19199)), and a second-hand audit reports 6.4% of the answer key corrupt ([arXiv 2607.21962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21962)). Scores above about 90% on LoCoMo or LongMemEval-S carry little design information.

### How strong it is

The raw-versus-structure conclusion is the best-supported finding in this report: four independent full-text studies agree, and Anthropic's vendor post points the same way. Individual architectures (Engram, BRIDGE, Mnemon, ReFind and others) are authors' own, mostly abstract-only, on incomparable harnesses. The lab picture is lopsided. Anthropic's material was read in full. OpenAI's "Dreaming" memory (2026-06-04) is snippet-only, and secondary sources give conflicting recall figures (41.5%, 67.9%, 82.8% across three generations) without a published test structure ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-memory-dreaming)). Google's Personal Intelligence expansion and memory import (March 2026) are snippet-only, and no Google DeepMind paper on agent memory was found in the window. The announcement date of Anthropic's dreaming preview differs between its blog page (2026-05-19) and press (2026-05-06). No source evaluates memory built from Git, browser and app telemetry, Portuguese–English content, or memory with a human confirmation step.

## Retrieval: the agent over FTS5 is already the favoured design

### What the evidence supports

**Keep the ranked lexical index and give the agent better controls.** The strongest independent result compared retrievers across 28 corpus tiers with one reader and one judging protocol. At 1,144 documents an agent calling ranked BM25 scored 90.1 against 81.3 for single-shot BM25, and dense RAG scored 58.1 at the smallest tier; raw-file exploration cost 39 times the tokens of BM25 (independent, full text; [arXiv 2607.26497, 2026-07-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26497)). "Agency therefore helps after globally ranked candidate discovery." The layer's corpus sits far below the roughly 10M-token point where raw grep-style exploration starts losing to ranked search. The controls that mattered in an iterative keyword loop were per-session rank fusion, context expansion around a hit, temporal narrowing and skipping already-seen items; swapping the lexical backend for dense or hybrid inside the same agent did not beat BM25 (authors' own; [arXiv 2608.12888, 2026-08-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12888)). A zero-ML SQLite FTS5 baseline reached NDCG@5 0.692 on LongMemEval-M, above all published baselines the authors compared (authors' own; [arXiv 2604.02431, 2026-04-02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02431)).

**Expand queries with exact rare terms, written by the calling model.** A reproducibility study of ten reformulation methods found expansion "consistently improves" BM25 and that expanded BM25 "frequently approaches or matches" dense retrieval on unexpanded queries (independent; [arXiv 2604.27421, 2026-04-30](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27421)). In code search, prepending identifiers, API names and error terms added 0.11–0.19 nDCG@10 to BM25; prose phrases did not help and pseudo-relevance feedback hurt (independent; [arXiv 2611.20455, 2026-11-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20455)). Bilingual keyword variants are an extrapolation; no study tested them.

**Extend index-time enrichment for the lexical index, keeping verbatim text.** In the one pre-specified study, one LLM call per chunk writing title, summary, keywords and questions gained +8.7 recall points under BM25 and matched contextual retrieval at a fraction of the tokens, but added nothing detectable under a strong embedder or reranker. The mechanism is vocabulary bridging: questions using terms the chunk lacks gained +24.4 points. Its author's recommendation for a "keyword-only stack" is enrichment (independent, full text; English only; earlier versions of the author's evaluation were withdrawn; [arXiv 2603.23533, v1 2026-03-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)). The owner's hand-written Portuguese and English key terms are this technique. Distilled text alone degraded BM25 significantly while verbatim plus distilled was best (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2603.13017, 2026-03-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13017)).

**Build a test set from real queries before adding any model.** A decision protocol from the "Fusion Tax" study: measure BM25 and dense per query on 200–400 representative queries, use dense-weighted interpolation (0.6–0.7) if dense leads by 0.04 nDCG@10 or more, RRF or equal weights if balanced, check that fusion beats its best component, and prefer a fixed policy to per-query routing (independent, full text; [arXiv 2610.30612, 2026-10-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)). Real queries differ from synthetic ones: authentic queries averaged 6.8 words against 15.7 and picked a different winning configuration (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2609.14579, 2026-09-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14579)).

**If an embedder is added, the candidates and their costs are known.** The table shows open models small enough for CPU with native Brazilian-Portuguese retrieval scores (mean nDCG@10 over six tasks, computed from the public leaderboard data generated 2026-11-26; [MTEB-BR results](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MTEB-BR/mteb-pt-results)) and CPU throughput where measured (batch 64, Ryzen 9 7950X, OpenVINO; authors' own; [arXiv 2607.25180, 2026-07-28](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25180)).

### What it warns against

**Expecting large gains from hybrid retrieval.** Even the best fusion ranks relevant documents below the better of its two components on 13–48% of queries while winning in aggregate by only 0.008–0.051 nDCG@10, and untuned RRF can score below its own dense half ([arXiv 2610.30612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612); [arXiv 2603.23533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)).

**Rerankers as a default.** An off-the-shelf cross-encoder degraded Hit@1 by 6.9 points on conversational memory (authors' own, abstract, one configuration; [arXiv 2606.04194, 2026-06-02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04194)), another study found reranking failed to improve NDCG (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2604.15484, 2026-04-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15484)), and the one timing found is about 1.6 s per query at depth 30 on an integrated GPU ([arXiv 2610.30612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)). The memory literature is not unanimous: removing the reranker cost 3.92 points in the raw-records study ([arXiv 2610.30755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30755)). Test on own data.

**Static embeddings, per-query routers, graph indexes and sub-agent search.** Static models score 0.374–0.445 on native Portuguese retrieval and received zero weight in all 50 folds of a BM25-plus-transformer fusion (independent; [arXiv 2608.02112, 2026-08-03](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02112)). Four studies report router nulls. Graph-based RAG hit construction walls and stayed below BM25 ([arXiv 2607.26497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26497)). Delegating code search to a sub-agent lost at the hand-off: 41.8% of failures were "usually silent, ending in a fluent and confident answer that was wrong" (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2608.01507, 2026-08-02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01507)).

**Trusting multilingual leaderboards for Portuguese.** A model's global multilingual rank predicts its Portuguese rank only moderately (Spearman 0.754), and harrier-270m scores 66.4 on multilingual retrieval but 0.576 on native PT-BR (independent, full text; [arXiv 2607.04581, 2026-07-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04581)).

**Loading look-alike or superseded items together.** Neutral filler costs nothing, but one statement giving the queried key a rival value multiplies the loss 9 to 110 times (authors' own, small open models, abstract; [arXiv 2611.04169, 2026-11-03](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04169)). Whole wiki pages are cheap to load at this scale; near-duplicate claims are the risk.

### How strong it is

Strong and mostly independent for the central claims: the agent-over-BM25 result, the fusion tax, the enrichment study and the Portuguese benchmark are independent and read in full. Weaker for specifics: Bekko's CPU and quality numbers come from one developer's preprint, Granite's from the vendor, cross-checked only by the leaderboard; the "grep beats vector" paper reports different numbers for the same pairs in different tables without explanation ([arXiv 2605.15184, 2026-05-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184)); the embeddinggemma-2 leaderboard row (271M, Gemma licence) conflicts with its Hugging Face metadata (744M, Apache-2.0); sqlite-vec's ANN status and the SQLite team's vec1 extension rest on snippets. Mixed-language queries are a known weakness of BM25 (−6.56 nDCG@10 under English–Chinese code-switching; independent; [arXiv 2604.17632, 2026-04-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17632)), but no source measures Portuguese–English cross-lingual retrieval, single-query latency on an x86 laptop, or a mixed store of work events.

## Grounding: a real citation is not a supported sentence

### What the evidence supports

**Add a support check behind every citation.** Across 14 models writing cited reports, link validity exceeded 94% for 12 of them while the share of citations whose source supports the specific claim ran from 24% to 77% (Claude Opus 4.5 highest), and that accuracy fell about 42% as tool calls scaled from 2 to 150 (independent, full text; [arXiv 2605.06635, 2026-05-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06635)). Models catch wrong-case legal citations 93–100% of the time but wrong-page ones only 37–61%, accepting "topical overlap rather than page-level support" (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2608.12571, 2026-08-12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12571)). The layer's current lint checks existence, the dimension that least predicts support.

**Verify against the underlying evidence, not only the claim text.** A compressor's citations scored 0.86 precision against its own summaries and 0.12 against source spans (authors' own, single author, abstract; [arXiv 2609.14245, 2026-09-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14245)). Wiki sentences that cite claims inherit this problem.

**Run a cascade: deterministic checks, a small local verifier on short spans, an LLM for the uncertain band.** On human-labelled scientific claims a local 3B attribution model, a DeBERTa NLI model and GPT-4o separated supported from unsupported equally well (ROC-AUC 0.88, 0.90, 0.87), "so a strict frontier judge such as GPT-4o buys no separation advantage" (authors' own, full text; [arXiv 2607.20527, 2026-07-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20527)). A 184M NLI model on a laptop CPU reached AUC 0.873 on QA but was near chance on summarisation (independent, full text; [arXiv 2606.29809, 2026-06-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29809)). Short claims against short quoted spans is where small verifiers work; long evidence is where one collapsed from 0.90 to 0.53 AUROC (independent, full text; [arXiv 2606.23915, 2026-06-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23915)).

**Write from source-linked claims and keep free rewriting out of the last step.** Replacing only the report writer with one that drafts each section from source-linked claims cut hallucinated claims from 15.89 to 5.02 and misattributed ones from 18.94 to 5.43 while recall rose (authors' own, full text; [arXiv 2608.28643, v1 2026-08-12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28643)). In one audited system 84.7% of final-report errors entered at the orchestrator, and "almost every agent makes a lot of mistakes with the exception being those that summarize a single document" (independent, partial full text; [arXiv 2608.24306, 2026-08-25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24306)).

**Replace word-overlap contradiction heuristics with keyed supersession.** Cosine similarity separates a contradicted fact from a duplicate with AUROC 0.59, because "contradictions are often more embedding-similar to the original than rephrased duplicates"; a deterministic subject–relation–object rule writing to a bi-temporal ledger reached 0.95–1.00 on evolving knowledge with no LLM call (authors' own, single author, templated data, full text; [arXiv 2606.26511, 2026-06-25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26511)). Word overlap is the same family of signal. The hard part moves to key assignment: "missed merges leave stale values active, whereas false merges silently remove current values" ([arXiv 2610.10265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10265)). Proposed merges and supersessions are therefore the best use of owner review. Entity resolution on both the write path and the query recovered most of the freshness lost when full names were missing (independent, full text; [arXiv 2611.19752, 2026-11-15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19752)), which matters for a project named differently in two languages. Any contradiction detector needs matched hard negatives: baselines that catch 76–97% of contradictions also flag 16–43% of safe look-alikes (authors' own, single author, abstract; [arXiv 2609.28575, 2026-09-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28575)).

**Screen extracted commitments with three lookups.** In a simulated workplace, 60% of 1,663 false-positive tasks re-opened a request already answered, 28% took on a colleague's request and 13% cited background with no request behind it (authors' own benchmark, synthetic organisation, full text; [arXiv 2611.12393, 2026-11-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12393)). In one bilingual inbox, the sender's relationship was recovered from text 0.60 of the time but inherited correctly from the contact graph 98.6% of the time, and the system's own stored labels scored 28% against human labels (authors' own, single owner, partial full text; [arXiv 2610.32955, 2026-10-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32955)). Keep the speaker's verbatim words: rewrites turned "I am working at X" into "works at X" in 210 of 381 pairs across 11 models (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2609.36457, 2026-09-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36457)).

**Compute labels from observable checks and calibrate thresholds on owner history.** Seven 3–9B open models gave numeric confidence at ceiling in 91.7% of answers (independent, single author, abstract; [arXiv 2604.22215, 2026-04-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22215)); disagreement between two structurally different reads gave 99.1% accuracy at 80% coverage for extracted fields (authors' own, single author, abstract; [arXiv 2606.24420, 2026-06-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24420)). A three-way verdict (supported, contradicted, not found) matches how the benchmarks are framed, and "not found" needs its own tracking because missing support is harder to detect than contradiction ([arXiv 2610.10971, 2026-10-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10971)).

**Treat any LLM judge as an instrument to be validated.** Anthropic's guidance: atomic yes/no criteria, a different model as grader, blind human labels with chance-corrected agreement, re-grading a sample with another model or rubric ("if the conclusions change when the grader does, you've likely measured the grader"), and failing loudly on grader errors (vendor guidance; [Anthropic, 2026-11-13](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-strong-safety-evaluations)). A bootstrap test suggests "about 100 items often suffice" to decide whether a judge may replace an annotator (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2611.26073, 2026-11-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26073)).

### What it warns against

**"Label equals the best of its citations."** Three identical reprints in place of one report raised accepted false claims from 0.06 to 0.71 on one model (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2611.09203, 2026-11-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09203)). A commit, the chat message announcing it and the session that produced it are one source chain, not three confirmations.

**Measuring before and after with an unnamed verifier.** On identical outputs the unsupported-citation rate ran from about 3% to about 18% across five validated verifiers ([arXiv 2607.20527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20527)), and no automatic scorer transferred across datasets ([arXiv 2606.23915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23915)).

**Unaudited judges and self-consistency as proof.** A deployed production judge agreed with human gold at κ=0.04 on hard cases, while a self-hosted 27B model matched a frontier model at about 1/300 the cost (authors' own audit, abstract; [arXiv 2609.30290, 2026-09-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30290)). A judge repeated its verdict 98.2% of the time yet agreed with state-based scoring on 56.2%, and a rule with no model call caught all 11 forbidden writes the judge passed (independent, single author, abstract; [arXiv 2611.14581, 2026-11-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14581)). Protocol choice alone moved one reported accuracy from 0.551 to 0.899 (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2606.00093, v1 2026-05-25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00093)).

**Naive decomposition, iterative self-assessment and schema-forced fields.** Rigid claim decomposition cost up to 27.6 points where facets lost full-claim context ([arXiv 2606.24627, 2026-06-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24627)). Iterative retrieval lowered correct abstention in 30 of 40 configurations (independent, abstract; [arXiv 2610.28340, 2026-10-20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28340)). A field-by-field extractor "hallucinates values for absent fields", so mandatory owner and due-date slots invite invention ([arXiv 2606.24420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24420)).

**Mixing citations with structured output in one run.** Anthropic's Citations API guarantees valid pointers, not support, and "cannot be used together with structured outputs"; it suits the wiki compile run, not JSON claim extraction (vendor documentation, living page; [Claude Platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)).

### How strong it is

Good for the diagnosis, thinner for the cure. The existence-versus-support gap rests on independent audits read in full. Verifier and judge methods are mostly authors' own, many single-author and abstract-only. Direct evidence for commitment extraction is thin: no 2026 benchmark reports owner or due-date error rates on real meetings, chat or mail. Nothing measures groundedness checking, contradiction detection or judge agreement for Portuguese or mixed-language text; the multilingual evidence says detection is best in English and degrades elsewhere ([arXiv 2602.06920, 2026-02-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06920)). No source gives CPU latency per claim for small verifiers. OpenAI system-card factuality figures were seen only as snippets and are not used as findings.

## Security: integrity is the residual risk, and code must hold it

### What the evidence supports

**Keep deterministic capability limits as the load-bearing control.** Anthropic reports attack success of roughly 0.1% on single attempts and 5–6% after 100 adaptive attempts for Opus 4.7, and says "protection in the model layer will never be 100% effective, which is why it can't stand alone" (vendor on an external benchmark; [Anthropic, 2026-05-25](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-we-contain-claude)). On the benchmark built with Gray Swan, UK AISI and US CAISI, success within 15 attempts was 2.0% for Opus 5 and 20.0% for GPT 5.6 Sol (vendor-run; [Anthropic Transparency Hub, July 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/transparency)). In the academic literature, a task-bound reference monitor admitted 1.3% and 7.7% of attacker calls where the models proposed them in 64% and 84% of episodes, and in simulation an adaptive attacker drove every guardrail stack to 100% (authors' own, small models plus simulation, abstract; [arXiv 2611.11571, 2026-11-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11571)). Exact tool allowlists per headless run and a tool-less wiki run are this class of control. Wrapping tool results as data is helpful but not load-bearing: with identical weights, changing only the system prompt moved attack success from 0.2% to 43% on small models ([arXiv 2610.23254, 2026-10-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23254)).

**Record each claim's origin channel in code at write time and never let it be upgraded.** Memory converts a one-off injection into a durable one: one crafted email planted a dormant payload with 85–100% success across four memory backends, "persisting for 100 or more benign sessions"; labelling entries by origin and blocking send tools in any session that read an untrusted entry gave 0% in all evaluated configurations, at a utility cost (authors' own, full text; [arXiv 2605.01970, 2026-05-03](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01970)). Content and lineage signals are launderable through "the agent's own summarization, a trusted-tool echo, and manufactured corroboration" (authors' own benchmark, abstract; [arXiv 2606.24322, 2026-06-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24322)). A statement's authority should be capped by its least-trusted evidence, and several items from one origin count once.

**Treat the owner's confirmation as the integrity control for false facts, and render the card in code.** Write-time content screening rejected 0 of 360 plainly worded false memories; 1.2% poisoned content cut answer accuracy from 0.850 to 0.300 (authors reporting a negative result on their own system, full text; [arXiv 2608.21230, 2026-08-21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21230)). An approval card rendered from the real pending effect cut forged-narration success from 0.54 to 0.03, though flooding still succeeded 0.45–0.60 of the time against a modelled approver (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2610.21063, 2026-10-15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21063)). Cards should show exact claim text, origin channel and evidence excerpt from the stored record, with a deterministic approval budget per run.

**Normalise ingested text before any model sees it.** Across about 145 million GitHub items, hidden-text attacks replayed against five models succeeded against one small model in 19% of cases "and against none once HTML comments are stripped" (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2611.28763, 2026-11-21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28763)). Models also follow instructions encoded in zero-width characters and Unicode Tags (authors' own, five models, not replicated, abstract; [arXiv 2603.00164, v1 2026-02-26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00164)). Strip comments, tag-block, variation-selector, zero-width and bidirectional characters, and log what was removed. README text is a higher-risk surface: the same instruction was followed 2.6 times more often there than in a code file ([arXiv 2610.38867, 2026-10-28](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38867)).

**Apply the MCP maintainers' local-server defaults to third-party connectors.** The official guide states a local server "is an ordinary operating system process", that "every server you configure shares the same model context", and that a server "can quietly change" tool descriptions after approval; it recommends pinned versions, clients that re-prompt on tool-definition change, a small roster, per-server scoped secrets and no network unless needed ([MCP Local Server Security, 2026-07-28 docs](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/2026-07-28/tutorials/security/local-server-security)). Because a third-party connector in the desktop app can steer calls to the store's tools, the store's server-side rules are the control that does not depend on the client. A measurement across 12 frameworks found "no framework implements intentional defense" against poisoned tool metadata (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2611.03555, 2026-11-02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03555)).

**Add a model-based second pass to regex redaction, at the store's write handler.** An independent benchmark found a rule-based detector reached recall 0.07 on high-sensitivity categories while LLM detectors stayed robust (abstract; [arXiv 2606.19881, 2026-06-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19881)). OpenAI released an Apache-2.0 local token classifier with a `secret` category and reports 96% F1 on a public PII set, stating it "is not an anonymization tool" (vendor; [OpenAI Privacy Filter, 2026-04-22](https://openai.com/index/introducing-openai-privacy-filter/)). On 900 synthetic PII values regex caught 88.9% because "it cannot find names that are not known in advance", while regex-plus-NER hybrids caught 100% (authors' own, small synthetic set, abstract; [arXiv 2611.07633, 2026-11-05](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07633)). Regex stays the right first pass for structured secrets.

**Build an owner-run, code-level deletion path over recorded derivation links.** In the first benchmark of forget requests, "no system passes more than 1.5% of forget requests end to end" (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2610.18052, 2026-10-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18052)). "No delete tools" protects integrity against a hijacked agent but provides no erasure. The same derivation graph that carries provenance lets code follow a source item to every claim, wiki sentence, version and index entry that depends on it.

**Test security with staged, effect-based cases.** A tool-identity scorer reported 21.7% attack success where the argument-level rate was 1.2% (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2609.32691, 2026-09-26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32691)), and single-run labels disagreed with a rerun up to 35% of the time ([arXiv 2610.24048, 2026-10-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24048)). A suite should plant payloads through each real ingestion channel, score "written", "survived validation", "reached a wiki statement" and "changed a later answer" separately, verify payload delivery, repeat runs and record benign-task results alongside.

### What it warns against

**Per-item approval as a safety net.** Anthropic reports users approved "roughly 93%" of permission prompts ([2026-05-25](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-we-contain-claude)) and later "97%" ([2026-08-07](https://claude.com/blog/auto-mode-default-in-claude-code)); the two figures are from the same vendor and are not reconciled. In its study of 1,053 paid testers, humans refused a planted dangerous command 13.6% of the time, falling from about 17% early in a session to about 5% after 50 or more prompts.

**Any run that combines private data, untrusted content and an outbound channel.** The MCP maintainers' working rule is that "the only way to solve the trifecta is to cut off one of the three legs" ([MCP blog, 2026-03-16](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-03-16-tool-annotations/)). Anthropic's internal red-team prompt completed credential exfiltration in 24 of 25 retries, and "the only defense that holds in this situation is the environment"; egress allowlists act as capability grants ([Anthropic, 2026-05-25](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-we-contain-claude)).

**Relying on injection classifiers, memory auditors or trajectory detectors.** At a 2% attack rate a detector with AUROC 0.879 has precision 0.076 (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2610.41803, 2026-10-30](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41803)); an LLM memory auditor cut propagation of a planted goal only from 71% to 34% ([arXiv 2610.04083, 2026-10-02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04083)); dormant conditional payloads succeeded in 43–83% of trials on nine production agents ([arXiv 2609.22510, 2026-09-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22510)). Instruction-like phrasing in a claim suits a review flag, not an automatic gate.

**Long auto-compacting privileged runs and command-pattern allow rules.** Compaction placed a model-written summary as a user message so quoted injected text "reads as the user speaking" (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2611.32194, 2026-11-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32194)). An agent that listed `cat` as safe was led by a README to run downloaded code (relayed report; [Simon Willison, 2026-03-18](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/18/snowflake-cortex-ai/)).

**Treating tool annotations or state handles as enforcement.** Annotations "aren't enforcement", and under the stateless 2026-07-28 revision servers "MUST NOT treat possession of a state handle as authentication" ([MCP Security Best Practices, 2026-07-28 docs](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/2026-07-28/tutorials/security/security_best_practices)).

### How strong it is

Vendor robustness numbers are not comparable across labs (different attempt budgets, safeguards on or off, benchmark membership), and Anthropic itself says none are independently verified ([Anthropic, 2026-04-09](https://www.anthropic.com/research/trustworthy-agents)). Google's "0.0%" for Gemini 4 Carbon gives no attempt count, and one preprint reports the most robust model resisting only 66% of attacks at maximum attacker budget ([arXiv 2611.11861, 2026-11-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11861)). An independent researcher reported an auto-mode bypass claimed to work 80% of the time ([Simon Willison, 2026-08-27](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/27/breaking-claude-code-opus-5-auto-mode/)). The 0% results for origin-bound authority, flow labelling and signed writes are all authors' own on their own benchmarks, unreplicated. Two papers disagree on how easily Claude Code writes untrusted content to memory (73.7%/66.9% injection in [arXiv 2609.13889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13889) against "difficult" in [arXiv 2607.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14611)), using different payloads and models. Regulatory statements (Spain's AEPD, 2026-02-18; France's CNIL, dated 2026-07-20 or 2026-08-05 by different sources) come from law-firm summaries only. NIST and six-agency guidance were snippets. No source tests a claims-and-wiki store with human confirmation, injection through meeting transcripts, or deletion propagation into indexes.

## Lab method: pre-register, repeat, baseline, and version every prompt

### What the evidence supports

**Turn the five-day manual evaluation into a pre-registered, repeated, baselined suite.** Anthropic's minimum: "write down the construct you want to measure before collecting data, validate your grader against human judgment before trusting any number it produces, and report uncertainty". It adds running each case several times, paired per-item comparisons, cases built "from observed behavior", and paired should and should-not cases; it treats 100 items as the floor for a complex multi-turn evaluation and notes a 98% versus 94% difference that was not significant (vendor guidance; [Anthropic, 2026-11-13](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-strong-safety-evaluations)). Claude Code's `claude plugin eval` (2026-09-11) runs each case three times by default and repeats with the plugin unloaded, because "if a case scores 1.0 both with and without the plugin, the plugin isn't what made it pass" (vendor docs for a feature shipped in the window; [plugin evals](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-evals)). Five labelled days catch only large regressions and should be reported that way.

**Evaluate at three fidelity levels and do not assume transfer.** An OpenAI Cookbook article evaluates an MCP plugin by direct tool tests, a function-calling loop and the product harness, because "a score from one setup doesn't transfer to the other" ([OpenAI Cookbook, 2026-09-23](https://github.com/openai/openai-cookbook/blob/main/examples/partners/harness_aware_plugin_evals/harness_aware_plugin_evals.ipynb); a partner-folder article, not a statement of internal practice). The layer's unit tests, a loop over its 24 tools, and the real `claude -p` run with its allowlist are those three levels.

**Use a deterministic oracle and store-level audits.** The most reusable single-user method computes ground truth with SQL that is separate from the summarisation path, adds absent-fact probes, fixes tolerances and freezes the corpus ([arXiv 2608.05784, 2026-08-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)). "Ground truth first" generation seeds facts with validity intervals before any text exists ([arXiv 2607.21962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21962)), and store inspection catches what answers hide ([arXiv 2610.22209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22209)). Anthropic's wiki work suggests three direct metrics: a question set answered wiki-only, search-only and both; a fact-survival count; and wiki size relative to sources ([Anthropic, 2026-11-06](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)).

**Give each unattended run a progress file, a verifier it cannot argue with, and a hard budget.** Anthropic's long-running work names "a progress file, a test oracle, an agent prompt with clear rules", with failed approaches recorded so later sessions do not repeat them ([Anthropic, 2026-03-23](https://www.anthropic.com/research/long-running-claude)). "It's important that the task verifier is nearly perfect, otherwise Claude will solve the wrong problem", and harness output should be a few lines with aggregates pre-computed ([Anthropic, 2026-02-05](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler)). Self-evaluation fails: agents "respond by confidently praising the work", and a separately tuned sceptical evaluator "is worth the cost when the task sits beyond what the current model does reliably solo" ([Anthropic, 2026-03-24](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps)). Google's ADK 2.0 post makes the same split: "if you can clearly map the workflow, use determinism" ([Google, 2026-07-01](https://developers.googleblog.com/why-we-built-adk-20/)).

**Evaluate the 2026 headless additions.** Dated changelog entries relevant to scheduled `claude -p` runs: `--permission-prompts none` (2026-09-02) denies anything that would prompt; `StopFailure` hooks (2026-03-17) fire on API-error endings; `last_assistant_message` in Stop hooks (2026-02-18) allows output validation without parsing transcripts; `mcp_server_errors` in the stream-json init event (2026-07-24) reveals a server that failed to load; `CLAUDE_CODE_MCP_STARTUP_WAIT_MS` (2026-09-17) bounds startup; a stderr warning flags deprecated or auto-updated models (2026-06-19); `--bare` (2026-03-20) and `--restricted` (2026-08-27) give more reproducible runs for evaluation; `/goal` (2026-05-11) sets a completion condition in `-p` mode ([Claude Code changelog](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/changelog)). In headless auto mode, 3 consecutive or 20 total denials terminate the process ([Anthropic, 2026-03-25](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-auto-mode)).

**Keep the always-loaded layer small, tool results short, and the prompt prefix stable.** Anthropic says "longer instruction files reduce adherence" and that Claude Code loads only the first 200 lines or 25 KB of its memory index ([Anthropic, 2026-11-06](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)). Managed Agents spill tool outputs over 100k characters to a file with a preview (2026-05-19; [release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview)); the Codex guide truncates at 10k tokens ([OpenAI Cookbook, 2026-02-25](https://github.com/openai/openai-cookbook/blob/main/examples/gpt-5/codex_prompting_guide.ipynb)). OpenAI's caching guidance is to "keep the early portion of the prompt stable" ([OpenAI Cookbook, 2026-02-18](https://github.com/openai/openai-cookbook/blob/main/examples/Prompt_Caching_201.ipynb)).

**Plan for the MCP 2026-07-28 revision.** The protocol is now stateless: the initialize handshake and session header are gone, list responses carry cache hints and deterministic order, Roots, Sampling and Logging are deprecated with a twelve-month window, and elicitation moves to an `input_required` retry pattern ([MCP blog, 2026-07-28](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/)). State that must cross calls should be an explicit handle passed as a tool argument. The maintainers advise setting the four annotations truthfully, and their roadmap notes that "tool selection tends to get worse as the list grows" ([MCP roadmap, 2026-08-22](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/mcp-roadmap/)).

**Treat every instruction-template edit as a release.** Anthropic's postmortem traced degraded quality to three small changes, including one system-prompt line that passed weeks of internal testing and later showed a 3% drop under broader ablation; remedies are per-model evaluation on every prompt change, line-by-line ablations, soak periods and prompt-audit tooling (vendor; [Anthropic, 2026-04-23](https://www.anthropic.com/research/april-23-postmortem)). On model change: "re-examine a harness, stripping away pieces that are no longer load-bearing" ([Anthropic, 2026-03-24](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps)), with the same grader, since "graders' verdicts may change in subtle ways" ([Anthropic, 2026-11-13](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-strong-safety-evaluations)). Google's evaluation platform runs the same metrics offline and on sampled live traffic so that drift "points to a problem with the agent rather than with the way it was measured" ([Google, 2026-07-31](https://developers.googleblog.com/agent-and-model-evaluations-in-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-are-now-ga/)).

### What it warns against

Self-critique in place of a separate checker; parallel agents on a coupled task or a shared write target ("every agent would hit the same bug ... and then overwrite each other's changes"; [Anthropic, 2026-02-05](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler)); irreversible context pruning ("it is difficult to know which tokens the future turns will need"; [Anthropic, 2026-04-08](https://www.anthropic.com/research/managed-agents)); comparing scores across graders, harnesses or configurations (API versus product system prompt differed by up to 34 points; a 6-point gap came from infrastructure alone, [Anthropic, 2026-02-05](https://www.anthropic.com/research/infrastructure-noise)); and keeping scaffolding a newer model no longer needs. On newer Claude models `tool_choice` types `any` and `tool` return a 400 error (2026-09-01 onward; [release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview)), so forced-tool patterns need strict tool use or structured outputs.

### How strong it is

All of this is vendor guidance and case reports, read in full for Anthropic, Google's developer blog and the MCP blog. OpenAI's practice is known only through Cookbook articles on GitHub (some relying on third-party tools) and snippets; its "Harness engineering" post was not opened. No system card from any lab was opened; system-card methods are known only through Anthropic's descriptions. The MCP specification text itself was read by one note file and not by another; findings here come from the changelog, the security guides and the official blog. The headless flag reference pages are undated living documentation, so only dated changelog entries are cited. No lab source gives guidance for evaluating a personal store with a single annotator, a soak-period length, or a rule for when sub-agents hurt.

## Assistants, confirmation load and bilingual speech

### What the evidence supports

**Compute durations, counts and rankings in SQL and give the model a ledger.** In the one direct measurement of activity summaries, answers from an LLM-written day summary scored 66.1% (Sonnet 4.5) and 80.4% (Opus 4.5), answers from raw rows 82.1% and 91.1%, and answers from a deterministically compiled ledger 98.4% for both. Models did not invent applications or sites (0% on absent-fact probes); they failed on magnitudes, with one summary inflating an application's time 2.9 times and fabricating a session spilling into the next day. The compiled block was byte-identical across runs (authors' own, one user, 8 days, 64 questions, full text; [arXiv 2608.05784, 2026-08-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)). The same paper found intervals ending in an idle heartbeat carried 45% of credited time, so app-usage time should be labelled as foreground presence, with gaps reported separately.

**Review fewer, larger, evidence-bearing units.** Users rejected 39% of plans but 3% of individual permission requests (vendor telemetry; [Claude blog, 2026-08-07](https://claude.com/blog/auto-mode-default-in-claude-code)). Reviewing the top 10% of items by a stronger-model risk score captured 81.6% of major errors in one translation study (authors' own, single author, abstract; [arXiv 2609.14963, 2026-09-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14963)). Limiting agents to one to three questions cut a user-attention cost by 36–73% while keeping 86–94% of task reward (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2610.24411, 2026-10-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24411)). The pattern these support: auto-accept reversible, evidence-backed items that pass checks; ask about items where checks disagree or consequences are external (commitments with dates and owners, merges, supersessions); audit a random sample of auto-accepted items; and occasionally plant a known-wrong card, the method Anthropic used to measure vigilance. Grouping by project is an inference from a coding product, not a tested result for note-taking.

**Keep work in scheduled runs and make confirm-before-write a hard gate.** In a rated-scenario study with 30 participants, willingness to accept proactive help rose from 26.7% for correct help that competes with ongoing work to 97.8% for sleep-time help, and one intervention at the wrong depth cost 1.86 trust points against 1.27 regained by a correct one (authors' own, full text; [arXiv 2609.37267, 2026-09-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37267)). Persistent memory led agents to treat unacknowledged proposals as user instructions (authors' own, abstract; [arXiv 2610.18684, 2026-10-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18684)), so unconfirmed items must never be retrieved as facts.

**Run a side-by-side speech trial before committing to Whisper large-v3-turbo.** Within single sources, turbo scores 13.88–16.13% WER on English meeting speech (AMI) against 9.42–11.39% for Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3, which covers 25 European languages including Portuguese ([Phonon-2 card, 2026-09-28](https://huggingface.co/FermionResearch/Phonon-2), vendor; [arXiv 2604.14493, 2026-04-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14493), authors' measurements of third-party models). Parakeet runs 28–45 times real time in open runtimes on 8 server cores (vendor-reported, English, short utterances; [Moondream card, 2026-09-18](https://huggingface.co/moondream/parakeet-redux)). The test needs owner-recorded audio with separate WER for Portuguese-only, English-only and mixed segments.

**Gate every track with voice-activity detection before decoding.** On 4,421 real clips confirmed to contain no speech, every OpenAI Whisper checkpoint emitted words on 100%; turbo wrote text on 96.7% of music clips and has "the worst repetition profile of the three base models" (95th-percentile repeat ratio 18.3 against 1.00 for large-v3). A released lexicon of known hallucination phrases matches 72.1% of turbo's outputs on blank clips, but "only once the audio is already known to be blank" (authors' own, partial full text; [arXiv 2609.32560, 2026-09-26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32560)). Two-track recording makes this the normal condition, since each track is silent while the other party talks.

### What it warns against

**Free-form LLM clean-up of transcripts.** Text-only correction broke 13–32% of already-correct transcripts; with audio available, 1–3% (authors' own, partial full text; prompts differed between arms; [arXiv 2610.30385, 2026-10-21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30385)). Limit edits to a glossary of names and project terms and measure break rate.

**Assuming Portuguese benchmark scores describe European Portuguese meetings.** Zero-shot Whisper large-v3 scored 15.62% WER on in-domain and 25.21% on out-of-distribution European Portuguese; full fine-tuning helped Portuguese but degraded English (authors' own, partial full text; [arXiv 2603.05354, 2026-03-05](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05354)). Reported Portuguese scores for new models are read speech and largely Brazilian (independent; [arXiv 2609.36920, 2026-09-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36920)). Models that expect a language tag and monolingual audio, such as Cohere Transcribe, fit code-switching poorly by their vendor's own statement ([Cohere, 2026-03-26](https://huggingface.co/blog/CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026-release)).

**Reading a high accept rate as accuracy, or LLM-judged proactivity as trust.** LLM judges "conflate task capability and trust" and tolerate misaligned interventions humans penalise ([arXiv 2609.37267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37267)); showing step-level uncertainty reduced users' external verification (independent user study, abstract; [arXiv 2605.28571, 2026-05-27](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28571)).

**Expecting useful proactive output from day one.** In a one-week study with 10 participants, 6 had zero F1 on day 1, rising to 0.515 overall by day 7 from thumb signals, with the observer on a 9B model and an RTX 5090, not a CPU (authors' own, full text; [arXiv 2611.13457, 2026-11-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13457)).

### How strong it is

The approval-fatigue numbers are the strongest human data in the report, and all of them are vendor-reported by Anthropic on a coding product, without independent replication; the planted-command task differs from confirming a decision. No 2026 source gives a safe daily confirmation count: 25 a day sits below the 50-prompt point where catch rates were lowest, but vigilance was only about 17% even early. The claim that more escalation can lower safety is a simulation, as its authors state ([arXiv 2606.08919, 2026-06-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08919)). The activity-summary result is one person's corpus. For speech, **no 2026 source evaluates Portuguese–English code-switching, European Portuguese meeting speech on any 2026 model, two-track attribution, or Whisper large-v3-turbo speed on a laptop CPU**; CPU claims are vendor figures on server cores, and figures for the same base model differ between sources (Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 averages 4.96, 6.26 and 6.32 in three places), so comparisons hold only within one source. Lab products in this space (OpenAI's Chronicle, 2026-04-20; Google's Daily Brief, 2026-05-19) are snippet-only with no evaluation data.

## Conclusion

The 2026 literature reframes where an LLM belongs in a system like this one. The model proposes: it writes claims, wiki prose, queries and merge candidates. Code decides: which value is current, what a citation points at, how long something took, where a claim came from, what may be written or deleted. Each area's failures cluster where that line was blurred, whether a memory system letting a model classify its own updates, a summary doing arithmetic, a citation checked only for existence, or an approval card narrated by the agent it is meant to check. The layer already draws this line in most places; the improvement plan is mostly a matter of extending it to supersession, support checking, origin labels, deletion and the daily ledger.

The second implication concerns order of work. Because benchmark scores move by several points with the judge, the reader or the harness, and because no source covers Portuguese–English work data, almost no published number transfers directly. The owner's own evaluation is the only instrument that can decide whether an embedder, a reranker, a verifier model or a different speech model earns its dependency. A frozen database copy with a SQL oracle, a few hundred real queries with written relevance notes, about a hundred blind-labelled claim and citation pairs, and a small bilingual audio set would cost little and would convert every recommendation here from an extrapolation into a measurement. The owner's confirm and reject history, which no published system has, is the natural calibration set for all of it.
