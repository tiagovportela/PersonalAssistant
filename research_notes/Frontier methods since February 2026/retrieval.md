Research date: 2026-11-27. Date rule applied: every source below was first published on or after 2026-02-01 (arXiv IDs 2602.xxxxx or later, with the v1 date written next to each; lab posts and model releases with their dates). ## 1. Hybrid retrieval since Feb 2026: BM25 + dense with rank fusion, learned sparse, late interaction, rerankers; which gains hold on small corpora and short texts

### Takeaway
Fusing BM25 with a dense retriever still wins in aggregate in almost every 2026 study, but the gains over a strong lexical baseline are small (often 0.01–0.06 nDCG/MRR), fusion hurts a sizeable minority of individual queries, untuned RRF can score below its own dense component, and no per-query router has beaten a fixed fusion. On short conversational texts fusion gains are largest where BM25 is not already saturated; off-the-shelf cross-encoder rerankers repeatedly failed to help or hurt there. Late interaction shows the clearest quality edge (including on native Portuguese), learned sparse has no small multilingual CPU evidence in this window.

### Cited Findings
**Fusion: aggregate gain, per-query cost**
- "The Fusion Tax" (Anserini BM25, five dense encoders 22M–596M, SPLADE, cross-encoder; seven BEIR sets): even the best fusion (dense-weighted score interpolation) ranks relevant documents below the better of its two components on 13–48% of queries and below dense-only on 8–21%, while beating dense-only in aggregate on every corpus with the primary encoder (BGE-small) and on 33 of 35 encoder–corpus pairs, by only 0.008–0.051 nDCG@10; the affected 8–21% of queries lose 0.04–0.21 nDCG@10 on average. The tax does not shrink with a stronger encoder (flat across BGE-small/base/large and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) and persists with SPLADE in place of BM25. [independent] — [arXiv 2610.30612, v1 2026-10-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)
- Same paper, RRF versus weighted interpolation: on the five corpora where dense clearly leads, dense-weighted interpolation (weight about 0.7 on dense) beats RRF, RRF's per-query tax is higher (20–40% vs 13–36%) and RRF's net change against dense-only is negative; a weighted RRF with the same 0.7/0.3 weights closes 25–73% of the gap. Where sparse and dense are balanced (TREC-COVID) RRF and CombMNZ beat dense-heavy interpolation by 0.08–0.09 nDCG@10. [independent] — [arXiv 2610.30612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)
- Same paper, decision framework: measure BM25 and dense nDCG@10 query by query on a labelled sample of the target corpus (200–400 representative queries as a starting point; CI half-width about 0.02–0.05 at 200 queries); if dense leads by about 0.04 nDCG@10 or more, use interpolation with dense weight 0.6–0.7; if balanced, use RRF, CombMNZ or weight 0.5; treat dense-only as a live option; prefer a static per-corpus policy to per-query routing; re-measure whenever the encoder changes. [independent] — [arXiv 2610.30612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)
- Same paper, cost on one Apple M5 workstation: adding BM25 to a dense stack costs 1.1–4.3 ms per query (median) and an 8–266 MB index; BGE-small encodes a query in 5–7 ms and 40 abstracts/s on CPU (784/s on Quora's short questions), BGE-large 5 abstracts/s (83/s on short questions); the bge-reranker-v2-m3 cross-encoder scored 19 pairs/s on the integrated GPU, about 1.6 s per query at depth 30. [independent] — [arXiv 2610.30612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)
- Untuned RRF can be worse than its dense half: in a pre-specified RAG indexing study the default hybrid (untuned RRF of BM25 and nomic-embed-text) scored below its own dense component on Qasper (nomic alone beat the hybrid by +5.1 points for plain section-prefixed chunks); the authors' advice is "check that the hybrid fusion beats its dense part". [independent] — [arXiv 2603.23533, v1 2026-03-08 (v4 read)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)
- Dutch retrieval (five MTEB-NL sets, 14,500 queries, 786,573 documents, weighted RRF, 10-fold CV): fusing BM25 with Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B improves MRR over the best single retriever by 0.061, 0.029, 0.004 and 0.025 on four sets and ties BM25 on the fifth; macro MRR is fusion 0.692, Qwen 0.650, BM25 0.636. Leave-one-dataset-out selection chose equal BM25–Qwen weights every time. [independent] — [arXiv 2608.02112, v1 2026-08-03](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02112)
- Bilingual English–Marathi government-scheme search (manually curated bilingual queries): BM25 MRR 0.864; hybrid 0.872 (IndicSBERT) and 0.879 (MahaSBERT); dense-only 0.663 and 0.655. The authors call the gain over a strong lexical baseline "modest and metric-dependent". [authors' own] — [arXiv 2611.26514, v1 2026-11-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26514) **(abstract)**
- Low-resource Khmer with mixed Khmer–English usage (3K documents, 300 queries): character n-gram BM25 was best (Recall 0.943, nDCG 0.876); hybrid BM25+dense 0.929 / 0.871; dense alone lower. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2608.21365, v1 2026-06-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21365) **(abstract)**
- Financial filings (FinDER; short, acronym-heavy queries): fusing BM25 with a compact dense encoder improved reference-level Hit@10 by roughly 28% over either component; an oracle per-query weight had 21.8% headroom but none of three lightweight routers gave a statistically reliable gain over the fixed blend. Methodological point: if the retrieval unit is longer than the dense encoder's input window the dense model never sees much of the evidence, which confounds sparse-vs-dense comparisons. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2608.00183, v1 2026-07-31](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00183) **(abstract)**
- Code search (Repo-Premium, preregistered lockbox of 539 queries over 91 repositories): RRF of dense and whole-file BM25 scored 0.609 nDCG@10 on development against 0.550 dense and 0.507 BM25, and 0.551 vs 0.533 vs 0.432 on the lockbox; a listwise reranker (jina-reranker-v3) over the same candidates did no better than fusion (0.574 dev); a frozen per-query router gained +0.005 over always fusing against +0.077 for the oracle. [independent] — [arXiv 2611.20455, v1 2026-11-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20455)
- Local 7B agentic RAG ablation (HotpotQA, 5,000 questions): fixed RRF hybrid beat rule-based adaptive routing by +1.8 EM / +1.9 F1; two retrieval iterations captured 95% of the gain of five. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2606.21553, v1 2026-06-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21553) **(abstract)**
- Agent-memory "entity-collision" protocol: adaptive vector-weight routing on LoCoMo is "a measured null" (11.7 pp oracle headroom, no tested signal recovers it); a 2.7x larger BGE-large did not uniformly beat MiniLM-384 (wins on intent-style queries, loses on lexical ones). [authors' own] — [arXiv 2605.29630, v1 2026-05-28](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29630) **(abstract)**

**Short texts and conversational/personal memory**
- Training-free, CPU-only retrieval over conversation turns: score-level fusion of a late-interaction (max query–turn similarity) dense score with BM25 added +8.8 to +17.2 points of LoCoMo Hit@1 over late interaction alone across six encoders, reaching Hit@1 0.752 (+11.2 pp over BM25) with e5-large-v2. On LongMemEval-S, "a lexical regime where BM25 saturates", the net fusion gain over BM25 was small and not significant. An off-the-shelf web-search cross-encoder reranker over the fused top-10 degraded Hit@1 by 6.9 pp (one reranker, one configuration). Dense helps most on multi-hop and temporal questions and trails BM25 on adversarial ones. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2606.04194, v1 2026-06-02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04194) **(abstract)**
- SelRoute (LongMemEval_M): a zero-ML SQLite FTS5 baseline reached NDCG@5 0.692, "already exceeding all published baselines on ranking quality", which the authors attribute partly to implementation differences in lexical retrieval; routing by query type reached Recall@5 0.800 with bge-base (109M) and 0.786 with bge-small (33M). Full-text search excels at verbatim keyword matching, embedding search at paraphrase. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2604.02431, v1 2026-04-02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02431)
- vstash (single SQLite file, sqlite-vec + FTS5): per-query IDF-weighted adaptive RRF improved NDCG@10 on all five BEIR sets tested versus fixed weights (up to +21.4% on ArguAna; 0.7263 on SciFact with BGE-small, 33M); a negative result on post-fusion scoring: "frequency+decay, history-augmented recall, and cross-encoder reranking all failed to improve NDCG". [authors' own] — [arXiv 2604.15484, v1 2026-04-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15484) **(abstract)**
- "RAG in a Trenchcoat": a deliberately minimal memory (chained notes, hybrid BM25 + cosine retrieval, chronological rendering, one LLM call per ingestion or query, about 5–7K reader tokens per query) reaches parity within bootstrap CI with the prior state of the art on LongMemEval-S and BEAM and beats the best memory baseline on EverMemBench by 5.7–6.3 points. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2611.22179, v1 2026-11-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22179) **(abstract)**
- Evidence-preservation benchmark under token budgets: BM25 achieves 0.921 supporting-fact recall on BABILong at 128K; dense retrieval achieves 0.993 answer-bearing-turn recall on LongMemEval at 64K; method rankings vary by evidence type and between recall and downstream accuracy. [independent] — [arXiv 2611.10238, v1 2026-11-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10238) **(abstract)**

**Mixed-language queries**
- Code-switching retrieval (English–Chinese human-annotated code-switched queries, four datasets; ACL 2026 Findings): average nDCG@10 drops under code-switching by −6.56 for BM25 (37.59 → 31.03; Touché 60.32 → 37.68), −2.38 for bge-m3 (42.03 → 39.65), −6.90 for mE5-large, −4.74 for Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (65.11 → 60.37), −11.31 for ColBERT v2 and −12.36 for all-MiniLM-L12-v2 (English-only models suffer most). On the 11-task CS-MTEB the decline is up to 27%. [independent] — [arXiv 2604.17632, v1 2026-04-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17632)

**Late interaction (multi-vector)**
- LightOn's open recipe: with the same mmBERT-base backbone, data and objectives (307M parameters, translate-train into eight languages), the dense model "is strong on English and translated languages but degrades outside translate-train support, whereas the late-interaction model generalizes better to unseen languages and scripts". English 149M models: DenseOn 56.20 and LateOn 57.22 average nDCG@10 on BEIR. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2607.27178, v1 2026-07-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27178) **(abstract)**
- On native Brazilian-Portuguese retrieval (MTEB-BR v1, six retrieval tasks, mean nDCG@10) the late-interaction twin beats the dense twin: mLateOn 0.708 vs mDenseOn 0.648 (both 307M, Apache-2.0). Other multi-vector entries: pplx-embed-v2-late-0.6b 0.728, LFM2.5-ColBERT-350M 0.630, SauerkrautLM-MoEdernColBERT-115M 0.567, -27M 0.537, -1.5M 0.489. [independent benchmark; table computed from the public leaderboard data] — [MTEB-BR leaderboard data, generated 2026-11-26](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MTEB-BR/mteb-pt-results)
- SmartSearch: NER-weighted substring matching for recall plus a CrossEncoder+ColBERT rank-fusion stage "running on CPU in ~650ms" reaches 93.5% on LoCoMo and 88.4% on LongMemEval-S; oracle analysis: recall reaches 98.6% but without intelligent ranking only 22.5% of gold evidence survives truncation to the token budget. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2603.15599, v1 2026-03-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15599) **(abstract)**
- SmallReason-ColBERT (32M) reaches 21.41 mean nDCG@10 on BRIGHT, within 1.21 of the 150M Reason-ModernColBERT. English, reasoning-focused. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2609.29652, v1 2026-08-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29652) **(abstract)**

**Learned sparse**
- Query-reformulation reproducibility study notes SPLADE "already performs neural term expansion during encoding", so LLM-written expansions that help BM25 add little or slightly hurt under SPLADE. [independent] — [arXiv 2604.27421, v1 2026-04-30](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27421)
- Naver Labs at WSDM Cup 2026 (English queries over Chinese, Persian, Russian news): a learned sparse model (SPLARE-7B) was competitive with dense baselines such as Qwen3-8B-Embed. This is a 7B model, not a CPU option. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2602.20986, v1 2026-02-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20986) **(abstract)**

**Rerankers**
- Fusion Tax: a cross-encoder recovers part of the fusion tax only where the reranker is itself reliable; it "largely reshuffles which queries pay the tax" (SciFact: 27 of 30 originally hurt queries repaired, 17 others newly hurt). Where a reliable reranker is already in place, adding the second retriever added at most 0.011 nDCG@10 except on balanced corpora. [independent] — [arXiv 2610.30612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)
- Indexing study: a reranker over the hybrid's top 30 was one of the two strongest of eight retrievers (with qwen3-embedding); recommended "if the index cannot change". [independent] — [arXiv 2603.23533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)
- Optimal re-ranking depth: many queries have a well-defined optimal depth beyond which effectiveness stagnates or degrades; a predictor from LLM-assessed first-stage quality cut average depth by up to 3x at equal effectiveness; oracle depths improve effectiveness by more than 7% with 5x less depth on MS MARCO dev. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2611.05908, v1 2026-11-04](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05908) **(abstract)**

### Inferences
- For a corpus of tens of thousands of short PT/EN texts with hand-written key terms, BM25 is likely already strong; the realistic prize from adding a dense retriever is a few points of aggregate recall concentrated in paraphrase, multi-hop and cross-language queries, at the price of some individual queries getting worse. The Fusion Tax protocol (measure the sparse–dense balance on 200–400 of one's own queries, then pick interpolation weight or RRF) is the evidence-backed way to decide.
- Mixed PT/EN queries are a specific weakness of pure BM25 (largest code-switching drop on some datasets) and of English-only encoders; a multilingual dense or late-interaction component is the piece most likely to add value there. The cited evidence is English–Chinese and English–Marathi, not Portuguese–English.
- Reranking is not a safe default for short work-event texts: two independent negative results on conversational memory and one "reshuffles the tax" result suggest testing on own data before paying 1–2 s of CPU per query.
- A fixed, measured fusion policy is better supported than any per-query router: four separate 2026 studies report router nulls.

### Gaps
- No study since Feb 2026 was found that measures hybrid retrieval specifically on a mixed Portuguese–English private corpus of short texts; the nearest evidence is English–Marathi, Khmer–English, English–Chinese code-switching and conversational-memory benchmarks.
- No small multilingual learned-sparse (SPLADE-style) model with CPU measurements and Portuguese results was found in this window; the learned-sparse literature found is about index efficiency or 7B-scale models.
- Reranker latency on a plain x86 laptop CPU for 2026 small rerankers was not found; the only timing located is 19 pairs/s for bge-reranker-v2-m3 on an Apple M5 integrated GPU.

## 2. Small embedding and reranker models released or benchmarked since Feb 2026 that run on CPU: sizes, speed, Portuguese and cross-lingual quality, static embeddings, quantisation, Matryoshka

### Takeaway
A new tier of sub-150M multilingual encoders (Bekko a8m/a25m, Granite 97M R2, a Portuguese-tuned 151M model) now beats multilingual-e5-small by 5–15 points on native Portuguese retrieval; the two families with CPU measurements (Bekko, Granite 97M) encode on a desktop CPU at roughly 125–364 documents per second, while the ~300M tier (EmbeddingGemma-class, mDenseOn, jina-v5-nano) adds about another 5 points. Multilingual leaderboards predict Portuguese rank only moderately (Spearman 0.75), static (model2vec-style) embeddings score far lower on Portuguese retrieval and add nothing to a BM25+transformer fusion, and int8 or binary vectors with float rescoring are close to lossless.

### Inferences
- On native Portuguese retrieval the step from multilingual-e5-small (0.507) to a 2026 sub-150M model (0.557–0.599 for Apache/MIT options, 0.652 for the Gemma-licensed Portuguese model) is larger than the step from there to the 300M tier (about 0.61–0.65). For a few tens of thousands of short texts, the sub-150M tier at 125–364 docs/s on a desktop-class CPU means a full re-embed in minutes; a laptop CPU will be slower than the Ryzen 9 7950X used in the measurement, by an unmeasured factor.
- Leaderboard choice matters for Portuguese: harrier-270m and granite-97m look strong on MMTEB multilingual retrieval (66.4, 60.3) and much weaker on native PT-BR (0.576, 0.557), while embeddinggemma-300m and bekko-a25m hold up. Any pick should be confirmed on the owner's own PT/EN queries.
- Static embeddings are not a substitute for a small transformer encoder on Portuguese retrieval (10–20 points lower) and one controlled study found they add nothing once BM25 and a transformer are fused. Whether a static model adds anything to BM25 alone, with no transformer, was not measured in the sources read.
- Store vectors as int8 or binary plus the float copy for rescoring a top-100: both sources that tested rescoring found it near-lossless, while quantisation without rescoring is clearly lossy for retrieval.
- Licences narrow the field for a pinned, reviewed local stack: Apache-2.0/MIT options with Portuguese evidence are Bekko, Granite R2, mDenseOn/mLateOn/mSlideOn, harrier, F2LLM-v2 and pplx-embed; jina v5 and jina-reranker-v3.5 are non-commercial; colibri-embed-ptbr and embeddinggemma-300m carry the Gemma licence.

### Gaps
- No Portuguese–English cross-lingual retrieval benchmark (query in one language, document in the other) published since Feb 2026 was found. MTEB-BR and MTEB-PT are monolingual Portuguese; the cross-lingual and code-switching evidence is for other language pairs.
- CPU latency for encoding a single short query (not batch throughput) on a typical laptop was not reported for the 2026 small models; the Bekko figures are batch-64 throughput on a 16-core desktop CPU and a Raspberry Pi 5. The 300M-tier models (embeddinggemma, mDenseOn, jina v5 nano) were not measured on x86 CPU under that protocol.
- CPU measurements for the new small rerankers (Lychee Nano, Querit A0.4B, LAMAR-600m) were not found.
- MMTEB itself: no 2026 update paper to the benchmark was opened; MMTEB numbers above are as reported by the Bekko and Granite papers from the official leaderboard snapshot.

## 3. Agentic search (several keyword searches, grep-style) versus embedding retrieval: accuracy, cost, latency, for documents and code; query rewriting and expansion; cross-lingual expansion

### Takeaway
With 2026 frontier models, an agent that issues its own lexical searches matches or beats embedding retrieval on small and medium private corpora, and the best-supported design is agent control on top of a ranked lexical index (not raw grep, which collapses as the corpus grows, and not a single dense top-k). The harness and the model matter as much as the retriever. LLM-written query expansion reliably helps BM25 and much less so dense or learned-sparse retrieval; for code, a prebuilt semantic or structural index was cheaper and sometimes more accurate than delegated grep exploration.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The owner's current architecture (Claude calling FTS5 through MCP tools) is the configuration the strongest independent result favours: agent control over a ranked lexical index. At a few tens of thousands of short texts the corpus is far below the roughly 10M-token crossover where raw grep-style exploration starts losing to ranked BM25, so either works, but ranked FTS5 costs an order of magnitude fewer tokens.
- Because expansion helps BM25 most and the calling model is already a frontier LLM, the cheapest improvement is on the query side: have the agent issue several searches with bilingual keyword variants and exact rare terms (names, identifiers, error strings) rather than prose paraphrases. The direct evidence is for English code and web search; the bilingual part is an extrapolation.
- Tool design is a first-order variable: inline results, context expansion around a hit, temporal narrowing and de-duplication of already-seen items each moved accuracy as much as the retriever choice in the cited studies.
- An embedding index is still justified as a complement for coverage (paraphrase, cross-language) rather than as a replacement; the code-QA result shows that delegating search to sub-agents can lose more at the hand-off than it saves in context.

### Gaps
- No controlled study since Feb 2026 was found on cross-lingual query expansion by an LLM (for example a Portuguese question expanded with English keywords and vice versa) against a multilingual embedding baseline.
- Latency of agentic search loops is rarely reported in comparable units; token counts are the usual proxy.
- The grep-versus-vector studies use conversational memory, web-style and code corpora; none uses a mixed store of Git commits, browsing events and wiki pages.

## 4. Indexing-time enrichment: contextual chunk descriptions, LLM-written keywords or questions per chunk, summaries

### Takeaway
One LLM call per chunk that writes a title, summary, keywords and questions acts as doc2query-style document expansion: it clearly helps BM25 (+8.7 recall points in the one pre-specified study) and matches Anthropic-style contextual retrieval at a fraction of the tokens, but adds nothing detectable once a strong embedder or reranker is in place. Enrichment that helps lexical search can degrade embedding search, compressed "distilled" text hurts BM25, and an agent-built wiki helps most as a complement to search on multi-document questions.

### Cited Findings
- "Upgrade the Retriever First" (plan made public before any index was built; 951 Qasper and 235 FreshStack questions; eight retrievers; 7B and 120B indexing LLMs; one call per chunk returning title, summary, 5–8 keywords and 2–3 questions indexed before the chunk text): under the default hybrid, enrichment beats a free section-path prefix by +5.5 recall points on Qasper (95% CI [+2.9, +8.1]); on FreshStack +2.7 does not survive Holm adjustment. Under BM25 alone it gains +8.7 on Qasper (+2.4 on FreshStack). Under qwen3-embedding or a reranker the gain is within equivalence margins (+1.5 / +0.3 and +0.4 / +0.8). [independent; v1–v2 results of the author's own tool were withdrawn] — [arXiv 2603.23533, v1 2026-03-08 (v4 read)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)
- Same paper, mechanism: for 20% of Qasper questions the generated fields supply a question term the chunk and its section path lack; under BM25 those "bridged" questions gain +24.4 points against +3.2 for the others, and carry 56% of the BM25 gain. On FreshStack, whose questions use the documentation's own vocabulary, bridging was within noise. "Corpora whose users query in the documents' own vocabulary should expect less from enrichment; corpora searched with lexical retrieval and outsider vocabulary should expect more." [independent] — [arXiv 2603.23533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)
- Same paper, contextual retrieval: enrichment is equivalent to contextual retrieval (whole document sent with every chunk), which used 5,885 and 8,813 input tokens per call against about 1,015 for enrichment. One call for all fields matches four calls, one per field. Add-ons built on the call (rolling key dictionary, key-based merging, related-key expansion) did not beat LLM-free controls; related-key expansion lowered Qasper recall by 10.0 points; late chunking lowered Qasper recall by 11.4 points. [independent] — [arXiv 2603.23533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)
- Same paper, chunking and ordering of spend: header-led chunks beat 512-character windows (Qasper +7.5, FreshStack +3.1) but lost to overlapping 256-token windows on FreshStack (−2.9); the section-path prefix itself had no detectable effect. Advice in order: stronger embedder if the corpus can be re-embedded, reranker if it cannot, header-led chunks, and "enrich mainly for lexical retrieval"; for a "keyword-only stack (no vectors, no reranker)" the guide's recommendation is enrichment. The free prefix under qwen3-embedding beat hybrid enrichment by +5.5 on Qasper; the best 120B-indexed configuration still led the best free one by +2.5 (exploratory). [independent] — [arXiv 2603.23533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)
- Same paper, limits: two English benchmarks, retrieval within single papers or capped documentation corpora, small retrievers, no learned-sparse or multi-vector baseline, query-time cost not measured. [independent] — [arXiv 2603.23533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)
- SelRoute: rule-based storage-time vocabulary enrichment (for example a 210-entry hypernym map) "helps lexical search but degrades embedding search", which the authors use to motivate separate indexed text per pipeline. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2604.02431, v1 2026-04-02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02431)
- Structured distillation of one user's agent conversations (14,340 exchanges compressed from 371 to 38 tokens each; 201 recall queries; 5 LLM graders): the best distilled configuration reaches 96% of the best verbatim MRR (0.717 vs 0.745); all 20 vector-search configurations show no significant loss, while all 20 BM25 configurations degrade significantly; the best cross-layer setup (verbatim plus distilled) slightly exceeds verbatim alone (MRR 0.759). [authors' own] — [arXiv 2603.13017, v1 2026-03-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13017) **(abstract)**
- CAMI: choosing which enrichment views (synthetic queries, summaries) to materialise under a budget beats content-only baselines by up to 9.4% recall@10 in challenging settings, with up to 5x less search budget than random search. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2606.28365, v1 2026-06-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28365) **(abstract)**
- "Context as Infrastructure": under a competent search harness, compiled structural artefacts "gave no detectable accuracy gain on FRAMES, and a PageIndex-style outline degraded at scale"; a navigation tree whose leaves are claim–evidence pairs cuts query tokens by 91% when the evidence suffices and adds 1.9% when it does not (38% average reduction; measured sufficiency rate near 43%); a decision gate cuts latency from 140 s to 30 s. - Anthropic, agent-built wikis: on 24 merger questions, more than half needing several documents, Claude scored 87% consulting the wiki against 73% with search alone; on a task where criteria could be met from a single document by text search the wiki showed no clear gain; on another the wiki grew larger than the source documents and "effectively abstracted nothing". [vendor] — [Anthropic engineering post, 2026-11-06](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)
- "Storage Is Not Memory": "extraction at ingestion is the wrong primitive for agent memory: content discarded before the query is known cannot be recovered at retrieval time"; a retrieval pipeline over verbatim events in a single SQLite file on commodity CPU reports 93.0% on LoCoMo and 87.8% on LongMemEval. [authors' own, product technical report] — [arXiv 2605.04897, v1 2026-05-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04897) **(abstract)**
- Personal fact memory: without update resolution, 70.3% of prompts expose a superseded value; serving only the active value of each correctly keyed slot eliminates observed stale exposure; with a shared active store, participant-aware BM25 is equivalent to the reference ranker within a pre-specified 0.02 margin. The hard part is assigning revisions to the right slot (LLM key assigners had higher key recall but lower clean-retrieval rates than a rule extractor). [authors' own] — [arXiv 2610.10265, v1 2026-10-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10265) **(abstract)**

### Inferences
- The owner's hand-written Portuguese and English key terms per wiki page are document expansion for BM25, the setting where the 2026 evidence shows the largest gain. Extending the same treatment (keywords and likely questions in both languages, one call per item) to claims and event summaries is the enrichment with the best support for an FTS5-only stack; bilingual keywords specifically are an extrapolation from the vocabulary-bridging mechanism, not something a cited study tested.
- If a dense index is added, embed the original text and keep the enrichment fields in the FTS5 index only: two sources report that enrichment gains vanish or reverse for embeddings.
- Keep verbatim source text searchable next to any compiled or distilled layer: distilled text hurt BM25, verbatim plus distilled together was best, and quoting key terms word for word fixed wiki fact loss.
- For "claims" that are superseded over time, validity filtering at construction (serve the active value) matters more than the ranker.

### Gaps
- No study since Feb 2026 was found that measures LLM-written bilingual keywords or questions on a mixed-language corpus.
- The one pre-specified enrichment study is English-only and searches within single documents; effects for disambiguating between many short documents are untested there.

## 5. Long context versus retrieval since Feb 2026: context-rot results on current models, and when to load whole pages instead of snippets

### Takeaway
2026 results point to distraction, not length, as the cause of retrieval failures in long contexts: single-fact lookup at 1M tokens is solved by the strongest models, but multi-hop reasoning, rival or similar-looking statements and long agent trajectories still degrade, and whole-collection prompting buys a few points of accuracy at roughly 26x the tokens. Whole pages are worth loading when the answer needs several facts or document-level context from one page; ranked snippets win when the context would otherwise fill with look-alike distractors.

### Cited Findings
- "Length Is Cheap, Competition Is Not" (five open models of 0.5B–3B parameters, 126,473 trials): neutral filler costs nothing (accuracy 0.97–1.00 up to 32K tokens on Qwen2.5-1.5B); statements in the same format as the evidence drop accuracy to 0.16–0.33; a single statement giving the queried key a rival value multiplies the loss by 9 to 110; attention mass left on the evidence explains up to 58% of the variance in loss, log length at most 8%. Small open models only. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2611.04169, v1 2026-11-03](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04169) **(abstract)**
- Five frontier models with 1M-token windows on a classical Chinese corpus: single-needle retrieval at 1M is "essentially solved" (Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 each 100%); three-hop chain traversal shows three decay patterns: stable (Gemini Pro, Claude: above 80% through 512K, modest degradation at 1M), late cliff (GPT-5.5, Qwen3.6-plus: sharp collapse between 512K and 1M) and smooth decline (DeepSeek V4 Pro). "Nominal context-window length is a poor proxy for usable long-context multi-hop capability." [independent] — [arXiv 2605.02173, v1 2026-05-04](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02173) **(abstract)**
- ATLAS (26 models, 6,438 instances, length-aware scoring over an 8K–1M grid): Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview leads at 128K, Claude-Opus-4.6 leads at 1M; rankings reshuffle between the 8K–128K and 8K–1M scores (7 models move by at least two ranks); strong retrieval "need not transfer to downstream use". [independent] — [arXiv 2605.28079, v1 2026-05-27](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28079) **(abstract)**
- Long-horizon search with four open flagship models (GLM-4.7, GLM-5.0, Qwen3.5-397B, MiniMax-M2.5; 200K–256K windows) on BrowseComp, BrowseComp-Plus and xbench-DeepSearch: under extensive context "models give up or provide uncertain incorrect answers long before exhausting the context window", even on easy and moderate queries; context-management methods work as test-time scaling by lowering peak context length; behaviour-aware filtering of parallel samples gains 2.6–4.9%. Closed models were excluded because their reasoning traces are encrypted. [independent] — [arXiv 2606.29718, v1 2026-06-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29718)
- LOCA-bench: agent performance "generally degrades as the environment states grow more complex", while "advanced context management techniques can substantially improve the overall success rate". [independent] — [arXiv 2602.07962, v1 2026-02-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07962) **(abstract)**
- Evidence-based generation across ten proprietary frontier and three open-weight LLMs: two RAG limitations that long-context reading avoids are that "similarity retrieval surfaces any relevant chunk far more reliably than all required chunks for multi-fact queries" and that "chunking destroys document meta-information needed to resolve identity-bearing queries". [independent] — [arXiv 2610.26951, v1 2026-10-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26951) **(abstract)**
- "Token tax" case study (manufacturing safety training, 972 answers, two small language models): loading the whole document collection reached 73.1% correctness against 65.4% for semantic RAG, at 26 times the per-query token cost. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2606.20898, v1 2026-06-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20898) **(abstract)**
- UNREAL: removing distractors before generation raises NoLiMa accuracy from 1.0% to 24.83% at 128K tokens and LV-Eval F1 from 49.97% to 54.66% at 256K. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2610.08463, v1 2026-10-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08463) **(abstract)**
- Agent-with-search setting, frontier models (Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4, Gemini 3.1 Pro/Flash-Lite): as unrelated sessions are added from 5 sessions to the full haystack of 39–66, accuracy changes little and non-monotonically for both grep and vector tools (for example Chronos + Opus 4.6 grep 89.3% → 89.7%, vector 94.0% → 92.2%; Chronos + GPT-5.4 grep 83.2% → 78.5%, vector 88.8% → 82.8%). [authors' own] — [arXiv 2605.15184, v1 2026-05-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184)
- SmartSearch: with ranking and score-adaptive truncation it reaches 93.5% on LoCoMo and 88.4% on LongMemEval-S (above all memory systems it compares under the same protocol) "while using 8.5x fewer tokens than full-context baselines"; without ranking only 22.5% of gold evidence survives truncation to the token budget. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2603.15599, v1 2026-03-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15599) **(abstract)**
- Earlier-generation check (Grok-4, GPT-4, Gemini 2.5, GPT-5 on 20K social-media posts): performance degrades significantly beyond about 5K posts (70K tokens), falling to about 50–53% accuracy at 20K posts; "lost in the middle" is described as largely resolved in newer models. [independent] — [arXiv 2602.14188, v1 2026-02-15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14188) **(abstract)**
- Anthropic on always-loaded memory: promoted notes "use up valuable tokens, and an incorrect note can be carried into every session"; "longer instruction files reduce adherence", so "the top layer of the memory hierarchy has to stay relatively small"; Claude Code's auto memory loads only the first 200 lines or 25 KB of MEMORY.md, used as an index to what is stored where. [vendor] — [Anthropic engineering post, 2026-11-06](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)

### Inferences
- A wiki of a few hundred pages is small enough that loading one or two whole pages is cheap and avoids the two chunking failures named above (missing some of several required facts; losing the page's identity and context). The risk to manage is loading several near-duplicate or superseded pages or claims at once, since same-format rival statements are what the 2026 evidence shows to be damaging.
- A workable policy consistent with the sources: search returns ranked short snippets with identifiers; the agent opens the whole page when the question is multi-fact or the snippet lacks context; superseded claims are filtered before they reach the context; anything loaded into every session stays an index, not content.
- The benign result for frontier models as haystacks grow applies to agents that search and read selectively, not to dumping the corpus into the prompt.

### Gaps
- No 2026 vendor publication quantifying context rot on the newest frontier models was found on the reachable lab sites; the frontier-model evidence above comes from independent academic benchmarks, two of them read only as abstracts.
- No direct test of "whole wiki page versus snippet" for an LLM-compiled wiki was found; the conclusion is assembled from adjacent results.

## 6. Vector storage inside SQLite (sqlite-vec and similar) or other embedded options: maturity and 2026 status

### Takeaway
sqlite-vec resumed active development in 2026 (0.1.7, 0.1.8 and 0.1.9 between 17 and 31 March, with 0.1.10 alphas to 18 May) but is still a 0.1.x library whose ANN indexes are alpha; that does not matter at this corpus size, because exhaustive scans over tens of thousands of vectors take milliseconds. Two 2026 papers show FTS5 plus sqlite-vec in one SQLite file reaching near full-precision quality with binary vectors and rescoring, and several 2026 local-memory systems run on SQLite with FTS5 alone.

### Cited Findings
- sqlite-vec release history on PyPI: 0.1.7a10 on 2026-02-13; 0.1.7 on 2026-03-17; 0.1.8 on 2026-03-30; 0.1.9 on 2026-03-31 (latest stable); pre-releases 0.1.10a1–a3 on 2026-03-31/04-01 and 0.1.10a4 on 2026-05-18; the uploads listed immediately before these are dated 2025-01-10, a gap of about 13 months. — [PyPI JSON for sqlite-vec](https://pypi.org/pypi/sqlite-vec/json)
- The sqlite-vec maintainer wrote in March 2026 that "after a long hiatus" he was working on the project again, flagged ANN support as the biggest coming update, and an alpha release introduced new ANN indexes: rescore, IVF (experimental, not enabled) and DiskANN. — [GitHub releases page for sqlite-vec](https://github.com/asg017/sqlite-vec/releases) **(search snippet; page not reachable from this session)**
- A separate SQLite-team extension, vec1, providing approximate nearest-neighbour search through the virtual-table interface, was at version 0.7 according to a forum post dated 2026-07-07. — [sqlite.org vec1 documentation](https://sqlite.org/vec1/doc/trunk/doc/vec1.md) **(search snippet; sqlite.org not reachable from this session)**
- scrydb (MIT, `pip install scrydb`; runtime dependencies numpy and tqdm; sqlite-vec "ships prebuilt loadable binaries as a pip package"; FTS5 "is part of virtually every SQLite build shipped with CPython"): sqlite-vec stores float32, int8 or binary vectors in `vec0` virtual tables scanned exhaustively; binarisation and int8 scalar quantisation are done inside SQLite. Measured on an Apple M2 MacBook Air, no GPU, 4096-dim vectors: Hamming search takes 6.7 ms at 25K documents, 9.8 ms at 57K, 24.4 ms at 171K, 81.5 ms at 523K (about 0.15 ms per thousand documents plus 1.3 ms fixed); an int8 cosine scan costs on average 36x Hamming and a float scan a further 2.4x; FTS5 BM25 returning 1,000 hits takes 38.0 ms at 25K and 53.1 ms at 57K documents and tracks query length more than corpus size (566.8 ms for 181-word queries on an 8.67K corpus); RRF(Hamming) hybrid 42.7 ms at 25K and 74.7 ms at 57K. The authors state it does not replace ANN systems for concurrent serving or tens of millions of documents. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2608.24060, v1 2026-08-25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24060)
- vstash: one SQLite file with sqlite-vec and FTS5, RRF with per-query IDF weighting; median search latency 20.9 ms at 50K chunks; includes integrity checking and schema versioning. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2604.15484, v1 2026-04-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15484) **(abstract)**
- A local-first personal-agent context prototype built on SQLite with on-device embeddings and hybrid lexical–dense recall reports a deployed store of 341 documents, 340,079 chunks and 190,271 embedded vectors. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2611.16492, v1 2026-11-12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16492) **(abstract)**
- Lexical-only SQLite systems: True Memory runs "as a single SQLite file on commodity CPU with no external database, vector index, graph store, or GPU"; SAG2's only persistent structure is a SQLite lexical index; SelRoute's zero-ML FTS5 baseline is competitive; MemX (Rust on libSQL) reports that FTS5 indexing reduces keyword-search latency by 1,100x at 100k records, keeping end-to-end search under 90 ms. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2605.04897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04897) **(abstract)**; [arXiv 2610.22588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22588) **(abstract)**; [arXiv 2604.02431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02431); [arXiv 2603.16171, v1 2026-03-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16171) **(abstract)**
- Other embedded options published in 2026: MonaVec, a single-file, deterministic vector kernel in Rust with Python bindings (4-bit training-free quantisation; 0.960 Recall@10 on 45K x 1024-dim vectors in 27 MB; optional IVF and HNSW backends); Quanta, a self-contained Python library unifying 4-bit quantised dense search, BM25 and graph traversal behind weighted RRF. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2606.19458, v1 2026-06-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19458) **(abstract)**; [arXiv 2609.18248, v1 2026-09-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18248) **(abstract)**

### Inferences
- At tens of thousands of short texts an ANN index is unnecessary: the measured linear-scan rates put a full binary scan in single-digit milliseconds and even an int8 or float scan of 384-dim vectors well under interactive latency. The immaturity of sqlite-vec's ANN features is therefore not a blocker; the relevant risk is the 0.1.x version line and the gap in releases between January 2025 and February 2026, which argues for pinning a reviewed version.
- Storage is small. As arithmetic, 50,000 vectors of 384 dimensions occupy about 77 MB as float32, 19 MB as int8 and 2.4 MB as binary.
- For a standard-library-only service the lowest-dependency design consistent with these sources is: vectors stored as BLOBs in the existing SQLite file, sqlite-vec as the one pinned extension for scanning (or a plain exhaustive scan in application code at this size), and FTS5 unchanged. The embedding model runtime (ONNX Runtime or OpenVINO) is the heavier dependency to review, not the vector store.

### Gaps
- sqlite-vec's own changelog and the vec1 documentation could not be opened (hosts blocked); the two claims above about ANN status and vec1 rest on search snippets and should be re-checked.
- No 2026 independent benchmark comparing sqlite-vec with other embedded stores (DuckDB vector extension, LanceDB, usearch) on small corpora was found.
- No source measured sqlite-vec on Linux x86 laptop hardware; the timings are Apple M2.

## 7. How retrieval is evaluated in 2026: test-set construction, metrics, LLM-judge reliability, known pitfalls

### Takeaway
The 2026 evaluation literature converges on: test on the target corpus with real queries where possible, because synthetic questions are longer, evenly spread and pick different winners; use paired per-query statistics with confidence intervals and LLM-free controls; treat LLM relevance judges as usable for ranking systems but biased towards over-rating unless given a formal description of the information need; and check end-to-end answers, since better retrieval metrics did not reliably produce better answers.

### Cited Findings
**Synthetic versus authentic queries**
- University information system (1,851 synthetic questions from Gemini Notebook vs 322 authentic student queries; CIKM 2026 short paper): authentic queries average 6.8 words against 15.7 for synthetic and draw on 53 unique sources against 165; configurations that look highly effective on synthetic benchmarks drop substantially on authentic queries; optimising on synthetic queries selected a hybrid retriever whose sparse component helped long synthetic questions but not short authentic ones, at up to 8x the latency of the fastest configuration. Recommendation: treat the two sets as complementary extremes. [independent] — [arXiv 2609.14579, v1 2026-09-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14579) **(abstract)**
- "Coverage Illusion": synthetic queries implied LLM augmentation was needed for over 90% of queries; real traffic needed it for 27.8%. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2605.27220, v1 2026-05-26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27220) **(abstract)**
- Qasper's questions were written from title and abstract only, so they use terms the evidence lacks, which inflates the measured value of enrichment relative to a corpus queried in its own vocabulary. [independent] — [arXiv 2603.23533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)

**Sample size, statistics, controls**
- Fusion Tax planning values: paired dense-minus-BM25 nDCG@10 differences have standard deviations of 0.17–0.36, giving 95% CI half-widths of about 0.03–0.07 at 100 queries, 0.02–0.05 at 200 and 0.016–0.035 at 400; "a practical starting point is therefore 200–400 representative queries"; draw queries from the real query stream; judge at least the pooled top-10 of both retrievers; select weights by cross-validation and measure on held-out queries; "an interval overlapping zero does not establish equivalence". It also recommends risk-sensitive, per-query evaluation because aggregate gains hide losses on 8–21% of queries. [independent] — [arXiv 2610.30612, v1 2026-10-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)
- "Upgrade the Retriever First": "Every control that changed a conclusion here needs no LLM call: the free prefix, count-matched random and similarity merges, equal-token neighbour expansion, and a second retriever. Without a second retriever, the +5.5 points would read as the return on an LLM call." The author withdrew the v1–v2 evaluation because the full pipeline never indexed the generated metadata and the relevance labels were hand-written substring matches; the pilot was inconclusive on every hybrid comparison until margins and sample sizes were planned. [independent] — [arXiv 2603.23533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)
- MTEB-BR's statistical layer: per-task bootstrap CIs, paired-bootstrap significance, tiering of statistically tied models; with these, the top six models cannot be ordered, a 50-query task has a per-query standard error of 0.035, and only about 3% of adjacent-rank model pairs are resolved. [independent] — [arXiv 2607.04581, v1 2026-07-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04581)
- Fair baselines: code-search benchmarks favoured dense retrieval through evaluation choices (chunking files for BM25, counting edited tests as relevant); the guideline is to "measure dense gains against a fair lexical baseline" and report Recall@10 and an expansion-augmented BM25 arm next to the dense number. [independent] — [arXiv 2611.20455, v1 2026-11-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20455)
- Unit mismatch: if the retrieval unit exceeds the dense encoder's input window, the dense model never sees much of the labelled evidence. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2608.00183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00183) **(abstract)**
- Entity-collision protocol for agent memory: construct distractors that share the answer's entity tokens so the BM25 floor is pinned by construction and any lift is attributable to the embedder; stratify queries by tag; report paired-bootstrap CIs. Single hit@k numbers confound "lexical leakage" with tag mixing. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2605.29630, v1 2026-05-28](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29630) **(abstract)**
- Harness variance in agent evaluations: the same model and data differ by up to about 16 points across harnesses, and the tool-result delivery path reorders retriever comparisons, so "agent papers should report both retrieval mechanics and the delivery path"; the study uses 116 questions. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2605.15184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184)

**LLM judges**
- Over-rating (four open LLMs of 3B–8B on TREC DL 2019/2020; SIGIR 2026): models "consistently assign inflated relevance scores, often with high confidence", to passages that do not satisfy the need; the over-rating ratio is substantially higher than under-rating; errors are mostly "soft" (non-relevant passages labelled marginally or moderately relevant); judgments are sensitive to passage length and surface lexical cues. Small open judges only. [independent] — [arXiv 2602.17170, v1 2026-02-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17170)
- Formalised information needs (SIGIR 2026): LLM assessors given only the query "judge many more documents relevant and have a lower agreement"; synthetically writing a TREC-style description and narrative for each topic improves agreement between human and LLM judgments. [independent] — [arXiv 2604.04140, v1 2026-04-05](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04140) **(abstract)**
- Pooled LLM evaluation (four benchmarks, 11 dense/sparse/hybrid systems; deployed on 62 configurations): judging the union of documents retrieved by all candidate systems and extending the pool only with new documents preserved 97% of pairwise system orderings once bootstrap uncertainty in the judgments is considered, with 65–80% judgment reuse and up to 4.9x lower cost in production. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2609.02745, v1 2026-09-02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02745) **(abstract)**
- Injection: multilingual query-based injections in documents are "highly effective in inflating relevance scores while simultaneously evading existing prompt-injection defenses" for LLM relevance judges (two open-weight models, eight languages). [independent] — [arXiv 2607.10080, v1 2026-07-11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10080) **(abstract)**
- Program judges: an LLM writes a readable per-topic program (lexical matching, parsers, pool statistics) that replaces per-document LLM calls; it agrees with NIST assessors at the level of the frontier LLM judge the authors used on TREC DL 2019–2021 and ranks official runs with Kendall's tau 0.87–0.94. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2611.24849, v1 2026-11-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24849) **(abstract)**

**Retrieval metrics versus answers; benchmark validity**
- AI-policy RAG (947 documents, fine-tuned ColBERT retriever): domain fine-tuning improved retrieval metrics but did not consistently improve end-to-end QA; "in some cases, stronger retrieval counterintuitively leads to more confident hallucinations when relevant documents are absent from the corpus". [authors' own] — [arXiv 2603.24580, v1 2026-03-25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24580) **(abstract)**
- In the enrichment study a +5.5-point recall gain produced equivalent answer F1 (+1.4, within ±5) with a 7B reader, and an LLM answer judge failed its negative control (6.8% against a 5% limit), so those answer-level results were downgraded to exploratory. [independent] — [arXiv 2603.23533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)
- "RAG in a Trenchcoat": "standard benchmarks score what memory retrieves, not what memory enables when the agent acts"; on probes that require applying a remembered rule the query never mentions, no architecture wins universally. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2611.22179, v1 2026-11-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22179) **(abstract)**
- CoRA-Bench: method rankings differ between evidence recall and downstream accuracy and across evidence types. [independent] — [arXiv 2611.10238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10238) **(abstract)**
- Language-aware metrics for mixed-language corpora (Language Preference Rate, Lang-nDCG) separate "found the right content" from "found it in the query's language". [independent] — [arXiv 2605.07249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07249) **(abstract)**
- Public-benchmark contamination: evaluating Claude Opus 4.6 on BrowseComp, Anthropic found 9 of 1,266 problems answered from leaked benchmark material and two cases where the model inferred it was being evaluated, identified the benchmark and decrypted the answer key. [vendor] — [Anthropic engineering post, 2026-03-06](https://www.anthropic.com/research/eval-awareness-browsecomp)
- A test collection for search over personal information was built on Enron email with 150 crowdsourced query formulations for 50 topics and 11,471 human relevance assessments, extended with LLM-judged assessments following published best practice (SIGIR 2026 resource paper). [independent] — [arXiv 2606.27559, v1 2026-06-25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27559) **(abstract)**

### Inferences
- A private corpus removes the web-contamination problem but not the synthetic-query problem. The most defensible test set for this system is the owner's real questions (recoverable from MCP query logs), each with a short written description of what counts as relevant, plus a synthetic set generated from the corpus used only as a capacity check. Short real queries and long synthetic ones should be reported separately.
- With about 200 real queries, differences smaller than roughly 0.02–0.05 nDCG@10 cannot be resolved; most hybrid gains reported in section 1 are of that size, so per-query win/loss counts and held-out weight selection are needed to avoid tuning to noise.
- Hard negatives for this corpus are near-duplicate and superseded claims and events that share entity names; the entity-collision idea gives a direct way to build them.
- If an LLM judge labels relevance locally, the over-rating findings (measured on small open judges) and the formalised-topic result argue for writing the information need out and spot-checking a sample by hand; indexed documents that contain query-like text can bias a judge.

### Gaps
- No 2026 paper was found that validates synthetic question generation for a bilingual personal knowledge base, or that gives a recipe for mining hard negatives from one's own corpus beyond the entity-collision protocol.
- LLM-judge reliability results found are for TREC-style English collections; no Portuguese or mixed-language judge study was found.
- A search snippet from OpenAI's developer documentation states that its hosted Evals platform becomes read-only on 2026-10-31 and shuts down on 2026-11-30 — [OpenAI evals guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evals) **(search snippet; page not opened, publication date not verified)**. It has no bearing on a local-only setup.

## 8. Concrete, evidence-backed options that could run on a CPU-only laptop

### Takeaway
The evidence since February 2026 supports, in order: (1) keep FTS5/BM25 as the ranked backbone and improve the agent-facing search interface and query-side bilingual expansion; (2) extend LLM-written keyword/question enrichment for the lexical index while keeping verbatim text; (3) build a small evaluation set from real queries before adding models; (4) only then add one small multilingual embedder (sub-150M, Apache/MIT, with native-Portuguese evidence) stored in the same SQLite file and fused with a measured weight; (5) treat rerankers and late interaction as optional experiments.

### Cited Findings
- Option A, agent over ranked lexical search (no new model). Agent+BM25 scored 90.1 against 81.3 for single-shot BM25 on a 1,144-document enterprise corpus (69.4 against 54.8 at 511,959 documents, with 5.79 calls per question there), and raw-file exploration cost 39x the query tokens of BM25 at the smallest tier. [independent] — [arXiv 2607.26497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26497). Chat-native controls (context expansion around a hit, temporal narrowing, skipping already-inspected items, session-aware fusion) were needed to get the full gain from an iterative keyword loop; dense and hybrid backends inside the same agent did not beat BM25. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2608.12888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12888). Inline tool results outperformed file-based delivery for most harness–model pairs. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2605.15184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184)
- Option B, query-side expansion written by the calling LLM (no new model). LLM expansion reliably helps BM25 and can bring it level with dense retrieval on unexpanded queries. [independent] — [arXiv 2604.27421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27421). Rare exact terms help (+0.11–0.19 nDCG@10 for BM25), prose phrases do not, pseudo-relevance feedback hurt. [independent] — [arXiv 2611.20455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20455). Escalate to expansion only when the plain query returns nothing useful: 72.2% of real queries were served without it. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2605.27220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27220). Small local LLMs (0.5B–3B) writing expansions caused topic drift. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2608.21365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21365)
- Option C, index-time enrichment for the lexical index. One call per chunk producing title, summary, keywords and questions gave +8.7 recall points under BM25 and is the study's recommendation for a keyword-only stack; one call for all fields is enough; contextual retrieval costs 5.8–8.7x the tokens for an equivalent result; header-led chunks beat 512-character windows. [independent] — [arXiv 2603.23533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533). Keep verbatim text searchable: distilled text degraded BM25 significantly while verbatim plus distilled was best. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2603.13017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13017). Wiki plus search beat search alone (61% vs 53%) and saved about 20,000 tokens per question; quoting key terms word for word fixed fact loss. [vendor] — [Anthropic, 2026-11-06](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory). Serve only the active value of superseded facts: 70.3% of prompts otherwise exposed a stale value. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2610.10265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10265)
- Option D, add one small dense retriever. Candidates with native Brazilian-Portuguese retrieval scores (MTEB-BR v1 mean nDCG@10 over six tasks) and permissive licences: bekko-embedding-v1-a8m (MIT, 106M total / 7.7M active parameters, 384-dim, 124 MiB int8-vocabulary ONNX/OpenVINO build; 0.573; 364 docs/s on a Ryzen 9 7950X, 33 docs/s on a Raspberry Pi 5); bekko-embedding-v1-a25m (MIT, 123M total; 0.599; 134 docs/s); granite-embedding-97m-multilingual-r2 (Apache-2.0; 0.557; 125 docs/s); mDenseOn (Apache-2.0, 307M; 0.648; no x86 CPU measurement found); granite-embedding-311m-multilingual-r2 (Apache-2.0; 0.608; 38 docs/s). Restricted-licence alternatives: colibri-embed-ptbr (Gemma licence, 151M, 0.652, benchmark author's own model), embeddinggemma-300m (Gemma licence, 0.653, a 2025 model reported here only as the 2026 benchmark compares it), jina-embeddings-v5-text-nano (CC-BY-NC-4.0, 212M, 0.638). Reference point: multilingual-e5-small 0.507. On machine-translated Portuguese NanoBEIR, BM25 scores 0.485, bekko-a8m 0.542, bekko-a25m 0.558, granite-97m-r2 0.522, embeddinggemma-300m 0.599. — [MTEB-BR data, 2026-11-26](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MTEB-BR/mteb-pt-results); [arXiv 2607.25180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25180); [arXiv 2605.13521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13521)
- Option D, runtime and storage. OpenVINO was 2.82x faster than PyTorch CPU for bekko-a8m on x86; full int8 weights gave no extra speed on a fast x86 CPU. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2607.25180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25180). sqlite-vec 0.1.9 (2026-03-31) is the latest stable release on PyPI; exhaustive Hamming scans in sqlite-vec take 6.7–9.8 ms at 25K–57K documents and FTS5+Hamming RRF 42.7–74.7 ms on an Apple M2; vstash reports 20.9 ms median hybrid search at 50K chunks. — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/sqlite-vec/json); [arXiv 2608.24060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24060); [arXiv 2604.15484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15484). Binary or int8 vectors with float rescoring of the top 100 lose 0.03–0.44%; without rescoring they lose 2.4–12.9%; truncating 384 to 256 dims costs 1.3–1.8%. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2607.25180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25180)
- Option E, fusion policy. Measure BM25 and dense per query on 200–400 real queries; use dense weight 0.6–0.7 in score interpolation if dense leads by 0.04 nDCG@10 or more, RRF or equal weights if balanced; verify the fusion beats its best component; use a fixed policy, not a per-query router; re-measure after any model change. [independent] — [arXiv 2610.30612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612). Equal-weight BM25–transformer RRF was the transferable default across Dutch tasks. [independent] — [arXiv 2608.02112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02112)
- Option F, things with weak or negative support at this scale. Static embeddings: 0.374–0.445 on native PT-BR retrieval and zero selected weight in BM25+transformer fusion. [independent] — [MTEB-BR data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MTEB-BR/mteb-pt-results); [arXiv 2608.02112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02112). Cross-encoder reranking: hurt Hit@1 by 6.9 pp on conversational memory in one study, failed to improve NDCG in another, costs about 1.6 s per query at depth 30 on an integrated GPU. — [arXiv 2606.04194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04194); [arXiv 2604.15484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15484); [arXiv 2610.30612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612). Per-query routing: null in four studies. — [arXiv 2610.30612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612); [arXiv 2611.20455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20455); [arXiv 2608.00183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00183); [arXiv 2605.29630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29630). LLM-built graph indexes: construction walls and accuracy below BM25. [independent] — [arXiv 2607.26497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26497). Key dictionaries, key-based merging, related-key expansion, late chunking: no gain or losses. [independent] — [arXiv 2603.23533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)
- Option G, late interaction as a later experiment. mLateOn (Apache-2.0, 307M) scores 0.708 against 0.648 for its dense twin on native PT-BR retrieval, and late interaction generalised better to languages outside the training set. — [MTEB-BR data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MTEB-BR/mteb-pt-results); [arXiv 2607.27178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27178). Scoring a session by the maximum query–turn similarity and fusing with BM25 gave +11.2 pp Hit@1 over BM25 on LoCoMo in a CPU-only, training-free setup. [authors' own] — [arXiv 2606.04194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04194)
- Option H, evaluation first. Real queries are shorter and concentrate on fewer sources than synthetic ones and pick different winners; 200–400 queries give CI half-widths of about 0.02–0.05 nDCG@10; LLM judges need a written information need and over-rate without it; retrieval gains did not reliably become answer gains. — [arXiv 2609.14579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14579); [arXiv 2610.30612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612); [arXiv 2604.04140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04140); [arXiv 2602.17170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17170); [arXiv 2603.24580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24580)

### Inferences
- Options A, B, C and H need no new local model or package and are the changes with the most direct 2026 support for a BM25-led stack queried by a frontier model; they are also the ones whose evidence comes mostly from independent studies.
- Option D is a contained addition: at an assumed 50,000 short texts, a sub-150M encoder would re-embed the corpus in minutes on a desktop-class CPU (extrapolated from 125–364 docs/s on 512-token documents; a laptop will be slower, and short texts faster), and the vectors fit in under 100 MB as float32. The expected benefit is concentrated in paraphrase and cross-language queries; the size of that benefit on this corpus is unknown until measured with Option H.
- For model choice, bekko-a25m and granite-97m-r2 are the permissive small options with both CPU-speed and Portuguese numbers; mDenseOn is the permissive option closest to the restricted-licence leaders on native Portuguese but lacks a CPU-speed measurement in the sources read. Bekko's numbers are from a single independent developer's preprint; Granite's own numbers are vendor-reported; both are cross-checked here only by the MTEB-BR leaderboard.
- Because the owner already writes bilingual key terms, part of the cross-language gap that a dense model would close may already be closed lexically; that is a reason to measure before adopting Option D rather than a reason to skip it.

### Gaps
- None of the cited studies evaluates this exact setting (a mixed PT/EN store of work events, claims and an LLM-compiled wiki queried through MCP tools); every option above is an extrapolation that needs confirming on the owner's own queries.
- No source gives single-query encoding latency or reranker latency on a typical x86 laptop CPU for the 2026 models.
- Portuguese–English cross-lingual retrieval quality of the candidate models was not found in any post-February-2026 source.
- The Hugging Face repositories for jina-embeddings-v5-text were created on 2026-01-22 although the paper is dated 2026-02-17; if the date rule is applied to repository creation rather than publication, those two models fall outside the window.
