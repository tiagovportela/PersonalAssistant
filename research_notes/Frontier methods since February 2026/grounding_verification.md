## 1. Citation/attribution verification since Feb 2026: methods, measured precision/recall, cost, failure cases, small local (CPU, multilingual) entailment checkers

### Takeaway
2026 audits consistently show that "the cited thing exists" says little about "the cited thing supports the sentence": link/pointer validity is above 94% while factual support is 39–77% in deep-research reports, and models accept citations on topical overlap. The working pattern is claim-level (atomic or normalized) checking against the cited span with a verifier separate from the writer, validated on a small human-labelled gold set; small local verifiers (3B attribution model, 184M–435M DeBERTa NLI, MiniCheck-class) separate supported from unsupported about as well as frontier judges on short claims, but no single automatic scorer transfers across datasets, and measured unsupported rates swing from 3% to 18% depending only on the verifier.

### Cited Findings

**How bad is existence-only checking**
- Across 14 LLMs writing cited deep-research reports (130 queries), link validity is above 94% for 12 of 14 models and topical relevance above 80% for frontier models, but "Fact Check" (does the source support the specific attributed claim) ranges from 24% (OSS-120B) to 77% (Claude Opus 4.5); OpenAI models 39–59%, Gemini 45–49%, Claude Haiku 4.5 69%. Models that emit more citations score lower. Fact Check accuracy drops about 42% on average for two frontier models as tool calls scale from 2 to 150. Judges are rubric LLMs "calibrated through human review" — [Cited but Not Verified, arXiv:2605.06635, v1 2026-05-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06635) `[independent audit]` (full text)
- Legal citation check with controlled corruptions, 14 model configurations: models catch 93–100% of wrong-case citations but only 37–61% of wrong-pinpoint (same case, wrong page) corruptions on court opinions and 52–83% on briefs; GPT-5.4 at high reasoning still misses 40% and 18%. When they fail, "they accept the citation based on topical overlap rather than page-level support". Prompting to verify support at the cited page raises recall but also the false positive rate — [Is this Citation on Point?, arXiv:2608.12571, v1 2026-08-12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12571) `[independent audit]` (abstract)
- PARCEL (3,396 legal parenthetical claims labelled Supported / Refuted / Not Found, cast as 3-way NLI): strongest LLMs reach 0.97 accuracy zero-shot, yet still mark unsupported claims as supported even with the full opinion text; "missing support is harder to detect than direct contradiction" — [When Citations Mislead?, arXiv:2610.10971, v1 2026-10-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10971) `[authors' own benchmark]` (abstract)
- Post-rationalized citations (answer first, then attach whatever passage looks close): on Wikipedia-based questions roughly one citation in seven is unfaithful; RL agents trained for answer correctness post-rationalize at their base model's rate — [Attributable Post-Rationalization in RAG Citations, arXiv:2609.23053, v1 2026-09-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23053) `[authors' own]`, free-tier GPUs, small open models (full text)
- Citing a summary instead of the source hides unsupported content: a RECOMP-style compressor's citations score 0.86 precision against its summaries but 0.12 against source spans; claim verification after source recovery gives an unsupported rate of 0.88 versus 0.17 when checking against the summaries. Authors caution the estimates share one NLI model and lack human calibration — [The Attribution-Compression Frontier in RAG, arXiv:2609.14245, v1 2026-09-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14245) `[authors' own]`, single author (abstract)
- A mechanistic study (Llama-3.1-8B) finds citation attachment is driven by a distributed set of heads/MLPs separable from answer generation and concludes that "inline citations can create a false sense of security" — [How Do LLMs Cite?, arXiv:2606.28358, v1 2026-06-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28358) `[authors' own]` (abstract)

**The verifier itself is an unreliable instrument unless anchored on human gold**
- On identical agent outputs the measured unsupported-citation rate ranges from about 3% to about 18% across five gold-validated verifiers (AttrScore-3B, an OpenScholar post-hoc judge, GPT-4o, RAGAS, a DeBERTa-NLI judge); verifiers agree on what is supported but not on what to flag (negative-specific agreement 0.27–0.30). On SciFact gold (n=208) separation is comparable: ROC-AUC AttrScore-3B 0.88 [0.84, 0.93], DeBERTa-NLI 0.90 [0.85, 0.94], GPT-4o 0.87 [0.82, 0.92], "so a strict frontier judge such as GPT-4o buys no separation advantage over the local 3B model". Adopted local verifier: supported-class recall 0.942 on a held-out split (n=802); on the dev set its Table 2 row reads 0.898 / 0.535 / 0.463, which I read as recall, specificity and Cohen's kappa (kappa CI [0.332, 0.589]). The proposed guard is verify → re-attribute (BM25 matches the best open generator at finding the true supporting passage) → flag, with a split-conformal threshold calibrated on human-gold negatives that guarantees catching at least 1−α of truly unsupported citations, assuming calibration and deployment data are exchangeable; the authors say the threshold must be recalibrated on target-domain negatives. Kit stated to run "on one 80GB GPU or on CPU" — [Evaluating and Guarding Citation Faithfulness in Agentic Scientific Synthesis, arXiv:2607.20527, v1 2026-07-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20527) `[authors' own]` (full text)
- 8 rubric LLM judges scored on 624 claim–citation pairs (1,248 decisions, all human-reviewed, 378 hard cases adjudicated; about 60% of claims adversarially edited): GPT-5-mini has the best source-relevance pass-class F1 0.908 (κ=0.636); Claude Opus 4.6 leads factual support at F1 0.750 (κ=0.701) but judges are statistically indistinguishable on factual support. All 8 judges are stricter than gold on source relevance (pass rates 42.9–72.0% vs gold 79.3%), while three cheap models (GPT-5.4-mini, Claude Haiku 4.5, Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite) are more lenient than gold on factual support (gold pass rate 18.4%). "Cost does not track quality"; at similar F1 judges differ substantially in false-positive rate, false-negative rate and pass-rate drift — [Do You Need a Frontier Model as a Citation Verifier?, arXiv:2607.08700, v1 2026-07-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08700) `[authors' own benchmark of third-party judges]` (full text)
- Audit of eight automatic scorers against human labels: no scorer transfers. An off-the-shelf NLI scorer that is best on short-claim AttributedQA (AUROC 0.90) collapses to 0.53 on long-form LFQA (about 320-word evidence), where BERTScore wins (0.91). On HAGRID (n=2,150, short cited quotes) clean MNLI 0.800, BERTScore 0.793, MiniCheck 0.791, lexical 0.752. On VitaminC (n=4,000) FEVER-NLI 0.894, clean NLI 0.811, MiniCheck 0.810, surface metrics about 0.60–0.63. Choosing the "best on average" scorer fails leave-one-dataset-out (mean regret 0.172 AUROC). A prompt-based LLM judge avoids the chance-level collapses but is "~100x costlier, and non-deterministic" — [Do LLM Attribution Metrics Transfer?, arXiv:2606.23915, v1 2026-06-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23915) `[independent audit]` (full text)
- Label noise matters: a two-annotator audit confirmed 1,909 of 2,468 harvested "natural" citation errors as genuine, and "noise in automatic labels biases measured verifier accuracy and can reverse system rankings" — [AtomCite, arXiv:2609.05802, v1 2026-09-05](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05802) `[authors' own]` (abstract)

**Methods: span/quote citations, cite-then-verify, decomposition, normalization, escalation**
- FullCite (structured inline citations with document + evidence span; prompt-based, constrained decoding over a citation grammar, and post-hoc span alignment; Qwen3-8B and Gemma-3-12B): models find the right document (the generate-then-retrieve baseline reaches Doc-F1 80% and 94% on ASQA and ExpertQA) but not the span; post-hoc alignment raises snippet-F1 on ASQA from 12.80 to 61.87 (Qwen3-8B) and 12.42 to 41.80 (Gemma-12B); even with constrained decoding most responses cite only a few of the relevant documents, with a primacy bias in which document is cited — [Explicit Evidence Grounding via Structured Inline Citation Generation, arXiv:2606.07130, v1 2026-06-05](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07130) `[authors' own]` (full text)
- AtomCite (parse answer into claims, check each against the cited page, deterministic repair policy): about 93% binary verification accuracy on injected errors across Gemini, Claude and GPT; repair lifts citation precision from a constructed 34% to 87–90% while keeping over 90% of correct claims; with frozen prompts it lifts two open 7–8B models above the same models used as direct judges on five public hallucination benchmarks — [AtomCite, arXiv:2609.05802, v1 2026-09-05](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05802) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Claim normalization before verification (rewrite the citing sentence to fix scope mismatch, perspective mismatch and entangled propositions): +12% macro-F1 on average for encoder classifiers and +10% for generative LLMs across 18 classifiers; evidence quality is the dominant factor — [Understanding before verifying, arXiv:2608.30145, v1 2026-08-31](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30145) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Naive decomposition can hurt: fact-checking LLMs "routinely output Supports labels whose cited evidence does not license the claim"; rigid decomposition costs up to 27.6 accuracy points on some cells because facets lose full-claim context; re-scoring spans against the full claim recovers it. An automatic NLI check that the cited warrant entails the claim calibrates against human gold evidence at AUC 0.92, precision 0.98 — [The Warrant Gap, arXiv:2606.24627, v1 2026-06-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24627) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Selective escalation: verify against the abstract first, fetch full-text passages only for uncertain cases; 86.7 micro-F1 on SCitance (+4.5 over abstract-only) while resolving 67% of instances without full text — [DeepSciVerify, arXiv:2605.27710, v1 2026-05-26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27710) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Type-routed atomic verification (financial QA): uniform detectors miss 43% of computational errors that need arithmetic re-verification; a verify-then-ground pipeline cuts hallucination 68% versus the strongest baseline under identical retrieval; an 8B distilled detector keeps 91.4% F1 at 18x lower per-claim latency ($0.003 per query) — [FinGround, arXiv:2604.23588, v1 2026-04-26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23588) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Extractive "verbatim" answering (map queries to verbatim spans, no free generation): a 150M-parameter ModernBERT token classifier reaches word-level F1 53.6, ahead of the strongest LLM extractor (48.7) on an NLP-researcher-annotated set — [ACL-Verbatim, arXiv:2605.21102, v1 2026-05-20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21102) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- How much of the source the writer sees bounds faithfulness on a 4B on-device model: raising per-source exposure from 400 to 1,500 characters lifts cited-claim faithfulness from 0.45 to 0.58 (retrieved sources) and 0.37 to 0.58 (gold sources) for about 235 extra output tokens — [On-Device Deep Research at 4B, arXiv:2607.12257, v1 2026-07-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12257) `[authors' own]`, single author (abstract)

**Lab feature: Anthropic Citations API (living documentation, fetched 2026-11-27; the feature itself launched before the date window, so only the page's current text is reported)**
- Plain text and PDFs are chunked into sentences; "custom content" documents let the caller supply the blocks, which become the smallest citable unit ("for example, for bullet points or transcripts"). Citation objects carry `cited_text` plus `char_location`, `page_location` or `content_block_location`. `cited_text` does not count toward output tokens. Stated guarantee: "citations are guaranteed to contain valid pointers to the provided documents". Stated quality claim, no number given: "In Anthropic's evaluations, the citations feature is significantly more likely to cite the most relevant quotes from documents than purely prompt-based approaches." Limits: citations "cannot be used together with structured outputs" (400 error), and in the docs' extraction recipe "Check that each field has a value and at least one citation. The API doesn't guarantee either." Works with prompt caching and batch processing — [Citations, Claude Platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations) `[vendor-reported]`, living page

**Small, local verifiers (CPU-feasible or sub-1GB) and what they can and cannot do**
- CPU-only benchmark on HaluEval (2,000 test items per task, laptop CPU): an NLI detector (`MoritzLaurer/DeBERTa-v3-base-mnli-fever-anli`, about 184M parameters) ensembled with MiniLM similarity reaches F1 0.792 / AUC-ROC 0.873 on QA and F1 0.694 / AUC 0.749 on dialogue; on summarisation every single-pass method is near chance (AUC 0.469–0.574). Raising the premise budget from 800 to 1,600 characters lifts AUC from 0.567 to 0.629, and sentence-level chunk aggregation reaches 0.683 at roughly twenty times the NLI inference — [How Far Can You Get Without a GPU?, arXiv:2606.29809, v1 2026-06-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29809) `[independent audit of public models]` (full text)
- HallDetect: one generative call (4-bit 8B extractor) to split the response into atomic claims, then DeBERTa-v3-Large NLI (435M parameters, under 1 GB VRAM) scores each claim against a multi-scale library of source chunks, picking best-entailing and best-contradicting chunk independently; "a single confidently contradicted claim flags the response". Beats comparably resourced generative and embedding baselines on three of four benchmarks; the authors do not claim to beat full-precision or API detectors — [Decomposed Entailment for Factuality Checking and Hallucination Detection, arXiv:2608.05823, v1 2026-08-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05823) `[authors' own]` (full text)
- Span-level detection over code, tool output and structured documents (what a work-activity log actually contains): fine-tuned Qwen3.5-2B detector reaches 0.689 span-F1 overall and 0.60 on the code-agent split, where LettuceDetect-large scores 0.17 and the best zero-shot LLM judge at most 0.22; 81.8 example-F1 on RAGTruth. A 307M-parameter mmBERT-base encoder variant is released as a baseline — [Beyond Document Grounding, arXiv:2607.00895, v1 2026-07-01](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00895) `[authors' own]` (full text)
- Training-free sentence-level detector (re-score each answer sentence with and without the source using a 1.5B scorer): AUC 0.717–0.745 on RAGTruth (0.773 with a classifier), above entailment and attribution baselines, at about 1/47 of an LLM judge's compute; a full-context fact-checker and the LLM judge remain more accurate — [Sentence-Level Context Sensitivity, arXiv:2607.04223, v1 2026-07-05](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04223) `[authors' own]`, single author (abstract)
- Grounding Probe: logistic regression on hidden states of an "observer" model that reads context + response reaches 0.879–0.894 AUROC on RAGTruth, 0.924 when averaged with a supervised span detector; asking the same observer model outright costs at least 0.166 AUROC — [Grounding Probes, arXiv:2610.04642, v1 2026-10-03](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04642) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Numbers and units are a blind spot: a fine-tuned ModernBERT fact-checker with macro-F1 0.899 on typed-quantity errors drops to 36.5% accuracy on equivalent rewrites (95°C vs 368.15 K); training-time symbolic augmentation lifts that to 98.2% — [Symbolic Augmentation Closes a Canonical-Equivalence Blind Spot, arXiv:2607.16212, v1 2026-05-19 per arXiv API](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16212) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- A 33M-parameter NLI model is reported as a workable replacement for regex in extracting sub-check signals from a judge's reasoning trace — [VERDI, arXiv:2605.11334, v1 2026-05-11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11334) `[authors' own]` (abstract)

- LLM fact-checkers judge English better than every other language on every model tested (8 models, 3B–70B, 1,500 claims in 8 parallel languages); the gap is widest on the smallest models — [Fair Fact-Checking, arXiv:2609.34678, v1 2026-09-28](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34678) `[independent audit + own method]` (abstract)

### Inferences
- The system's current check ("cited item exists") corresponds to the "Link Works" dimension, which the 2026 audits show is the dimension that least predicts support. A support check per (wiki sentence, cited claim/event/file span) is the missing layer.
- "Label = best of its citations" is the pattern the 2026 results warn against twice: a sentence can cite a real, on-topic item that does not support it (pinpoint results), and several citations derived from the same underlying event are not independent support (see the duplication result in section 6). A label should follow the weakest link that the sentence actually depends on, after a support check.
- For wiki statements that cite *claims* (themselves LLM-written summaries of events), the attribution-compression result suggests verifying against the claim's underlying evidence span as well, not only against the claim text.
- A cheap cascade is supported by several results: deterministic checks first (pointer validity, quoted span present verbatim, dates/numbers normalized and compared), a small local NLI or attribution model on short atomic claims against short evidence spans, and an LLM judge only for the uncertain band. Short claims against short quoted spans is exactly the regime where small NLI models did well (AUROC 0.80–0.90); long evidence is where they collapsed.
- The Citations API's custom-content mode (one block per event/claim/file hunk) gives span pointers that are valid by construction, but the docs state that it cannot be combined with structured outputs, so it fits the wiki-compile run (prose with citations) rather than a JSON claim-extraction run. Valid pointers are not a support guarantee; the docs make no such claim.
- Because verifier choice moves the measured unsupported rate by a factor of six, any before/after measurement in the improvement plan needs a named verifier, a fixed prompt, and a small human-labelled gold set from the owner's own data.

### Gaps
- No 2026 source measured entailment/groundedness checking specifically for Portuguese or for mixed Portuguese–English evidence; the only Portuguese item found is an encoder paper (NorBERTo) with ASSIN 2 / PLUE results, not a groundedness checker. Whether MultiWikiQHalluA's 30 languages include Portuguese was not confirmed.
- No 2026 source reported CPU latency per claim for small verifiers; the one CPU study gives accuracy and relative cost ("twenty times the NLI inference"), not seconds.
- No independent 2026 measurement of the Anthropic Citations API's citation precision/recall was found; the docs give a qualitative vendor statement without numbers.
- No 2026 Google or OpenAI lab publication describing a new citation-verification method was found (Google results were pre-window; OpenAI's system-card host is blocked here).
- Constrained decoding to quoted spans: only FullCite was found, and its reported gains come mainly from post-hoc alignment; I found no 2026 head-to-head of "quote-first then write" against post-hoc verification with human labels.

## 2. Rates of unsupported or partially supported statements in LLM summaries and agent-written reports (2026), and what reduces them

### Takeaway
In agent-written cited reports, 2026 studies put the share of citations that do not support their claim between roughly 15% (one in seven, RAG QA) and 23–61% (frontier deep-research agents), rising with search depth and multi-agent hand-offs; plain conversation summaries by strong models are much cleaner (about 4–5% of atomic facts unsupported). The interventions with measured effect are structural: write from source-linked claims, check each atomic claim with a separate verifier and repair or flag, show the writer more of each source, and keep the orchestration step from rewriting facts; holistic rubric scores and self-consistency checks often do not move even when claim-level error rates change severalfold.

### Cited Findings
- Deep-research reports: 23–61% of citations fail the factual-support check among frontier models (Fact Check 39–77%); accuracy falls about 42% as tool calls go from 2 to 150 — [arXiv:2605.06635, v1 2026-05-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06635) `[independent audit]` (full text)
- Claim-level audit of deep-research writers (judge GPT-5.4-mini, 100 tasks): replacing only the report writer with a hierarchical claim-based writer (extract source facts → map to outline → draft each section from source-linked claims) cuts hallucinated claims from 15.89 to 5.02 and misattributed claims from 18.94 to 5.43 while necessary-fact recall rises from 36.83 to 45.85 on one host framework; across three frameworks hallucination falls 2.6–4.5x and recall improves 1.2–1.7x, while holistic rubric scores stay roughly stable. Judge agreement with unanimous human labels: κ=0.484 (hallucination), 0.797 (misattribution), 0.743 (relevance), n=100. The same writer also propagates changed source facts into revised reports at the highest rate among update methods — [Redesigning and Auditing Deep Research Writing for Faithful Reports, arXiv:2608.28643, v1 2026-08-12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28643) `[authors' own]` (full text)
- Error localization in three open deep-research systems: global citation recall is 58.7% (Nvidia AI-Q), 28.5% (MS-Agent), 7.1% (TrajectoryKit); 84.7% of AI-Q's final-report errors originate at the orchestrator (about 31% hallucinations, the rest citation mistakes); "almost every agent makes a lot of mistakes with the exception being those that summarize a single document"; two targeted interventions raise citation recall by 5% and precision by 3–7% — [Who is the Agent to Blame?, arXiv:2608.24306, v1 2026-08-25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24306) `[independent audit]` (full text, partial)
- RAG QA: roughly one citation in seven is post-rationalized on Wikipedia-based questions — [arXiv:2609.23053, v1 2026-09-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23053) `[authors' own]` (full text)
- Agentic scientific synthesis (OpenScholar, PaperQA2-style): 3% to 18% unsupported citations on the same outputs depending on the verifier — [arXiv:2607.20527, v1 2026-07-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20527) `[authors' own]` (full text)
- Conversation summarization (1,800 conversations, six scenarios, 28 LLMs; summaries are decomposed into atomic facts, a three-model majority vote filters decomposition artefacts, and faithfulness is the share of atomic facts an LLM verifier finds supported by the conversation): faithfulness of strong models is in the mid-90s (e.g., DeepSeek-R1 95.7% vs DeepSeek-V3 96.2%, p<0.01); reasoning models gain clearly in completeness (71.7% vs 60.1%) and conciseness but "yield no gain, or even regress, in faithfulness" — [OmniCSEval, arXiv:2606.15974, v1 2026-06-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15974) `[independent audit]` (full text, partial)
- AI meeting summaries (114 meetings, 340 meeting–model pairs, claim-grounded scoring): accuracy differences between gpt-4.1-mini, gpt-5-mini and gpt-5.1 are not significant (highest mean accuracy 0.583 for gpt-4.1-mini); the newer model separates only on retention (completeness 0.886, coverage 0.942) — [Evaluating AI Meeting Summaries with a Reusable Cross-Domain Pipeline, arXiv:2604.21345, v1 2026-04-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21345) `[authors' own pipeline, third-party models]` (abstract)
- Small models, multilingual QA: Qwen3-0.6B has up to 60% of answers with at least one hallucination (peaking in Icelandic); larger models are generally lower — [MultiWikiQHalluA, arXiv:2605.02504, v1 2026-05-04](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02504) `[authors' own classifiers]` (abstract)
- One planted misleading document raises the rate of deep-research reports that endorse a false conclusion from 0% to 54.7% on average; a cross-model verifier classifies the same documents as misleading, yet agents still adopt them; pre- and post-research defenses reduce but do not eliminate adoption — [Is Deep Research Reliable?, arXiv:2607.20891, v1 2026-07-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20891) `[independent audit]` (abstract)
- Repeating one identical deep-research prompt ten times gives pairwise citation Jaccard below 0.02 and median entity Jaccard 0.12 — [Self-Consistency of Entity Sets and Citations in a Deep Research System, arXiv:2610.26421, v1 2026-10-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26421) `[independent audit]`, one prompt, one system (abstract)
- What a separate verifier buys: AtomCite repair raises citation precision 34% → 87–90% keeping over 90% of correct claims ([arXiv:2609.05802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05802), v1 2026-09-05, `[authors' own]`, abstract); FinGround cuts hallucination 68% versus the strongest baseline at equal retrieval ([arXiv:2604.23588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23588), v1 2026-04-26, `[authors' own]`, abstract)
- Cross-model beats same-model checks: pooling another model family's samples raises semantic-entropy AUROC by 1.7–4.0 points at every budget and "a same-model temperature check is the weakest design"; a strict three-call agree-gate automates 70% of items and still acts on 16% of errors — [Anchor and Check, arXiv:2611.25818, v1 2026-11-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25818) `[authors' own]`, single author (abstract)
- A verifier that ranks answers well may change nothing: a perturbation-agreement verifier reached AUROC 0.990 while every episode outcome matched single-sample decoding at 4.32 times the call count — [Consistency Is Not Correction, arXiv:2611.21651, v1 2026-10-15 per arXiv API](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21651) `[authors' own]`, single author (abstract)
- Deterministic computation plus human gates: an unconstrained multi-agent research baseline produced critical failures in 72% of 280 runs; the same model with "LLMs reason but do not execute data work", deterministic estimation and three human decision gates dropped to 16% (p<0.001); an 80-run ablation suggests the two contribute independently — [(Human) Attention Is (Still) All You Need, arXiv:2606.12848, v1 2026-06-11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12848) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Human "faithful" labels are themselves lenient: global human labels on four faithfulness benchmarks track the *average* of per-sentence judgments better than the strict every-sentence rule; manual review confirmed real local errors in 4 of 10 (TofuEval) and 5 of 10 (MSumBench) reviewed summaries that humans had labelled faithful — [Averaging Bias, arXiv:2608.00205, v1 2026-07-31](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00205) `[independent audit]` (full text, partial)
- OpenAI system cards (vendor-reported, **search snippet only**, host blocked): the GPT-5.5 card (2026-04-23) is summarized as reporting individual claims about 23% more likely to be factually correct than GPT-5.4 while making more claims per response; the October 2026 GPT-6 update reports factuality gains on nearly all metrics except claim-level errors on factuality-heavy prompts — [OpenAI Deployment Safety Hub](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-october/hallucinations) (not opened)

### Inferences
- The end-of-day and wiki runs resemble the "orchestrator" stage in the blame study (rewriting already-summarized material), which is where most errors entered; the stage that summarized a single document was the reliable one. Keeping the wiki writer on source-linked claim records and limiting free rewriting matches the ClaimWriter result.
- The exposure result (0.45 → 0.58) and the summarisation truncation result both say the same thing from two sides: both the writer and the verifier need the actual evidence text, not a 400–800 character stub.
- Multi-sample self-agreement by the same model is the weakest of the checks tested in 2026; if a second opinion is affordable, a different model family or a different route to the answer is the better use of the call.
- "Faithful" as judged by a person skimming a page is closer to "mostly faithful"; per-sentence review units are needed if the owner's confirmation is to mean every sentence is supported.

### Gaps
- No 2026 study measured unsupported-statement rates for an end-of-day work-activity digest (commits + chat + mail + calendar) or for an LLM-compiled personal wiki; the nearest are deep-research reports, meeting summaries and conversation summaries.
- No 2026 controlled comparison of prompting-only mitigations (e.g., "quote before you claim") against verifier-based ones with human labels was found.
- OpenAI system-card numbers could not be verified (host blocked); an Anthropic February 2026 risk report mentioning hallucinated citations as a red-team finding was seen only as a search snippet and not opened.

## 3. Extracting decisions, commitments, action items and deadlines from conversations, meetings and work logs (2026 results)

### Takeaway
Direct 2026 evidence is thin: I found no 2026 benchmark that reports owner or due-date error rates for commitment extraction. The closest results say the dominant false positives are stale and misowned items (60% already-answered requests, 28% someone else's request), that hedged or hearsay statements get rewritten as facts at write time, that speaker attribution and knowledge updates are the weakest categories in multi-party memory (27–47% accuracy), and that a system's own stored labels are not ground truth (28% accuracy against human labels in one personal inbox).

### Cited Findings
- Proactive task discovery in a simulated workplace (mail, chat, calendar, files; 17 backbone LLMs, 120 tasks, 51 runs; ground truth derived from a temporal graph of the organisation): agents raised 1,663 false-positive tasks, of which 60% re-opened a request already answered on an earlier day, 28% took on a request addressed to a colleague, and 13% cited only background material with no request behind it; of 2,304 closed items raised, 43% were treated as open. The weakest discoverers (claude-haiku-4.5, gpt-5.4-mini, DeepSeek-V4-Flash) miss 60, 36 and 32 of 120 tasks per run. Discovery precision correlates only weakly with execution quality (r=0.27; recall r=0.65). Every discovered task cites the records it came from, which is what made the false-positive analysis possible — [AutoBuddy, arXiv:2611.12393, v1 2026-11-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12393) `[authors' own benchmark, third-party models]`, synthetic organisation (full text)
- Personal multilingual inbox (Italian + English, one owner, 231 emails, human labels collected in the review loop; two blind annotators agree with the reference at κ 0.81 and 0.86): the type stored by the production system scores 28% against a 57% majority class ("the stored labels were written by the system itself"). With about 200 labels a TF-IDF + logistic regression has the highest point accuracy on every field (type 0.77 ± 0.02) at a thousandth of the latency. Composed into actions, the classifier ignores 18% of emails that needed action versus 4% for the best generative model. The sender's relationship to the owner is recovered from text only 0.60 of the time, but inheriting it from the contact graph is right 98.6% of the time where the sender was seen before — [Measure Before You Train, arXiv:2610.32955, v1 2026-10-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32955) `[authors' own]`, single-owner corpus (full text, partial)
- Enterprise email agent benchmark (206 scenarios, 258 executable assertions + 211 LLM rubrics): the best configuration passes 33.5% of scenarios although 99.7% of its tool calls complete without an API failure — [EmailBench, arXiv:2609.31906, v1 2026-09-25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31906) `[authors' own benchmark]` (abstract)
- Write-time "factwashing" (a rewrite keeps the claim but drops who said it, how sure they were, when it held): in a blind-labelled corpus of memory writes, 55% of bad writes occur in conversational hearsay versus 7% in business email (p<0.001). A deterministic negation word list reaches 0.91 F1 on untuned text; hedging and attribution cues plateau near half recall with vocabulary, and a one-question LLM "witness" recovers +17 and +15 recall points at equal precision; on unmodified mem0 2.0.7 the gate flags 5 of 8 hedged-hearsay writes — [FACTWASH, arXiv:2608.03372, v1 2026-08-04](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03372) `[authors' own]`, single author (abstract)
- Memory notes flatten temporal form: writers turned "I am working at X" into "The user works at X" while keeping the simple-present control in 210 of 381 matched pairs, never the reverse, in all 11 models tested and in mem0, Graphiti and Letta; readers treat the flattened note as more durable (one reader acted without asking on 64% of "works" notes vs 40% of "is working" notes). Notes that kept the user's words restored the difference — [Memory Consolidation Flattens the Temporal Shape of User Facts, arXiv:2609.36457, v1 2026-09-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36457) `[independent audit]` (abstract)
- Multi-party memory: the strongest system reaches 46.0% average accuracy on GroupMemBench, with knowledge update at 27.1% and term ambiguity at 37.7%, "while a simple BM25 baseline matches or exceeds most agent memory systems" — [GroupMemBench, arXiv:2605.14498, v1 2026-05-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14498) `[authors' own benchmark]` (abstract). A later evidence-chain memory with a "propose-verify-commit" write protocol that preserves message-level evidence reports 68.2% on the same benchmark (+22.7 points over the strongest baseline) and 77.9% on EverMemBench — [Propose, Verify, Commit, arXiv:2609.23465, v1 2026-09-20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23465) `[authors' own]` (abstract). A speaker-centred design that stores speaker-labelled verbatim messages next to derived states reports 47.9% binary accuracy on GroupMemBench — [SpeakerMem-R1, arXiv:2609.26780, v1 2026-09-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26780) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Decisions from meetings: a multi-agent workflow with an explicit critique node (to remove hallucinated, overlapping or inconsistent decisions) extracts architecture decisions from five real meeting transcripts; four senior architects judged it to capture most expert-identified decisions; retrieval-based enrichment improved depth "while simultaneously risking transcript-unfaithful content" — [GADR, arXiv:2608.17694, v1 2026-08-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17694) `[authors' own]`, feasibility study (full text, partial)
- Evaluation resource: OSynth, 570 hours of synthetic meetings generated from a latent ground-truth state (org chart, decisions, opened and closed action items, calendar, emails); adding longitudinal context yields significant gains on entity-sensitive dimensions; human raters tell it from real transcripts only 56% of the time — [OSynth, arXiv:2610.29319, v1 2026-10-21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29319) `[authors' own benchmark]` (full text, partial)
- Field extraction without invented values: a field-by-field extractor "hallucinates values for absent fields" under schema-slot pressure while a holistic reader misses non-salient ones; using their disagreement plus other signals gives 0.928 ROC AUC for per-field correctness on invoices (26% failure rate) and 99.1% accuracy at 80% coverage — [ExtractConf, arXiv:2606.24420, v1 2026-06-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24420) `[authors' own]`, single author (abstract)
- Dates: storing dialogue as subject-verb-object event tuples "with resolved datetime ranges and entity aliases" in an event calendar accounts for a 58.9% gain over baseline on LongMemEval-S (overall 92.6–95.6%) — [Chronos, arXiv:2603.16862, v1 2026-03-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16862) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- A proposed representation for commitments in long conversations: typed, source-grounded atoms with canonical identity, conflict, confidence, risk and evidence spans, with metrics such as Critical Atom Recall; only a small diagnostic study is reported — [Compress the Context, Keep the Commitments, arXiv:2605.17304, v1 2026-05-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17304) `[authors' own]`, framework paper (abstract)

### Inferences
- The AutoBuddy error split is a ready-made checklist for false commitments in the end-of-day run: (a) was this already answered or closed on an earlier day, (b) is the owner someone else, (c) is there an actual request or promise in the evidence. Checks (a) and (b) are lookups against existing claims and the contact/participant list rather than language-model judgments.
- Owner should be inherited from structured context (who sent, who was addressed, contact graph) where possible; the inbox study found text inference of relationship at 0.60 against 98.6% by inheritance.
- Keeping the speaker's verbatim words as evidence (not a paraphrase) matters for two measured reasons: hedges and attribution are lost in rewrites, and progressive/temporary phrasing is flattened into durable facts.
- Schema pressure creates values: a due-date or owner field that must be filled invites invention. Two differently framed readings whose disagreement is surfaced is the one 2026 method with numbers for this.
- The owner's chat review is the only source of ground truth; the system's own stored labels should not be reused as evaluation labels.

### Gaps
- No 2026 paper was found that reports precision/recall for commitment or action-item extraction with separate owner-error and due-date-error rates on real meetings, chat or mail. Searches on arXiv (titles and abstracts for "action item", "commitment", "task extraction", "deadline", "due date") and a web search of arXiv/ACL Anthology/OpenReview returned only pre-2026 work or unrelated papers.
- No 2026 evidence on relative-date resolution errors ("by Friday", "next sprint") in extraction, nor on Portuguese or code-switched work chat.
- No 2026 study of extraction from Git commits or coding-assistant sessions into decisions/facts was found; the nearest is the span-level detector for code and tool output (section 1).

## 4. Contradiction and knowledge-update detection between stored facts: methods, benchmarks, temporal validity, superseded facts

### Takeaway
2026 work converges on handling supersession structurally at write time, not by similarity at read time: embedding similarity cannot tell a contradiction from a duplicate (AUROC 0.59), without update resolution about 70% of prompts expose a superseded value, and memory systems classify create/update/supersede operations correctly as little as 20% of the time. Keyed slots with valid-from/valid-to and explicit SUPERSEDES/CONTRADICTS links eliminate stale exposure when the key is right; the unsolved part is assigning a new statement to the right slot (missed merges leave stale values, false merges delete current ones) and flagging true contradictions without over-flagging look-alikes.

### Cited Findings
- Similarity is the wrong signal: on a calibrated dataset cosine similarity separates a contradicted fact from a duplicated one with AUROC 0.59, "as contradictions are often more embedding-similar to the original than rephrased duplicates". A deterministic (subject, relation, object) supersession rule in a bi-temporal ledger (valid time and transaction time), with no threshold and no LLM call, reaches 0.95–1.00 accuracy on evolving knowledge where RAG reaches 0.20–0.47; RAG serves superseded values 15–40% of the time when forced to answer; retrieval latency about 2.1 s versus 16–18 s for LLM reranking, on a local 7B model. Stated limit: the benchmarks are structured single-value templates and "extraction quality — not the supersession mechanism — is the gating factor for messier natural-language contradictions" — [Temporal Validity in Retrieval Memory (MemStrata), arXiv:2606.26511, v1 2026-06-25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26511) `[authors' own]`, single author (full text)
- Pre-generation audit of personal memory: without update resolution 70.3% of prompts expose a superseded value; serving only the active value of each correctly keyed slot eliminates observed stale exposure. The hard part is slot assignment: "Missed merges leave stale values active, whereas false merges silently remove current values"; four LLM key assigners get higher key recall than a rule extractor yet lower clean-retrieval rates, and open-domain merge recall on LongMemEval never exceeds 0.062. Misattribution (a fact about the wrong person) survives validity filtering — [Stale, Misattributed, or Late, arXiv:2610.10265, v1 2026-10-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10265) `[independent audit + reference layer]` (abstract)
- Inspecting the store directly instead of downstream QA (30 fact-evolution trajectories across multi-author documents, emails and conversations, deterministic ground truth): intrinsic and extrinsic evaluation diverge by 16 percentage points on average, and a representative memory system classifies memory operations (create, update, reinforce, supersede) correctly only 20% of the time, "a failure entirely invisible to extrinsic evaluation" — [Beyond Downstream QA (FEB-30), arXiv:2610.22209, v1 2026-10-15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22209) `[authors' own benchmark]` (abstract)
- StateMemBench (234 multi-session scenarios; answers graded as current state, superseded state, or other failure): a state-first method that tracks supersession and dependencies improves current-state accuracy 0.205 → 0.363 over the strongest same-backbone baseline; used as a single-call wrapper over six existing memory/retrieval backends it adds +32 to +67 points, of which +15 to +32 are attributed to state structure by a length- and cost-matched control — [Can Agent Memory Systems Track Evolving State?, arXiv:2608.19652, v1 2026-08-20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19652) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- A stronger model does not fix it: on LongMemEval's knowledge-update subset, replacing full context with a bounded self-maintained memory drops accuracy from 92% to 77% on gpt-5.4; as the conversation grows 24x accuracy falls from 68% to 28%, and more memory gives no recovery — [Supersede, arXiv:2606.27472, v1 2026-06-25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27472) `[authors' own]`, single author (abstract)
- Contradiction detection versus over-flagging (human-validated track of 161 items, κ=0.85 after adjudication): flat-RAG baselines detect 0.76–0.97 of true contradictions but falsely flag 16–43% of surface-matched safe drafts; a deployed coherence-oriented system almost never over-flags (0.98–1.00 specificity) yet catches 42%. Every gold contradiction is detectable from its evidence alone (recall 1.000), pointing to retrieval coverage as the gap. Each detect metric is paired with a matched do-not-over-detect control — [TWIST, arXiv:2609.28575, v1 2026-09-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28575) `[authors' own proposed benchmark]`, single author (abstract)
- Write-time conflict handling with explicit item status: cheap symbolic conflict checks first, LLM reconciliation only for unresolved semantic cases; 0.97 accuracy on a label-blind ConflictBank evaluation versus 0.61 for the strongest aggregation baseline; removing the checker or the reconciler costs 12–14 points — [LatticeMind, arXiv:2608.08236, v1 2026-08-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08236) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Two-stage conflict detection (embedding-based MLP classifier, selective LLM refinement) cuts API cost 62% at 90.8% detection accuracy; 88.7% conflict-detection F1 on three benchmarks — [ConflictRAG, arXiv:2605.17301, v1 2026-05-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17301) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Why two sources conflict (several valid answers, contested, stale, false source): typed single-pass "decision models" reach 50.0–67.0% accuracy, Gemini 3.8 Flash 70.0%; routing the least confident quarter to the LLM ties it at 25–37% of its cost; "decomposing the taxonomy into yes/no questions lowers accuracy for every model" and "dates play no measurable role" — [Do We Need an LLM to Know Why Sources Conflict?, arXiv:2610.36915, v1 2026-10-27](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36915) `[independent audit]` (abstract)
- Models follow stale notes: stale-value reliance is 0.92–1.00 across Qwen3 0.6–8B checkpoints when stored information is needed; larger checkpoints resist a plain stale note "but incur substantial harm when it appears newer"; metadata helps, and resolving the conflict before the prompt ("pre-resolution") gives near-ceiling accuracy at all sizes — [The Memory Trust Gap, arXiv:2609.01852, v1 2026-09-01](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01852) `[independent audit]` (abstract)
- Provenance links are not followed unless prompted: with a budget of two records, sixteen models inspected a settled-looking constraint's provenance in about one episode in five and produced stale-consistent decisions in 77.3%, 74.7% and 74.7% of episodes; forcing one slot onto the critical path removed most (+74.0, +72.7, +61.3 points); a one-sentence rule ("prefer memories that state a limit on a candidate direction") recovered the effect (+89.3 points) while a content-free freshness cue did not — [When Stale Constraints Go Unchecked, arXiv:2608.25553, v1 2026-08-26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25553) `[independent audit]`, single author (abstract)
- Audit from stored state to draft: an LLM proposes old→new transitions from timestamped evidence; "deterministic code pins each quotation to a single entry, checks that the new evidence really is newer, and lets only these verified transitions trigger repair"; +5.0 points (95% CI [+2.9, +7.2]) on the STALE protocol. The authors state that what is verified "is provenance and chronology - not semantic supersession" — [When Memory Updates but Behavior Does Not (StateAuditor), arXiv:2608.01619, v1 2026-08-03](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01619) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Derived artefacts go stale even when memory is fresh: plans that carry exact links to the inputs they were derived from allow a pre-action check of whether those inputs changed; supplying inherited input versions raised detected hand-off conflicts from 0/30 to 30/30 — [Fresh Memory, Stale Plans (Planfence), arXiv:2609.03340, v1 2026-09-03](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03340) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Schema designs reported in 2026: typed FactEvents with mandatory provenance spans, mention time, occurrence time and validity, with SUPERSEDES / CONTRADICTS / UPDATES edges (92.8% on LongMemEval-S, self-graded with the same model as reader and judge) — [SodaMem, arXiv:2608.08055, v1 2026-08-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08055) `[authors' own]` (abstract); validity annotations "so obsolete facts remain accessible for historical queries but are discounted for current-state answers" — [TRACE, arXiv:2607.00339, v1 2026-07-01](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00339) `[authors' own]` (abstract); preference/plan updates stored as explicit transition records retrievable alongside facts — [TransMem, arXiv:2611.10683, v1 2026-11-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10683) `[authors' own]` (abstract); per-speaker history of revised figures (86.5% vs 73.4% on revised-figure questions) — [TrackMem, arXiv:2611.02001, v1 2026-11-01](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02001) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Long-horizon multi-app benchmark (15 months of synthetic activity per user): "no system both keeps facts that stay true and replaces facts that change", and over 93% of failures trace to what memory retrieves — [DynamicMem, arXiv:2606.22877, v1 2026-06-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22877) `[authors' own benchmark]` (abstract)

### Inferences
- Word-overlap heuristics are in the same family as the embedding-similarity signal that scored AUROC 0.59; the 2026 alternative is a keyed comparison: same (subject, relation) slot, different value, newer evidence → supersedes; contradiction candidates only among same-slot items. The LLM's job moves to slot assignment and to the residual semantic cases.
- Slot assignment is where errors will concentrate, and both error directions are harmful (missed merge vs false merge). The owner's confirm/correct step is a natural place to adjudicate proposed merges, since a false merge silently removes a current fact.
- "As of" dates need two timestamps (when it was true, when it was recorded) and superseded facts should be retained with validity closed, not deleted, so historical wiki statements remain citable.
- Wiki pages are derived artefacts; storing which claim versions each sentence was compiled from makes "this sentence depends on a superseded claim" a deterministic check (the Planfence and ClaimWriter results).
- Any contradiction detector should be scored with matched hard negatives, because the 2026 baselines that catch most contradictions also flag 16–43% of safe look-alikes.

### Gaps
- No 2026 result on contradiction detection in Portuguese or mixed-language stores.
- No 2026 benchmark covers claims with owner + due date where the "update" is a rescheduled deadline or a reassigned owner.
- Most supersession results are on synthetic or templated data; MemStrata's authors state natural-language contradictions are not yet covered.
- I did not find a 2026 head-to-head of small NLI models against LLM judges for pairwise contradiction detection between stored facts.

## 5. Calibration and abstention: getting a model to say "not enough evidence"; confidence labels that are reliable

### Takeaway
Self-reported confidence from small local models is unusable (ceiling in about 92% of answers), and token log-probabilities saturate under structured output; on recent top-tier proprietary models verbalized confidence has become a better soft signal than log-probs for judging tasks. The signals with measured reliability in 2026 are external: disagreement between two differently framed reads or between model families, decomposed sub-checks, and a threshold calibrated on human-labelled data (including conformal guarantees). Abstention is fragile to pipeline design: iterative retrieval and "thinking" lower correct-abstention rates, and missing support is harder for models to recognize than contradiction.

### Cited Findings
- Seven 3–9B open-weight instruct models, pre-registered: all seven are "Invalid" on numeric verbalized confidence, with a mean ceiling rate of 91.7%; categorical elicitation did not rescue validity and disrupted task performance in six of seven; token log-probability did not predict verbalized confidence (R² < 0.01) — [Verbal Confidence Saturation in 3-9B Open-Weight Instruction-Tuned LLMs, arXiv:2604.22215, v1 2026-04-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22215) `[independent audit]`, single author (abstract)
- On post-2025 top-tier proprietary models used as judges (SummEval, AggreFact, HelpSteer2; up to 18 LLMs), verbalized confidence is the more robust soft score than log-probabilities; an "overconfidence advisory" plus self-debate improves calibration with little balanced-accuracy cost on new models; "the shift is invisible under accuracy-only reporting" — [Rethinking Verbalized Confidence for LLM-as-a-Judge, arXiv:2609.10996, v1 2026-09-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10996) `[independent audit]`, single author (abstract)
- Judge log-probs "saturate above 0.999 with structured JSON output"; confidence derived from the judge's own decomposed sub-checks (step–verdict alignment, claim-level margin, evidence grounding) gives AUROC 0.72–0.91 on GPT-4.1-mini and 0.66–0.80 on GPT-5.4-mini; on Qwen3.5-4B/9B/27B answer-token logprobs are anti-calibrated (AUROC 0.32–0.49) — [VERDI, arXiv:2605.11334, v1 2026-05-11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11334) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Extraction confidence: "Token-level log-probabilities, verbalized confidence, and multi-sample self-consistency all collapse toward all-positive behaviour at practical thresholds"; disagreement between two structurally different reads fused with other signals gives 0.928 ROC AUC, 70% lower selective-prediction risk than logprob-mean, 99.1% accuracy at 80% coverage — [ExtractConf, arXiv:2606.24420, v1 2026-06-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24420) `[authors' own]`, single author (abstract)
- On a 0.5B model, gating expression on mean answer-token probability ranks correctness below chance (AUC 0.41) while sampling consistency discriminates (AUC 0.66) — [Truth for Believable AI, arXiv:2609.26035, v1 2026-09-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26035) `[authors' own]`, tiny model, several pre-specified criteria failed (abstract)
- Abstention benchmark with post-cutoff unanswerable questions, 27 LLMs: single-shot retrieval raises the correct-abstention rate in 38 of 40 configurations; iterative RAG (self-assess, retrieve again) lowers it in 30 of 40 with almost no accuracy change (68.5% → 69.3%); adding query rewriting and reranking lowers it for all 12 models tested; neither model size nor release date is associated with abstention; enabling thinking without retrieval lowers it for 8 of 13 models (by 14 points on average), shifting responses "from acknowledged uncertainty to premise denial" — [DeclineCraft, arXiv:2610.28340, v1 2026-10-20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28340) `[independent audit]` (abstract)
- Abstaining via an external confidence threshold matches learned abstention: "Train for Accuracy, then Post-hoc Select" with a probing-based estimator attains 1.5–9.4 points lower mean risk than three abstention-training methods at matched coverage, and keeps coverage adjustable — [Strong yet Controllable LLM Abstention without Learning to Abstain, arXiv:2610.16318, v1 2026-10-12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16318) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Distribution-free guarantee: a split-conformal threshold calibrated on human-gold unsupported citations bounds the fraction of truly unsupported citations that slip through, under exchangeability; needs recalibration on target-domain negatives — [arXiv:2607.20527, v1 2026-07-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20527) `[authors' own]` (full text)
- "Not Found" is the hard class: across models, missing support is harder to detect than direct contradiction — [PARCEL, arXiv:2610.10971, v1 2026-10-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10971) `[authors' own benchmark]` (abstract)
- Abstention is easy to mis-score: treating a context without gold documents as unanswerable is wrong for 28–72% of such contexts as retrievers improve; the authors recommend scoring refusals against content labels — [Better Retrievers, Worse Labels, arXiv:2610.40966, v1 2026-10-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40966) `[independent audit]` (abstract)
- Over-holding is the common error at commit boundaries: across 30 model conditions models wrongly hold authorized, evidence-cleared work on 28.1% of opportunities and wrongly allow unsafe work on 1.0%; accuracy on evidence-reversed mirrors of famous incidents is 63.8% versus 98.5% on the incidents themselves — [SteerBench-Work, arXiv:2608.12654, v1 2026-08-12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12654) `[authors' own benchmark]` (abstract)
- Lab guidance: measure a grader's uncertainty "by checking whether the grader gives different judgments across multiple attempts" and send those items to human review — [Building strong safety evaluations, Anthropic, 2026-11-13](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-strong-safety-evaluations) `[vendor guidance]` (opened via fetch summary)

### Inferences
- A reliable label on a claim or wiki sentence should be computed from observable checks (support verdict from a separate verifier, agreement between two reads, owner's confirmation status, slot validity), not from the writer's stated confidence; the 2026 evidence for self-reported confidence is positive only for the newest proprietary models in a judging role.
- Offering "insufficient evidence" as an explicit verdict in the verifier's three-way output (supported / contradicted / not found) matches how the 2026 benchmarks are framed, and the not-found class deserves its own precision/recall tracking because it is the one models miss.
- Multi-round self-assessment loops in the end-of-day agent may reduce abstention rather than improve it (iterative RAG result); a single evidence pass followed by an external check is the safer default suggested by this evidence.
- Thresholds only mean something once calibrated against the owner's confirmed/retracted history; that history is a free calibration set.

### Gaps
- No 2026 study of calibration for extracted commitments or for wiki-sentence labels specifically.
- No evidence on whether verbalized confidence from current Claude models is calibrated for extraction (the positive verbalized-confidence result covers judging tasks on "post-2025 proprietary models" without my having verified which ones).
- I did not find a 2026 lab publication with a quantitative evaluation of an "insufficient evidence" option.

## 6. LLM-as-judge reliability since Feb 2026: agreement with humans, biases, how labs calibrate judges

### Takeaway
Unaudited judges can be near-useless (a deployed production judge agreed with human gold at κ=0.04 on hard cases and 0.42 on a random sample), while audited ones reach κ≈0.7–0.8, and a self-hosted 27B open model matched a frontier model at about 1/300 the cost. Reported agreement is highly sensitive to protocol (the same verdicts yield accuracy 0.551 or 0.899), self-consistency does not imply validity (98% repeatability with 56% agreement), and judges show family preference, position reversals, leniency toward stronger examinees, scale-format effects and credulity toward duplicated sources. The lab and paper consensus: atomic binary criteria, a different model family as judge, a human-labelled anchor set of roughly 100 or more items, chance-corrected agreement compared with human–human agreement, per-direction error rates, and routing by unanimity or uncertainty to humans.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic guidance (2026-11-13): have humans label a sample blind to the grader and report chance-corrected agreement (e.g., Cohen's kappa); "A grader should agree with experts about as often as experts agree with each other"; if humans agree with each other but not with the grader, revisit the rubric; use "a set of atomic, binary (yes/no) criteria rather than one overall score" and state explicitly what does not meet each criterion; consider a different model as grader than the one tested because graders can favor their own outputs; graders favor longer responses and the first option in pairwise comparisons; re-grade a sample with a different model or reworded rubric — if conclusions change, "you've likely measured the grader, not the model"; do not compare scores from different graders; alert when a grader breaks and exclude broken items; treat 100 items as the floor for a complex multi-turn eval and 1,000 for a simple single-turn one — [Building strong safety evaluations, Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-strong-safety-evaluations) `[vendor guidance]` (opened via fetch summary; quotes as returned by the fetch tool)
- Production audit: a deployed gpt-4o-mini judge agreed with two-author gold at κ=0.04 on a disagreement-enriched set and 0.42 on a uniform-random spot-check, over-flagging 77.1% of human-FAITHFUL cases. A self-hosted Qwen3.6-27B (κ=0.72) lands with Claude Opus 4.7 (κ=0.71; n=96, underpowered) at roughly 1/300 the cost per call. "Pairing the weak judge with a stronger one degrades agreement, whereas three strong judges under unanimity routing reach kappa = 0.79 at 89.7% auto-coverage" — [Auditing and Repairing LLM-as-Judge Failures in a Production Text-to-SQL Pipeline, arXiv:2609.30290, v1 2026-09-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30290) `[authors' own audit]` (abstract)
- Measurement protocol: for binary verdicts Pearson, Spearman, Kendall τ-b, phi and MCC are the same statistic; on one rubric benchmark "protocol choice alone moves reported accuracy from 0.551 to 0.899" and carries κ across zero without changing a verdict (scale, retained cases, handling of abstentions/invalid outputs, pooling); a reporting checklist is provided — [Agreement Metrics for LLM-as-Judge Evaluation, arXiv:2606.00093, v1 2026-05-25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00093) `[independent analysis]` (abstract)
- When may a judge replace annotators: a bootstrap test that swaps one human annotation for the LLM's and checks non-inferiority of inter-annotator agreement; "about 100 items often suffice"; across 18 LLMs, 5 prompting techniques and 20 datasets, LLMs pass in about half of the setups, depending mainly on the task — [AltRA-Test, arXiv:2611.26073, v1 2026-11-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26073) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Consistency is not validity: on tau2-bench (2,160 episodes; 120 double-annotated) an LLM judge repeated its verdict 98.2% of the time but agreed with native state-based scoring on 56.2%; it passed 10–11 of 11 episodes containing a forbidden write (a stronger judge still passed 6), while a rule-based check with no model call caught all 11 — [When Evaluators of AI Agents Disagree, arXiv:2611.14581, v1 2026-11-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14581) `[independent audit]`, single author (abstract). Two local 7–8B judges show Pearson 0.275 and 0.340 with nine-annotator human means despite 97.3% and 92.3% exact self-consistency — [When Consistency Does Not Mean Reliability, arXiv:2609.13824, v1 2026-09-12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13824) `[independent audit]`, single author, weak examinee model (abstract)
- Biases measured in 2026: same-family preference of 3.4–8.4 percentage points across four open-weight families, 55.4% of AB/BA pairs reverse with position, and panel composition changes 18.5% of pairwise outcomes — [Who Judges Matters, arXiv:2609.17857, v1 2026-09-15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17857) `[independent audit]` (abstract); a model's task accuracy predicts its judging accuracy (r ≥ 0.90) and more capable examinees receive more lenient judgments from all judges (r ≥ 0.83); weighting judges by error rates estimated from inter-judge disagreement tracks an oracle within 0.5 points in simulation — [Can We Trust LLM Judges, arXiv:2609.12002, v1 2026-09-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12002) `[authors' own]` (abstract); numeric scale width, offset and direction systematically shift scores, and averaging both anchor directions reduces the dependence — [Same Judgement, Different Scores, arXiv:2611.26077, v1 2026-11-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26077) and [Rubric Semantics or Numerical Priors?, arXiv:2611.25588, v1 2026-11-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25588) `[independent audits]` (abstracts); changing only a source cue (human vs AI) shifts truth verdicts, so trust scores are not independent evidence — [When Trust Meets Truth, arXiv:2608.21097, v1 2026-08-21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21097) `[independent audit]` (abstract)
- Copies counted as confirmations: three identical reprints instead of one report raised accepted false claims from 0.06 to 0.71 (Llama 3.2) and 0.21 to 0.90 (phi4); showing the judge one report per detected copy chain cut accepted copied lies by 0.10–0.24 at a cost of at most 4.2 points of accepted truths; text similarity misses LLM-reworded copies — [Copies Are Not Confirmations, arXiv:2611.09203, v1 2026-11-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09203) `[independent audit]` (abstract)
- Criterion mismatch: on 900 human-labelled QA pairs, automated labelers (lexical, NLI, seven LLM judges) disagree substantially with each other and with humans and show directional error biases; switching the judge prompt from a faithfulness criterion to a factual-correctness criterion improves agreement — [The Labeling Problem in Hallucination Detection Benchmarks, arXiv:2610.08026, v1 2026-10-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08026) `[independent audit]` (abstract)
- Citation-specific judges: at similar F1, judges differ in false-positive rate, false-negative rate and pass-rate drift, and cheap judges are competitive ([arXiv:2607.08700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08700), v1 2026-07-09, full text); a claim-level judge calibrated on n=100 human items reaches κ 0.484 / 0.797 / 0.743 across three dimensions, scoring "one claim against a short shortlist at a time" ([arXiv:2608.28643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28643), v1 2026-08-12, full text); a refined rubric judge "does not align with human annotators on precise scoring" but "reliably identifies critical failure modes" ([CryptoAnalystBench, arXiv:2602.11304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11304), v1 2026-02-11, abstract)
- Post-hoc calibration on a small human anchor set: quantile matching between judge and human score distributions gives the best distributional fidelity and "narrows the raw error gap between smaller and larger judge models" — [Quantiles, Not Points, arXiv:2611.00447, v1 2026-10-30](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00447) `[authors' own]` (abstract); learning a correction from counterfactual probes improves agreement with human rankings without a rubric — [PRISM, arXiv:2610.18389, v1 2026-10-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18389) `[authors' own]` (abstract); discovering bias factors from human–LLM disagreement cases and regressing them out reduces bias-driven flips — [Auditing the Judge (HUB-J), arXiv:2611.33065, v1 2026-11-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33065) `[authors' own]` (abstract)

### Inferences
- The verifier proposed in section 1 is itself a judge and needs the same treatment: a blind human-labelled sample from the owner's own claims (on the order of 100 items by the AltRA and Anthropic figures), κ plus separate false-accept and false-reject rates, and a re-check whenever the model or prompt changes.
- Deterministic checks should carry everything they can (dates, owners, existence, quote-present, chronology); the tau2-bench result shows a zero-model rule catching what judges repeatedly passed.
- Counting citations as independent support is the same error as counting copies as confirmations: a commit, the chat message announcing it and the session that produced it are one source chain.
- If a judge ensemble is used, the 2026 data favour several strong, different-family judges with unanimity routing and humans on the remainder; adding a weak judge to a strong one made agreement worse.

### Gaps
- No 2026 lab publication from OpenAI or Google on judge calibration was reachable or found within the window; the Anthropic item is guidance without disclosed agreement numbers.
- No 2026 measurement of judge agreement with humans for Portuguese text specifically.
- Pairwise-versus-absolute judging was not directly compared for faithfulness in any 2026 source I read; the position-reversal figure (55.4%) comes from open-weight 2024-era model families.

## 7. How human confirmation is best used: which items a person should review; evidence on reviewer fatigue and rubber-stamping

### Takeaway
Blanket approval degrades into rubber-stamping: Anthropic reports users accept 93% of permission prompts and names approval fatigue, experienced users shift from approving every action to monitoring and interrupting, and controlled studies show reviewers judge whether the output looks right rather than checking the risky property, with upfront approval reducing later scrutiny. Evidence favours spending human attention on a risk-ranked minority (a stronger-model risk score put 81.6% of major errors in the top 10% of items; unanimity or agreement gates auto-handle 70–90%) plus a random audit sample for calibration, presenting evidence rather than a pre-filled verdict so the reviewer is not anchored. The claim that more escalation can lower safety is so far a modelling result, not a measured one.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor data on approval behaviour: Claude Code users "accept 93% of them anyway" (manual permission prompts), described as "approval fatigue, where people stop paying close attention to what they're approving". The replacement classifier's full pipeline has a 0.4% false-positive rate on real traffic (n=10,000) and misses 17% of real overeager actions (n=52) and 5.7% of synthetic exfiltration attempts (n=1,000); stage 1 alone: 8.5% FPR, 6.6% and 1.8% misses. The classifier sees only user messages and tool calls: "We strip assistant text so the agent can't talk the classifier into making a bad call" — [Claude Code auto mode, Anthropic engineering, 2026-03-25](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode) `[vendor-reported]` (opened via fetch summary). A later Anthropic announcement is reported to cite 97% (search snippet only, not opened).
- Vendor data on how oversight shifts with experience: full auto-approve is used in roughly 20% of sessions by newer users (under 50 sessions) and over 40% by 750 sessions; interruptions rise from about 5% of turns (around 10 sessions) to about 9% for experienced users ("auto-approve more but also interrupt more"); on the most complex tasks the agent stops to ask for clarification more than twice as often as humans interrupt it — [Measuring AI agent autonomy in practice, Anthropic, 2026-02-18](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy) `[vendor-reported]` (opened via fetch summary)
- Controlled study, 48 participants, 192 live web sessions, four oversight strategies: strategy changed exposure to problematic actions "but not users' ability to intervene"; control often returned too early or too late; "users judged whether the agent acted correctly, not whether the action was safe"; and "upfront approval inhibited runtime scrutiny" — [Comparing Human Oversight Strategies for Computer-Use Agents, arXiv:2604.04918, v1 2026-04-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04918) `[independent user study]` (abstract)
- Between-subjects study, N=192, medical questions with LLM uncertainty shown at three granularities: token-level uncertainty increased agreement with the AI; output- and step-level uncertainty did not increase agreement but lowered users' confidence in their own answers; step-level uncertainty also reduced external verification (searches, checking the provided URLs), "steering users away from independent fact-checking and toward reliance on the LLM and its accompanying uncertainty cues" — [Not All Uncertainty Is Equal, arXiv:2605.28571, v1 2026-05-27](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28571) `[independent user study]` (abstract)
- Anchoring: common human-in-the-loop evaluation set-ups "either accidentally anchor human experts (leading to rubber-stamping) or leave them unsupported"; the proposed division of labour has humans identify what information matters (nuggets) and LLMs do high-volume matching — [Human-in-the-Loop Nugget Annotation, arXiv:2606.29033, v1 2026-06-27](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29033) `[authors' own prototype]`, no measured rubber-stamping rate (abstract)
- Reviewer as a finite, fatiguing resource: on 125 hand-labelled agent actions reviewers only moderately agree on what is risky (Fleiss' κ=0.52); when the reviewer is modelled as fatiguing with escalation load, realized safety is an inverted-U in escalation rate. The authors state the inverted-U and the flooding attack "are modeling results that motivate a human study" — [Oversight Has a Capacity, arXiv:2606.08919, v1 2026-06-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08919) `[authors' own]`, single author (abstract + full text skim)
- Human "faithful" judgments average over sentences instead of requiring every sentence to be supported — [Averaging Bias, arXiv:2608.00205, v1 2026-07-31](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00205) `[independent audit]` (full text, partial)
- Risk-ranked review: with 15,493 LLM translations and no references, reviewing the top 10% by a stronger-model panel's risk score captured 81.6% of major errors; a same-tier panel was useful but worse; source novelty worked as a prior, not a per-item detector; signals were validated against a 500-item author-adjudicated anchor — [Can We Triage LLM Translation Errors..., arXiv:2609.14963, v1 2026-09-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14963) `[authors' own]`, single author, major-error labels partly LLM-adjudicated (abstract)
- Coverage gates with measured residual error: disagreement-based confidence gives 99.1% accuracy on the 80% auto-accepted fields ([ExtractConf, arXiv:2606.24420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24420), v1 2026-06-23, abstract); three strong judges under unanimity auto-decide 89.7% at κ=0.79 ([arXiv:2609.30290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30290), v1 2026-09-09, abstract); a strict cross-route agree gate automates 70% of items "and still acts on 16% of errors" ([Anchor and Check, arXiv:2611.25818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25818), v1 2026-11-19, abstract); a cascade that sends the least confident quarter to a stronger model ties it at 25–37% of the cost ([arXiv:2610.36915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36915), v1 2026-10-27, abstract)
- Statistical use of a small audit budget: LLM outputs guide which items humans audit, and audited labels are combined with LLM labels to give anytime-valid confidence sequences for the true rate, with fewer audits than standard approaches in experiments on three real datasets — [Anytime-Valid Empirical Likelihood Inference from LLM Annotations with Adaptive Human Auditing, arXiv:2611.15197, v1 2026-11-11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15197) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Human gates at decision points (not per item) cut critical failures from 72% to 16% of runs together with deterministic computation — [arXiv:2606.12848, v1 2026-06-11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12848) `[authors' own]` (abstract)
- Lab guidance: route to humans the items where the grader's verdict changes across repeated attempts — [Building strong safety evaluations, Anthropic, 2026-11-13](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-strong-safety-evaluations) `[vendor guidance]`
- Operational practice report: "alert-fatigue budgeting borrowed from SRE practice" for a persistent agent-memory subsystem over months of use (78,933 hook invocations, 85 recorded failures, none silent) — [Memory as Infrastructure, arXiv:2609.05510, v1 2026-08-31 per arXiv API](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05510) `[authors' own]`, N=1, self-reported (abstract)

### Inferences
- A daily review that asks the owner to confirm every claim is the set-up the 93% figure describes. The 2026 evidence supports auto-accepting low-risk items that pass deterministic and verifier checks, and showing the owner (a) items where checks or two reads disagree, (b) items with high consequence (commitments with dates and owners, proposed merges/supersessions, contradictions), and (c) a small random sample of auto-accepted items so the error rate of the automatic path stays measured.
- Review cards should show the verbatim evidence span next to the claim and ask a specific question (is this owner right, is this date right), since reviewers otherwise judge overall plausibility; showing a confidence badge at fine granularity may increase agreement without increasing checking.
- The reviewer's own acceptance rate and time-per-item are cheap fatigue indicators; a rising accept rate with falling attention is the pattern Anthropic describes. Planting a few known-wrong items would measure rubber-stamping directly, but I found no 2026 study that did this for claim review, so this is a suggestion without direct evidence.
- Verifier inputs should exclude the writer's own justification text, by analogy with the auto-mode classifier design that strips assistant prose so the checked agent cannot persuade the checker.

### Gaps
- No 2026 controlled study was found that measures how often a person approves seeded-wrong LLM-extracted claims or summaries (searches for "seeded/planted/injected errors" with participants returned nothing in the window); the rubber-stamping evidence is observational vendor data on tool-permission prompts plus two user studies in adjacent settings.
- The inverted-U "more review can be less safe" result is a model, not a measurement.
- No 2026 evidence on optimal daily review volume or session length for a single owner, or on review in Portuguese.
- The 97% approval figure and a reported comparison of human reviewers versus the classifier on dangerous commands were seen only in search snippets and are unverified.
