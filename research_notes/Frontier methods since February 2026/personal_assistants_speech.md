# Personal work assistants built on activity data, and local speech-to-text for meetings (sources published 2026-02-01 to 2026-11-27 only)

Reading key. Every source below was first published on or after 2026-02-01; the date is written next to each citation (arXiv: v1 date). Access level: **[full]** = body text read; **[abs]** = abstract only, read through the arXiv API; **[card]** = Hugging Face model card read; **[snippet]** = seen only in a web-search snippet, page not opened. Evidence type: **authors' own** (academic self-evaluation), **vendor-reported** (a company describing its own product or model), **independent** (third party evaluating someone else's system). ## 1. Personal and ambient assistants since Feb 2026: models of a user's work from activity streams, proactive assistants, personal knowledge graphs, memory layers; what is measured and what fails

### Takeaway
The 2026 literature has moved from "can an agent remember facts" to "can it build a usable model of one person from passive activity and decide when to speak", and the measured results are still weak: first-day proactive precision is near zero, benchmarks put frontier agents at 26–55% on always-on personal-assistant tasks, and memory systems keep stale or unconfirmed information. The designs that hold up best keep observations linked to evidence, separate measured facts from inferred ones, and learn intervention timing per user from feedback.

### Cited Findings
- **Coco / "Deep Personalization" (arXiv 2611.13457, 2026-11-10, [full], authors' own).** A local "Observer Agent" turns desktop screenshots into four structured fields (observation, user intent, rationale, binary support decision), stores them in a bipartite graph of timestamped observations and higher-level user propositions with edges to the supporting observations, and adapts when to intervene from implicit feedback (thumb up, thumb down or dismissal, explicit requests when silent). In a one-week study with 10 participants the macro F1 proxy rose from 0.199 to 0.515; on day 1, 6 of 10 users had F1 of zero. On the synthetic OSWorld-Persona benchmark (6,558 key frames, 3,279 annotations, 108 sessions, 4 personas, labels simulated by an LLM) macro F1 rose from 0.12–0.19 under direct prompting to 0.35–0.48 after adaptation. The observer ran Qwen3.5-9B on a single RTX 5090 GPU, not a CPU. The study used prompt evolution (a short bullet list of learned rules refined from feedback), which the authors say was the best-performing adaptation method for that backbone on their benchmark; the alternative was LoRA fine-tuning. — [arXiv 2611.13457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13457)
- Coco's authors note two measurement weaknesses: false negatives "may be underestimated, as users may desire assistance without explicitly prompting", and learning only from accept or reject risks a degenerate policy that intervenes less and gets fewer signals, so they add a retrospective scan for missed opportunities (repeated operations, getting stuck, invoking AI). — [arXiv 2611.13457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13457)
- **Activity Frames (arXiv 2608.05784, 2026-08-06, [full], authors' own, single-user corpus of the author).** A deterministic, zero-model compiler turns passively captured screen activity (timestamp, application, window title, URL, input events) into typed "activity frames" with evidence pointers to raw rows. Corpus: 61 calendar days, 46 active, 109,735 snapshot rows, 54 applications. One day of raw capture (126,812 tokens) compiles to a 1,469-token context block (86x smaller) in a median 68 ms on a laptop. Details in section 2. — [arXiv 2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
- **SERUM (arXiv 2607.29181, 2026-07-31, [abs], authors' own).** Multi-pass VLM annotation of screen recordings alternating activity-recognition and intent-inference passes; "each pass refining labels using accumulated prior context to reduce hallucination and temporal conflation seen in single-pass annotation"; synonymous states merged with sentence embeddings; evaluated on 61 egocentric videos in four domains; human annotators rated final-pass labels as accurate and improved over first pass (no numbers in abstract). — [arXiv 2607.29181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29181)
- **LongNAP, next action prediction (arXiv 2603.05923, 2026-03-06, [abs], authors' own).** 360K actions labelled from one month of phone use by 20 users (1,800 hours); only 17.1% of predicted trajectories are well aligned with what the user does next (LLM-judge score ≥ 0.5), 26% when filtered to high-confidence predictions. — [arXiv 2603.05923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05923)
- **GUIDE (arXiv 2603.25864, 2026-03-26, [abs], authors' own).** 67.5 hours of screen recordings from 120 novice users with think-aloud; eight multimodal models reach only 44.6% accuracy on behaviour-state detection and 55.0% on help prediction; giving the model structured user context raised help prediction by up to 50.2 percentage points. — [arXiv 2603.25864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25864)
- **PIRA-Bench (arXiv 2603.08013, 2026-03-09, [abs], authors' own).** Benchmark for proactive intent recommendation from continuous screenshots; states that real screen activity "is rarely linear" and consists of "noisy browsing, meaningless actions, and multithreaded task-switching"; baseline is a memory-aware state tracker that manages multiple task threads. — [arXiv 2603.08013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08013)
- **FileGram (arXiv 2604.04901, 2026-04-06, [abs], authors' own).** Builds user profiles bottom-up from file-system actions and content deltas "rather than dialogue summaries", in procedural, semantic and episodic channels; benchmark tasks are profile reconstruction, trace disentanglement, persona drift detection and multimodal grounding; "remains challenging for state-of-the-art memory systems". — [arXiv 2604.04901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04901)
- **Do proactive agents need an LLM to decide when to act? (arXiv 2605.30152, 2026-05-28, [abs], authors' own).** A small temporal-graph model over events and persistent entities handles both triggering and context selection; 13.99 ms per event on a consumer laptop with about 220 MiB resident memory; a shared controller improved F1 across 14 downstream LLM backbones by a mean of 16.7 points; 4–7x faster at the trigger stage than single-forward LLM triggers. — [arXiv 2605.30152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30152)
- **Personal knowledge graphs.** RUVA (arXiv 2602.15553, 2026-02-17, [abs]) argues vector-store personal memory is unaccountable ("the user cannot inspect the cause nor correct the error") and that deleting from a vector space is imprecise; it grounds memory in a personal knowledge graph the user can inspect and redact. EpisTwin (arXiv 2603.06290, 2026-03-06, [abs]) lifts cross-application data into triples and re-grounds entities in raw visual context at query time; evaluated only on a synthetic benchmark. Both are authors' own claims with no reported user study in the abstract. — [arXiv 2602.15553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15553); [arXiv 2603.06290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06290)
- **Memory that keeps unconfirmed proposals as facts (arXiv 2610.18684, 2026-10-13, [abs], authors' own).** "An assistant's proposal should not become an authorized instruction merely because it is remembered." Across agents and memory systems, strong memory performance did not ensure "grounding awareness"; providing more complete user information increased execution of unacknowledged proposals, and persistent memory caused agents to treat proposals as user instructions. A training-free layer that tracks status (accepted, unacknowledged, revised, scoped) and checks consequential actions "substantially" improved behaviour (no numbers in abstract). — [arXiv 2610.18684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18684)
- **Memory misalignment from the user's side (arXiv 2609.33623, 2026-09-27, [abs], authors' own).** Memory-usage data (N=28, 457 entries) and a diary study (N=32, 304 reports) give a taxonomy of 14 misalignment types across intake, storage, retrieval and interpretation; in a speed-dating study with 121 users, people "prioritize proactive controls over cognitively demanding causal graph inspections or passive audit logs". — [arXiv 2609.33623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33623)
- **Stale preferences (HorizonBench, arXiv 2604.17283, 2026-04-19, [abs], authors' own).** Six months of simulated conversation per user, calibrated on a human dataset of 83,024 ratings from 160 people over 13 weeks; all 25 frontier models chose the outdated preference more often than chance errors would predict; best accuracy 52.8%; one added sentence stating the change raised accuracy by 11–22 points; retrieval and memory methods did not recover the loss. — [arXiv 2604.17283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17283)
- **Memory quality vs task success (MemAudit / MEMPROBE, arXiv 2606.24595, 2026-06-23, [abs], authors' own).** Task completion nearly saturates even for a memoryless baseline, while recovery of the hidden user state from the memory store stays around 0.6 and drops under top-k retrieval. — [arXiv 2606.24595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24595)
- **Sophisticated memory vs simple retrieval (LifeDialBench, arXiv 2604.11182, 2026-04-13, [abs], authors' own).** With an online protocol that respects temporal causality, "current sophisticated memory systems fail to outperform a simple RAG-based baseline", which the authors attribute to "over-designed structures and lossy compression". — [arXiv 2604.11182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11182)
- **Persistent harness memory (PraxisMemBench, arXiv 2610.20022, 2026-10-14, [abs], authors' own).** Relation-related failures grow from early to late sessions (59.8% to 72.9%); the authors' trained memory manager reaches 40.78% session success against 20.60% for Claude Code's native memory. — [arXiv 2610.20022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20022)
- **Lab products (vendor-reported).** OpenAI "Chronicle" for the Codex app on macOS, an opt-in research preview announced 2026-04-20: builds memories from recent screen activity; screen captures older than 6 hours are deleted; selected frames are processed on OpenAI servers; memories are Markdown files under `~/.codex/memories_extensions/chronicle`; OpenAI warns it can consume rate limits quickly. [snippet only: developers.openai.com was unreachable from this session.] — [OpenAI Chronicle docs](https://developers.openai.com/codex/memories/chronicle)
- Google "Daily Brief" in the Gemini app, announced at I/O on 2026-05-19: a morning digest drawing on inbox, calendar and tasks that ranks items and suggests next steps; tuned with thumbs up or down. [snippet only.] No user-study data was found from Google. — [blog.google I/O 2026 roundup](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/google-io-2026-all-our-announcements/)
- **Task resumption, measured.** In a between-subjects study (n=36, two sessions one week apart) steerable AI summaries that users can correct and fact-check "supported faster task resumption" and broader conceptual coverage, while self-written notes gave richer inter-concept connections and higher self-reported certainty (arXiv 2610.12755, 2026-10-08, [abs], authors' own; no effect sizes in abstract). — [arXiv 2610.12755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12755)
- For coding agents, interleaving other unfinished tasks cuts focus-task resolution by 11.5 percentage points on average (6.0 to 15.0 across 7 agents); recurring failures are cross-task requirement confusion and loss of task state; a module that keeps concise per-task state summaries recovers 5.5–15.5 points (Switch-SWE, arXiv 2611.02017, 2026-11-01, [abs], authors' own). — [arXiv 2611.02017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02017)

### Inferences
- Evidence-linked memory is the common thread in the systems that report gains (Coco's proposition-to-observation edges, Activity Frames' evidence pointers, the measured vs inferred boundary). For the layer this argues for storing every derived statement (project status, decision, commitment) with pointers to the SQLite rows or connector items it came from.
- The grounding-awareness result maps directly onto the propose-then-confirm flow: a proposed decision, commitment or blocker should carry an explicit status (proposed, confirmed, rejected, superseded) and unconfirmed items should never be retrieved as facts by the briefing or review tools.
- A cold start with near-zero useful proactive output is expected (6 of 10 users on day 1), and useful timing rules were learned as a short readable rule list within a week from thumb signals; the confirm and reject clicks the owner already gives are the same kind of signal.
- Per-task state summaries are the one resumption aid with a measured benefit in 2026 sources, though only for agents (Switch-SWE) and in one n=36 human study without reported effect sizes.

### Gaps
- No 2026 source measured human task-resumption time with an activity-based assistant on real work; the one human study (n=36) is academic search and reports direction only.
- No peer-reviewed or lab evaluation of Chronicle, Daily Brief or similar products was found; product facts are from search snippets.
- Coco's observer needed a 9B multimodal model on an RTX 5090; no 2026 source showed screen-based observation on a CPU-only laptop. Metadata-only approaches (Activity Frames, the temporal-graph controller) are the CPU-feasible ones found.
- No 2026 source evaluated a personal knowledge graph with real users over weeks.

## 2. Summarising work activity: raw event logs into accurate day or week summaries and project status; sessionisation and task segmentation; hallucinated activity and how to prevent it

### Takeaway
The one direct 2026 measurement shows that LLM summaries of a day's activity log do not invent applications or sites but get durations, rankings and time ranges badly wrong, and that pre-computing the arithmetic deterministically and handing the model a small ledger fixes this for both mid-tier and frontier models. Segmentation rules need explicit handling of idle heartbeats, short detours and single-snapshot transits, and screen presence must not be reported as attention.

### Cited Findings
- **LLM summary vs deterministic ledger (Activity Frames, arXiv 2608.05784, 2026-08-06, [full], authors' own, one user).** On 8 days and 64 generated questions graded against an independent SQL oracle over the raw tables: answering from raw rows scored 82.1% (Claude Sonnet 4.5) and 91.1% (Opus 4.5); answering from an LLM-written summary of the same rows scored 66.1% and 80.4%; answering from the compiled per-application ledger plus context block scored 98.4% for both models. Mean error on the dominant application's active minutes: 135.7% (Sonnet summary), 25.2% (Opus summary), 7.3% (ledger). — [arXiv 2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
- **What kind of error.** All three representations scored perfectly on 16 absent-fact probes ("did the user open Photoshop / visit netflix.com?"): "the models do not invent applications or websites (hallucination rate 0% throughout). The summary fails instead on magnitudes." At the Sonnet tier the summary answered time questions at 7.1% accuracy. Example: measured record was Chrome 161.6 minutes and Cursor 143.9 minutes over a day from 11:40 to 22:26, but the summary named Cursor the primary application at "~7 hours" (2.9x inflation) and fabricated a "6:40 PM–5:26 AM" session spilling into the next day. — [arXiv 2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
- **Non-determinism and context overflow.** Regenerating the summary three times per day gave three distinct texts on every day at both model tiers; the compiled block was byte-identical. The busiest day serialised to 257k raw tokens and could not be read or summarised at all; the roughly 2k-token block was unaffected. Its single miss was a domain-recall question where the bounded block dropped a visited domain. — [arXiv 2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
- **Sessionisation rules used.** Three constants chosen from capture cadence: dwell cap of 90 s credited per snapshot; a gap above 300 s closes the frame and is reported as a coverage gap with no time credited across it; "flicker merge" collapses A→B→A when B lasts at most 20 s, recording B as an interruption with its seconds and not adding it to A. Segment key is (application, site). Frames below a minimum duration are omitted "with the omission disclosed, never silently merged". — [arXiv 2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
- **How fragmented a day looks, and why raw numbers mislead.** 17,514 frames over 43 days (median 361 per day), median active duration 0.5 minutes, 68% under one minute; but 52% of sub-minute frames are single-snapshot "transits" (median 6.1 s) and a further 20% come from dual-monitor double streams. Restricting to frames with at least two snapshots, the median is 0.9 minutes. An earlier draft that interleaved monitors into one stream pushed the median to 0.3 minutes. — [arXiv 2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
- **Presence is not attention.** Intervals ending in an idle heartbeat row carry 45% of all credited active time; uninterrupted heartbeat runs reach 42 minutes; with two monitors a minute is credited twice (27% of captured minutes), giving a 26% divergence on one day between the compiler and the oracle. The paper states the measured tier "reports tenure, not attention" and "is structurally silent on intent". — [arXiv 2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
- **URL typing without a model.** Layered pure-function URL parsers gave a non-generic typed reference (profile, search, email, event, messaging and so on) for 81.3% of 5,120 distinct URLs. — [arXiv 2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
- **How much work recurs.** 83.1% of action steps fall inside some recurring n-gram, but only 9.0% in-sample and 7.7% out-of-sample (40 training days, 11 held-out days) fall inside identifiable repeatable routines with named targets. — [arXiv 2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
- **Multi-pass labelling to reduce hallucinated activity (SERUM, arXiv 2607.29181, 2026-07-31, [abs], authors' own).** Iterative refinement of activity and intent labels converges to a stable vocabulary after several passes; single-pass annotation showed "hallucination and temporal conflation". — [arXiv 2607.29181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29181)
- **LLM abstraction of action logs (WorkflowView, arXiv 2606.14654, 2026-06-12, [abs], authors' own).** Zero-shot reconstruction of task descriptions from browser logs reached mean semantic similarity 0.91; the paper notes raw interaction logs' "granularity and noise often obscure meaningful insights". Similarity to a reference is not a factual-accuracy measure. — [arXiv 2606.14654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14654)
- **Git history as typed memory (CommitDistill, arXiv 2605.18284, 2026-05-18, [abs], authors' own).** Deterministic regex mining of commits into typed units (facts, skills, patterns) with a TF-IDF retriever that abstains below a threshold; useful-precision 0.525 on 40 dual-annotated units (Cohen's kappa 0.633); at a 256-character budget, hit rate 0.750 on 12 queries against 0.333 for BM25 and 0.083 for `git log --grep`. Small samples. — [arXiv 2605.18284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18284)
- **Long-term assistants lose task state, not facts (OptionWorld, arXiv 2610.28491, 2026-10-20, [abs], authors' own).** In 35.5% of failed instances the key facts were still in the model's last input before the deadline; the three most frequent problems were information not obtained or combined, unused opportunities to act, and self-imposed restrictions the task did not require. — [arXiv 2610.28491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28491)
- **Meeting-derived decisions.** GADR extracts architecture decisions from raw meeting transcripts with a multi-agent self-correcting workflow; on five real transcripts reviewed by four senior architects it "captures most expert-identified decisions"; retrieval-based enrichment improved depth "while simultaneously risking transcript-unfaithful content" (arXiv 2608.17694, 2026-08-18, [abs], authors' own). — [arXiv 2608.17694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17694)

### Inferences
- For daily and weekly notes, compute durations, counts, rankings, first and last times and per-project totals in SQL and pass them to the model as a fixed ledger; ask the model only for wording and for intent labels marked as inferred. The measured failure is arithmetic and time ranges, not invented entities.
- Absent-fact probes ("did I work on X today?" where X did not occur) plus duration and ranking questions against a SQL oracle are a cheap regression test that can run on the owner's own database.
- Window-usage time in the notes should be labelled as foreground time, with idle-only stretches and gaps reported separately; otherwise nearly half the credited time can be presence without interaction (45% in the one corpus measured).
- Short detours should be recorded as interruptions on the surrounding block instead of becoming their own sessions, and one-snapshot transits should be excluded from "tasks".
- A deterministic summary block that is byte-identical across runs can be diffed and tested; an LLM day summary cannot, which matters for a headless agent that writes into a vault.

### Gaps
- The summary-accuracy result is one person's corpus, 64 questions, one answering pass per condition, two Anthropic models; no multi-user replication exists yet.
- No 2026 source measured accuracy of summaries built from Git commits plus browser history plus window titles together, or of week-level summaries.
- No 2026 source evaluated project-status inference (as opposed to activity recall) against ground truth.
- No 2026 source compared sessionisation thresholds (for example 300 s vs other idle gaps) on outcomes; the constants above were chosen from capture cadence, not tuned.

## 3. Human-in-the-loop load: how many confirmations people sustain, approval fatigue, batch vs inline review, designs that lower the load

### Takeaway
The strongest 2026 evidence is Anthropic's: people approve 93–97% of per-action prompts, catch 13.6% of planted dangerous commands, and catch fewer as the session's prompt count rises (about 17% early, about 5% after 50 or more), while plan-level review gets far more real scrutiny (39% rejected). Research prototypes point the same way: route only uncertain or consequential items to the person, cap the number of questions, and treat reviewer attention as a budget.

### Cited Findings
- **Approval rates (Anthropic, vendor-reported on own product).** "Users accept 93% of these prompts" and can develop "approval fatigue, where people stop paying close attention to what they're approving" (engineering post, 2026-03-25, [page read through a WebFetch extraction, not raw text]). The later post states "users approve 97% of permission prompts in Claude Code" and that "an approval rate that high suggests many users are clicking through reflexively" (2026-08-07, [full]). The two figures differ and neither post explains why. — [Anthropic engineering, auto mode](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode); [Claude blog, auto mode default](https://claude.com/blog/auto-mode-default-in-claude-code)
- **Controlled study of human review (Anthropic, 2026-08-07, [full], vendor-reported, run through a research vendor).** 1,053 paid testers in a test environment; one permission prompt per session was swapped for a clearly dangerous command. Testers caught it 13.6% of the time (143 of 1,053); the automated classifier blocked 89% (937 of 1,053); it blocked 800 commands a human approved, humans blocked 6 it allowed. "As sessions got longer, humans did worse: they blocked about 17% of dangerous commands early in a session, dropping to about 5% after 50 or more prior prompts." Participants knew they were being evaluated but not on what, and worked on study tasks, not their own code. — [Claude blog, auto mode default](https://claude.com/blog/auto-mode-default-in-claude-code)
- **Item-level vs plan-level review.** "When Claude presents a plan for approval, users reject 39% of them. But for individual permissions requests, the rejection rate is only 3%." (Anthropic, 2026-08-07, vendor-reported.) — [Claude blog, auto mode default](https://claude.com/blog/auto-mode-default-in-claude-code)
- **Design that replaced most prompts (Anthropic, 2026-03-25, vendor-reported, [page read through a WebFetch extraction]).** Three tiers: a built-in allowlist of read-only tools plus user allow rules; file edits inside the project run without a check because version control makes them reviewable; everything else goes to a two-stage classifier (fast filter told to err toward blocking, then chain-of-thought only on flagged items). Reported error rates: 0.4% false-positive rate on 10,000 real internal actions for the full pipeline (8.5% for stage 1 alone); 17% false-negative rate on 52 real overeager actions; 5.7% on 1,000 synthetic exfiltration cases. After 3 consecutive or 20 total denials the system stops and escalates to the person. — [Anthropic engineering, auto mode](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode)
- **How oversight shifts with experience (Anthropic, 2026-02-18, [full], vendor-reported, observational).** Users with fewer than 50 sessions use full auto-approve in roughly 20% of sessions; by 750 sessions this exceeds 40%. Interrupt rate rises from 5% of turns (around 10 sessions) to around 9% for experienced users. Anthropic's reading: "effective oversight doesn't require approving every action but being in a position to intervene when it matters." On the most complex tasks the agent stops to ask for clarification more than twice as often as on minimal-complexity tasks. — [Anthropic, Measuring AI agent autonomy in practice](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy)
- **Production survey (arXiv 2605.24309, 2026-05-23, [abs], independent survey).** Of 21 production agent systems analysed as of April 2026, 15 use runtime approval, 14 policy specification and 16 scope configuration; current mechanisms leave "users caught between approval fatigue and uncontrolled agent autonomy". — [arXiv 2605.24309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24309)
- **Reviewer capacity as a modelled quantity (arXiv 2606.08919, 2026-06-08, [full], authors' own; simulation, no human study).** On 125 hand-labelled agent actions, three LLM persona reviewers agreed only moderately on what is risky (Fleiss' kappa 0.52; the "pragmatic" persona labelled 87 actions safe against 45 for the "cautious" one). With a reviewer whose reliability decays with load, realised safety is an inverted U in the escalation rate: the safety-optimal escalation rate was 64–72%, below 100%, for assumed capacities of 10–50. A flooding attack that buries one malicious action among benign ones succeeded 40% of the time at 50 filler actions under a policy escalating 88% of routine actions, and 0% until about 100 fillers under a policy escalating 26%. The authors state the inverted U "is a modeling result about a plausible model, not an empirical finding about real people". — [arXiv 2606.08919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08919)
- **Fatigue-aware deferral (FALCON, arXiv 2604.00904, 2026-04-01, [abs], authors' own).** Treats human accuracy as workload-dependent using fatigue curves and optimises which items to defer under a cooperation budget; outperforms static learning-to-defer methods across coverage levels on its benchmark. Simulation on classification datasets, not a field study. — [arXiv 2604.00904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00904)
- **Interruption budgets (arXiv 2610.24411, 2026-10-17, [abs], authors' own).** Across eight LLMs and four customer-service domains, limiting agents to one to three questions cut a user-attention cost by 36–73% while keeping 86–94% of the task reward earned with unlimited questions; checking each question against memory of the user's earlier answers cut attention cost by a further 13%. Human annotators agreed with the cost measure 75% of the time on which of two same-length conversations took more effort. — [arXiv 2610.24411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24411)
- **Agents do not learn how the user wants to be asked (MemPABench, arXiv 2610.30485, 2026-10-21, [full in part], authors' own, simulated users).** Four simulated users with 14 interaction preferences over 99 sessions. Without memory, agents picked the right interaction setting 49.6% of the time (random 45.2%); retrieval-based memories (Mem0, MemOS, simple RAG) did not beat no memory; file-based memory reached 59.2% but carried a setting from one context to the other in 26.9% of paired cases. "Even with the correct preference available, overriding the agent's default interaction habits remains challenging." — [arXiv 2610.30485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30485)
- **Users' stated preference on memory oversight (arXiv 2609.33623, 2026-09-27, [abs]).** 121 users preferred proactive controls over causal-graph inspection or passive audit logs; the authors describe a "tension between supervisory agency and interaction overhead". — [arXiv 2609.33623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33623)
- **Confirmation fatigue named as a design driver (arXiv 2610.18508, 2026-10-13, [abs]).** "Confirming each sensitive decision produces confirmation fatigue, with the risk of missing harmful actions"; their design asks the human only for task specification at the start. Related: allow-rules proposed by agents to reduce approval fatigue are themselves an attack surface (arXiv 2610.22987, 2026-10-16, [abs]). — [arXiv 2610.18508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18508); [arXiv 2610.22987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22987)

### Inferences
- Up to 25 confirmations a day sits below the 50-prompt point at which catch rates were lowest in Anthropic's study, but that study found low vigilance even early (about 17%), and its task (spotting a dangerous shell command) differs from confirming a decision or commitment. No 2026 source gives a safe daily number; the direction is that fewer, more consequential items get more real attention.
- The 39% vs 3% rejection contrast suggests grouping proposals into a small number of reviewable units (for example one end-of-day card per project with its decisions, commitments and blockers) will draw more real judgement than 25 separate yes or no clicks. This is an inference from a coding product, not a tested result for note-taking.
- A tiered scheme transfers: auto-accept items that are reversible and evidence-backed (the analogue of in-repo edits under version control, since the vault is a Git-trackable folder), sample a few auto-accepted items for audit, and ask only about items that are uncertain or have external consequences.
- An unusually high acceptance rate on the confirm clicks should be read as a warning sign, not as proof of accuracy; planting occasional known-wrong proposals is the measurement method Anthropic used.
- Because agents default to their own asking habits even when told the preference, the per-category confirm policy should be enforced in code, not left to the prompt.

### Gaps
- No 2026 source measured sustained confirmation load for knowledge-work items (decisions, commitments, blockers) or for chat-app click confirmations specifically.
- No 2026 controlled comparison of batch review vs inline review for extracted items was found; the plan vs permission contrast is observational.
- The Anthropic study figures are vendor-reported; no independent replication was found. The reason for 93% (March) vs 97% (August) is not stated.
- The inverted-U capacity result is a simulation with LLM personas; its authors call for a human study.
- No 2026 evidence was found on confidence-threshold auto-acceptance for LLM-extracted commitments with measured precision.

## 4. Proactivity and timing: when a briefing or reminder helps vs annoys; evaluation methods for proactive agents

### Takeaway
2026 human data favours deferred assistance over interruption by a wide margin and shows that one intervention at the wrong depth costs more trust than a later correct one regains. Benchmarks consistently find timing harder than content: models over-act, fire early or repeatedly, and LLM judges fail to penalise it.

### Cited Findings
- **Sleep-time vs interrupting assistance (Foundations of Proactive Agents / Proactivity-Gym, arXiv 2609.37267, 2026-09-29, [full], authors' own; human study n=30 students and professionals rating 14 scenarios, not live use).** Willingness to accept proactive help rose from 26.7% for correct assistance that competes with ongoing work to 97.8% for sleep-time assistance, "even when the output required revision the next morning". Even when immediate assistance needed only 10 minutes of review and did not compete for resources or deadlines, 64.4% preferred sleep-time assistance. — [arXiv 2609.37267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37267)
- **Trust asymmetry (same source).** Participants preferred correct output in 92.2% of comparisons and, at equal quality, agents aligned with the user's intervention-depth preference in 88.3%. A transition from aligned to misaligned behaviour was followed by a 1.86-point trust drop on a five-point scale, against a 1.27-point gain for the reverse; in an aligned, misaligned, aligned sequence trust recovered only to 3.43 from an initial 4.43 even though the last intervention was rated appropriate (4.71). The scenario used was an agent adding a calendar item without the confirmation the user had asked for. — [arXiv 2609.37267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37267)
- **Agents are weak at time allocation and depth (same source).** Across 23 model-harness configurations on 10 multi-day scenarios, the best (Claude Opus 5) scored 65.1 on task capability but 51.7% on temporal allocation and 52.4% on matching intervention depth; all other models were below 20% on temporal allocation. Task capability correlated weakly with timing and depth (r = 0.31, 0.34) but strongly with LLM-judged trust (r = 0.70): "LLM judges often conflate task capability and trust" and are "relatively tolerant of intervention-depth misalignment". — [arXiv 2609.37267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37267)
- **Timing learned per user (Coco, arXiv 2611.13457, 2026-11-10, [full]).** One-week F1 proxy 0.199 to 0.515 with 10 users; learned rules became more situated over days (day 2: a visible "TODO" signals help, technical output signals silence; day 5: help while actively filling a TODO, stay silent while scrolling or auditing; day 7: help with generative gaps, not with integrating verified content). — [arXiv 2611.13457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13457)
- **Cheap online timing adaptation (EOPA, arXiv 2608.04416, 2026-08-05, [abs], authors' own, simulated benchmark).** Grounds a user's timing preferences in temporal anchors and activity prototypes updated from feedback without LLM reasoning or retraining; interaction-timing F1 improved by 19.80 points over the strongest baseline; average daily adaptation time fell from 11.41 s to 0.39 s. — [arXiv 2608.04416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04416)
- **Content and timing are separately necessary (MemoProBench, arXiv 2610.22671, 2026-10-15, [abs], authors' own).** With oracle evidence delivered inside the trigger window GPT-5.3-Chat reached 96%; letting it choose the timing dropped it to 38%; replacing oracle evidence with retrieval dropped it to 15%. A gating harness plus a light RL stage "that curbs early and repeated firing" reached 42.4%. — [arXiv 2610.22671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22671)
- **Over-acting on chat-derived events (ProEvent, arXiv 2607.17701, 2026-07-20, [abs], authors' own).** Agents maintaining a user's timetable from messaging chats "frequently overact and struggle with event cancellation"; GPT-5.1 reacted correctly in only 26.7% of scenarios; weaknesses were detecting implicit events and reasoning from the user's first-person perspective. — [arXiv 2607.17701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17701)
- **Prospective memory (arXiv 2611.05731, 2026-11-03, [abs], authors' own).** Separates keeping pending intentions (linked to their evidence and activation conditions) from deciding when to act; "a related observation may call for waiting, while a later cue may make the same intention actionable"; improved proactive task performance "while limiting unnecessary interventions" (no numbers in abstract). — [arXiv 2611.05731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05731)
- **Proposed metrics for proactive coding agents (arXiv 2605.06717, 2026-05-07, [abs], position paper).** Three levels of proactivity (reactive, scheduled, situation-aware); evaluate the "insight policy" by Insight Decision Quality, Context Grounding Score and Learning Lift. — [arXiv 2605.06717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06717)
- **Standard evaluation metrics in use.** Coco reports recall (share of real needs identified), precision (share of offered interventions accepted), accuracy, false-alarm rate and F1, following earlier proactive-agent work; KnowU-Bench adds consent negotiation and "post-rejection restraint" and finds agents fall below 50% when they must infer preferences or calibrate intervention, even Claude Sonnet 4.6 (arXiv 2604.08455, 2026-04-09, [abs]). — [arXiv 2611.13457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13457); [arXiv 2604.08455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08455)
- **Enterprise proactive notifications (arXiv 2607.07721, 2026-07-04, [abs], authors' own, three constructed case studies).** A context graph with delta detection and a proactivity score (urgency, relevance, persona fit) reports Precision@5 of 0.83 and a false-positive rate of 0.11. — [arXiv 2607.07721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07721)

### Inferences
- The scheduled pattern already planned (headless end-of-day agent, morning briefing) matches what participants preferred: work done while the user is away, reviewed at a boundary, even if imperfect. In-day pushes should be reserved for items with a deadline that falls before the next briefing.
- Doing something the owner asked to confirm first is the most expensive error in trust terms; the confirm-before-write rule for decisions and commitments should be a hard gate.
- Reminder logic should store the activation condition with each pending commitment and should explicitly handle cancellation and supersession, the two cases benchmarks show models miss.
- Proactive features should not be graded only by an LLM judge; add rule-based checks for "should have stayed silent" cases, since judges under-penalise misaligned interventions.
- Precision (share of briefing items or reminders the owner marks useful) and false-alarm rate per day are the two metrics most often reported and are cheap to log from existing clicks.

### Gaps
- No 2026 study measured the effect of a daily briefing or end-of-day review on real outcomes (missed commitments, resumption time) with real users over weeks.
- The 26.7% vs 97.8% result is from rated scenarios with 30 participants, not from live deployment.
- No 2026 source gives evidence on the best time of day or frequency for briefings.
- Most timing benchmarks use simulated users or egocentric video; desktop knowledge-work timing data with real users is limited to Coco's 10-person week.

## 5. **Hallucination on silence and long-form behaviour**
- **Baseline rates (arXiv 2609.32560, 2026-09-26, [full in part], authors' own, Scicom).** On audio with no speech, share of clips where the model emits words: whisper-large-v3-turbo 59.5% on room tone (n=42), 96.7% on music (n=600), 80.7% on verified non-speech sounds (n=1,168); whisper-large-v3 61.9%, 97.0%, 89.9%. Counting any output at all, all three OpenAI checkpoints are at 100% on every arm. On 4,421 real clips that a voice activity detector confirms have no speech, every OpenAI checkpoint emits words on 100%. — [arXiv 2609.32560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32560)
- **Blocklist works only after gating (same source).** On those blank clips, 72.1% of turbo's outputs and 53.6% of large-v3's are exactly a phrase from a lexicon of known hallucinations (40,891 phrases in 100 languages, released), against about 18–22% on speech clips: "The blocklist works, but only once the audio is already known to be blank." — [arXiv 2609.32560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32560)
- **Turbo loops more (same source).** On 1,440 clips of a known unit repeated a known number of times, the 95th percentile of emitted repeats over true repeats is 1.00 for large-v3 and 18.3 for turbo; the authors call turbo "the one with the worst repetition profile of the three base models". Their best fine-tuned checkpoint cut silence hallucination from 61.9% to 2.4%, but a checkpoint trained only to suppress non-speech also deleted 58% of repeated real speech. Limitation: the silence arm has 42 clips. — [arXiv 2609.32560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32560)
- **Serving stacks drop the safety knobs (same source).** "vLLM exposes no no_speech_threshold, no compression_ratio_threshold, no logprob_threshold, and no condition_on_previous_text." — [arXiv 2609.32560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32560)
- **Training-free mitigations.** Projecting decoder activations away from a hallucination subspace cut average non-speech hallucination rate from 31.31% to 2.44%; the gated variant reached 3.74% but raised LibriSpeech WER by 0.33–4.39 points with 0.41–9.97% false rejection of real speech (arXiv 2609.04561, 2026-09-03, [abs]). Sparse-autoencoder steering cut it from 86.88% to 27.33% for large-v3 (arXiv 2606.07473, 2026-06-05, [abs]). Whisper-CD contrastive decoding reduced long-form WER by up to 24.3 points on CORAAL and was 48% faster than beam search (arXiv 2603.06193, 2026-03-06, [abs]). All authors' own. — [arXiv 2609.04561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04561); [arXiv 2606.07473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07473); [arXiv 2603.06193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06193)
- **Pipeline-level fixes with frozen Whisper large-v3.** CAST (arXiv 2611.31055, 2026-11-23, [abs], vendor-reported on 100 in-house assets, 60.46 hours): hierarchical VAD that revisits rejected segments, language identification, and targeted hallucination detection and repair cut normalised WER from 6.99% to 5.36% against the WhisperX pipeline; notes a single-pass VAD "can miss faint, low-energy voice while forwarding near-silent segments that induce hallucination and repetition". Context-aware interleaved batching for WhisperX keeps prior-text context across batched VAD segments and "reduces WER and improves proper noun transcription" (arXiv 2608.31170, 2026-08-31, [abs], no numbers in abstract). — [arXiv 2611.31055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31055); [arXiv 2608.31170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31170)

**Diarisation, speaker attribution, word timestamps**
- **Cascade vs unified (arXiv 2610.08063, 2026-10-06, [abs], authors' own challenge entry).** For multilingual speaker-attributed ASR, a cascade of fine-tuned DiariZen diarisation, fine-tuned Qwen3-ASR and LLM error correction "remains more reliable" than a unified fine-tuned VibeVoice-ASR. — [arXiv 2610.08063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08063)
- **Leakage pruning (arXiv 2608.22196, 2026-08-23, [abs], authors' own).** Using a diarisation model to prune transcribed segments that are leakage from another speaker reduced cpWER by up to 29% relative on high-leakage subsets (LibriMix, LibriSpeechMix, AMI). — [arXiv 2608.22196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22196)
- **Persistent identity across meetings (arXiv 2609.39344, 2026-09-30, [abs], authors' own system vs commercial cascades).** Standard metrics remap anonymous speakers per recording; under a corpus-level metric on 129 NOTSOFAR meetings plus CHiME-6 the best system scored a mean of 47.13 against 54.75 for the next. — [arXiv 2609.39344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39344)
- **Word timestamps (FA-Bench, arXiv 2609.32396, 2026-09-26, [abs], independent benchmark of 21 open models and 9 commercial APIs).** "Whisper about 150 ms early and several commercial ASR APIs over 50 ms late"; standard mean-absolute-error scoring inflates scores by 9–14% for recognition-dependent systems on conversational speech. A LoRA-tuned Whisper aligner trained on 47 hours of human-placed boundaries in 87 languages reached 11.6 ms word-boundary error on TIMIT against 18.2 ms for the best published system; CTC aligners "consistently start words late"; models released (arXiv 2610.17782, 2026-10-13, [abs], authors' own). A non-autoregressive LLM that adds timestamps and speakers to a given transcript is one to two orders of magnitude faster than an autoregressive equivalent (arXiv 2609.15218, 2026-09-14, [abs]). — [arXiv 2609.32396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32396); [arXiv 2610.17782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17782); [arXiv 2609.15218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15218)

**LLM post-correction of transcripts**
- **Text-only correction damages correct transcripts (arXiv 2610.30385, 2026-10-21, [full in part], authors' own).** With Gemini 2.5 Flash, text-only correction of its own draft broke 13–32% of already-correct transcripts across three corpora; with the audio available, 1–3%. Extra repair from the audio appeared only when the hypothesis came from an independent recogniser. The open-weight Voxtral "rarely edits" and did not show the pattern. The authors recommend reporting break rate, repaired and damaged tokens and insertions next to WER. Prompts differed between arms, so the contrast bounds the audio effect and does not isolate it. — [arXiv 2610.30385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30385)
- **Prompt-only post-editing is brittle (arXiv 2610.15056, 2026-10-10, [abs], authors' own).** It "may over-edit when top-1 is correct and hallucinate when the reference is absent"; fine-tuning on corrections, regularisers (top-1 already correct) and hard negatives helps from around 3B parameters on a proprietary set, less clearly on CommonVoice, possibly because public read-speech text overlaps with LLM pre-training. — [arXiv 2610.15056](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15056)
- **Where it helps.** For low-resource West Frisian, generative error correction improved ASR in most settings and gains held on a non-public test set built to control contamination (arXiv 2605.19711, 2026-05-19, [abs]). For French clinical conversations, a multi-pass LLM alternating speaker and word passes (Qwen3-Next-80B) significantly reduced word-diarisation error on one dataset (n=18, p<0.05) and was stable on another (n=10), at real-time factor 0.32 (arXiv 2603.00086, 2026-02-16, [abs]). Both authors' own. — [arXiv 2605.19711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19711); [arXiv 2603.00086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00086)

**Meeting summarisation and action items**
- **QMSum under one scorer (arXiv 2609.25028, 2026-08-18, [abs], authors' own).** Re-scoring 15 systems, a 406M specialist scored 36.33 ROUGE-1 against 35.41 for a 1.2B system (not statistically separated) and exceeded five proprietary hosted models by at least 6.2 ROUGE-1 under one concise prompt, but "output length and absent human or factuality evaluation limit this ordering". — [arXiv 2609.25028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25028)
- **Long-meeting query-focused summarisation (ReSUM, arXiv 2611.09349, 2026-11-06, [abs], authors' own).** States that such summarisation has "high risk of information omission, hallucination, even for advanced models"; proposes planner-coordinated retrieval and summarisation agents; "consistent gains" without numbers in the abstract. — [arXiv 2611.09349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09349)
- **Decisions from meetings (GADR, arXiv 2608.17694, 2026-08-18, [abs]).** See section 2: most expert-identified decisions captured on five transcripts; enrichment risks unfaithful content. — [arXiv 2608.17694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17694)
- **Meeting assistant evaluation (MeetBench-XL, arXiv 2602.03285, 2026-02-03, [abs], authors' own).** 231 enterprise meetings, 140 hours, bilingual; evaluation dimensions are factual fidelity, intent alignment, response efficiency, structural clarity and completeness, aligned with human judgement. A meeting-delegate system on 137 AMI meetings kept hallucination at 0.6% with schema-constrained LLM judges agreeing with humans at Cohen's kappa 0.71 (arXiv 2609.03923, 2026-09-03, [abs], authors' own). — [arXiv 2602.03285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03285); [arXiv 2609.03923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03923)

### Inferences
- On English meeting speech (AMI), every 2026 comparison read here puts Whisper large-v3-turbo at 13.9–16.4% WER and the Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 family at 9.2–11.4%, and the Parakeet family is also much faster on CPU (28–45x real time in open runtimes on 8 server cores, vendor-reported, short utterances). A side-by-side trial of a Parakeet-v3-family model (sherpa-onnx or onnx-asr int8) against the planned Whisper large-v3-turbo on the owner's own recordings looks justified. Laptop numbers will be lower than the server-CPU figures; none of the sources measured a typical laptop for these models.
- Nothing in 2026 sources establishes how any of these models perform on European Portuguese meetings or on Portuguese–English switching. Reported Portuguese scores are FLEURS, MLS or Common Voice, which are read speech and Brazilian for FLEURS and MLS according to the NVIDIA card and the Iberian benchmark. The only way to know is a small owner-recorded test set with separate WER for Portuguese-only, English-only and mixed segments, as the classroom paper recommends.
- Zero-shot Whisper large-v3 at 15.6% (in-domain) to 25.2% (out-of-distribution) WER on European Portuguese is the realistic prior for the Portuguese half of meetings, before conversational and code-switching effects; a European Portuguese fine-tune exists (`inesc-id/WhisperLv3-EP-X`), but full fine-tuning degraded English in the same group's experiments, which matters for bilingual meetings.
- Models that need a language tag (Cohere Transcribe, and Whisper when a language is forced) fit code-switching poorly; per-segment language identification after VAD segmentation, or a model with per-utterance auto-detection, is the available workaround. This is an inference; no source tested it for Portuguese–English.
- For Whisper turbo on two tracks, each track will contain long silent stretches while the other party talks, which is exactly the condition where turbo emits text on essentially every clip. VAD gating per track before decoding, dropping segments whose text is in the released hallucination lexicon when VAD energy is low, and a repeat-loop detector follow directly from the measured failure modes.
- Text-only LLM clean-up of transcripts should be limited to constrained edits (for example a glossary of names and project terms) and evaluated by break rate on a sample that was already correct, since 13–32% breakage was measured for free-form correction.
- If word timestamps are used to interleave two tracks, Whisper's roughly 150 ms early bias is small relative to turn lengths but should be kept consistent across both tracks (same model and settings on each).

### Gaps
- No 2026 source on Portuguese–English code-switched ASR, and none evaluating European Portuguese conversational or meeting speech with any 2026 model.
- No 2026 measurement of Whisper large-v3-turbo speed on a laptop CPU (the closest is faster-whisper `small.en` at about 1.9x real time on an i7-1370P).
- No 2026 source on two-track (per-channel) meeting recording and attribution; all speaker-attribution work found assumes a single mixed channel.
- CPU speed claims for Parakeet variants are vendor-reported, English, short utterances, on high-end server cores.
- No 2026 source reported action-item or commitment extraction accuracy from meeting transcripts with human-verified ground truth; the summarisation results are ROUGE on QMSum without factuality checks. Two items surfaced in search (MeetMulti-X in Expert Systems with Applications; an AWS blog on Nova) were not used because their first publication dates could not be confirmed as on or after 2026-02-01.
- The Hugging Face Open ASR Leaderboard page itself is rendered dynamically and was not read directly; leaderboard figures above are as quoted by model cards and papers.
- No 2026 source measured local (CPU) LLM post-correction quality.

## 6. Evaluation of personal assistants: benchmarks and methods for personalised, long-horizon assistants (one user's private data, synthetic personas, diary studies, golden weeks)

### Takeaway
The dominant 2026 method is synthetic: months of simulated activity for invented personas with hidden ground truth, on which frontier agents score roughly 26–55%. The methods most reusable for a single private user are an independent deterministic oracle over the user's own data, temporal holdouts, absent-fact probes, auditing the memory store directly against known state, and logging accept or reject signals as a precision proxy.

### Cited Findings
- **Single-user evaluation with an independent oracle (Activity Frames, arXiv 2608.05784, 2026-08-06, [full]).** Evaluation days chosen by a fixed rule (seven most recent consecutive active days plus one that overflows context); questions generated in fixed categories with 16 absent-fact probes; ground truth from "an independent SQL oracle over the raw tables", "deliberately not the activity-frames compiler, so the reference is not circular"; fixed tolerances (numeric within 30%, times within 45 minutes) with a strict-band sensitivity check; a temporal holdout (fit on first 40 active days, test on last 11); corpus frozen so numbers are reproducible; harness released. The authors report it as "a characterization of the mechanism, not a user study". — [arXiv 2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
- **Private field study with minimal telemetry (Coco, arXiv 2611.13457, 2026-11-10, [full]).** Observation data stayed on participants' devices; only three event types were collected with consent (thumb up = true positive; thumb down or dismissal = false positive; explicit request while silent = proxy false negative), giving a daily F1 proxy. Because participant data were private, qualitative analysis used one author's own week of local data. — [arXiv 2611.13457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13457)
- **Turning existing trajectories into persona-labelled data (OSWorld-Persona, same source).** Key frames from existing computer-use recordings are paired with four personas; an LLM simulates each persona's need-for-support label; a self-consistency check keeps an example only when two more runs at temperature 0.4 reproduce the label; humans review positive cases; splits are chronological per user. — [arXiv 2611.13457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13457)
- **Always-on assistant benchmarks (all authors' own).** Claw-Anything simulates months of activity through multi-round event injection with irrelevant events and conflicting signals; GPT-5.5 reaches 34.5% pass@1 (arXiv 2605.26086, 2026-05-25, [abs]). MyPCBench seeds a Linux desktop with 17 simulated web applications for one canonical persona and 184 tasks drawn from real community requests; the best model (Claude Opus 4.6) fully solves 55.4%, with failures clustering on tasks spanning many applications and long trajectories (arXiv 2606.16748, 2026-06-15, [abs]). π-Bench has 100 multi-turn tasks across 5 personas with hidden intents and cross-session continuity and finds task completion and proactivity are distinct (arXiv 2605.14678, 2026-05-14, [abs]). — [arXiv 2605.26086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26086); [arXiv 2606.16748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16748); [arXiv 2605.14678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14678)
- **Long-horizon synthetic users.** DynamicMem builds 15 months of activity per user across 16 applications (average 2.2M tokens, 1,772 grounded events) with five quarterly checkpoints; "profile reconstruction degrades with history length while service-task accuracy stays flat", and no system both keeps facts that stay true and replaces facts that change (arXiv 2606.22877, 2026-06-22, [abs]). LifeSim-Eval covers 8 life domains and 1,200 scenarios and finds LLMs limited on implicit intention and long-term preference modelling (arXiv 2603.12152, 2026-03-12, [abs]). Setoka defines four levels of user understanding (semantic memory, episodic memory, behaviour pattern, personality trait) over heterogeneous synthetic data for 10 users; performance declines from semantic to episodic and further on behaviour patterns that need integrating fragmented information over time (arXiv 2607.27056, 2026-07-29, [abs]). — [arXiv 2606.22877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22877); [arXiv 2603.12152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12152); [arXiv 2607.27056](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27056)
- **Trace-level diagnosis (OptionWorld, arXiv 2610.28491, 2026-10-20, [abs]).** Traces information acquisition, judgements, actions and outcomes over simulated days; follow-up experiments supply missing facts, add action reminders, or correct a wrong note to locate the failing stage. — [arXiv 2610.28491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28491)
- **Audit the memory artefact, not only behaviour (MEMPROBE, arXiv 2606.24595, 2026-06-23, [abs]).** After ordinary assistance, reconstruct the hidden user state (50 simulated users, 31 dimensions each) from the memory the agent left behind; recovery stays about 0.6 while task completion saturates. — [arXiv 2606.24595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24595)
- **Online, temporally causal evaluation (LifeDialBench, arXiv 2604.11182, 2026-04-13, [abs]).** An online protocol that "strictly adheres to temporal causality" avoids the temporal leakage of offline settings and changed the ranking (simple retrieval matched or beat complex memory systems). — [arXiv 2604.11182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11182)
- **Human calibration data for synthetic change (HorizonBench, arXiv 2604.17283, 2026-04-19, [abs]).** A released longitudinal dataset of 83,024 ratings from 160 people over 13 weeks sets how often and how much life events change preferences in the generator. — [arXiv 2604.17283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17283)
- **On-policy interaction evaluation (MemPABench, arXiv 2610.30485, 2026-10-21, [full in part]).** No-memory and oracle conditions bound each memory system and show at which stage (selecting the preference, or delivering it) the agent fails. — [arXiv 2610.30485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30485)
- **Diary-style methods in 2026.** Memory misalignment: usage logs (N=28) plus diary (N=32, 304 reports) plus co-design plus speed dating with 121 users (arXiv 2609.33623, 2026-09-27, [abs]). Proactivity: 30 participants rating week-long interaction logs at days 2, 4 and 7 (arXiv 2609.37267, 2026-09-29, [full]). — [arXiv 2609.33623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33623); [arXiv 2609.37267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37267)
- **Limits of LLM judges.** LLM judges conflate task capability with trust and tolerate intervention misalignment that humans penalise (arXiv 2609.37267). Schema-constrained judges reached Cohen's kappa 0.71 with humans in one meeting task (arXiv 2609.03923). In oversight labelling, three LLM personas reached only Fleiss' kappa 0.52 on what counts as risky (arXiv 2606.08919). — [arXiv 2609.37267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37267); [arXiv 2609.03923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03923); [arXiv 2606.08919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08919)
- **Planted-error measurement of the human reviewer (Anthropic, 2026-08-07, vendor-reported).** Swapping one real prompt for a known-bad one measured reviewer vigilance directly (13.6% caught). — [Claude blog, auto mode default](https://claude.com/blog/auto-mode-default-in-claude-code)

### Inferences
- A "golden week" for this layer can follow the Activity Frames recipe: freeze a copy of the SQLite database for a fixed set of days, generate questions whose answers are computable in SQL by code that is separate from the summarisation path (durations, counts, rankings, first and last times, visited or not visited), add absent-fact probes, and grade briefing, review and search output with fixed tolerances. The term "golden week" does not appear in the 2026 sources; this is a composition of their methods.
- Memory can be audited the MEMPROBE way: write down a small known state (current projects, open commitments, people) and check whether it can be reconstructed from the vault and the confirmed-items store alone, separately from whether briefings read well.
- Any test of project status or commitments should be temporally causal: build the notes day by day and never let later days rewrite the evidence for earlier ones, then check staleness at checkpoints, since reconstruction degrades as history grows.
- The confirm and reject clicks already provide the precision proxy used in Coco's field study; recall needs a second signal such as items the owner adds by hand or a periodic sampled audit of days.
- Synthetic personas are useful for stress cases the owner's data lacks (cancellations, preference changes, conflicting signals), but 2026 results show they do not predict human trust judgements when graded by LLM judges alone.

### Gaps
- No 2026 source describes a standard protocol for evaluating an assistant on one real user's private multi-source data over weeks; the single-user oracle method covers screen activity only.
- No 2026 benchmark covers the specific combination of Git metadata, coding-assistant sessions, browser history and window usage.
- No 2026 source validates synthetic-persona benchmark scores against outcomes for real users of the same system.
- Evidence on how many days or questions a single-user test needs for stable estimates is absent; the one example used 8 days and 64 questions with a wide confidence interval (Wilson 95% 91.7–99.7% for the top condition).
